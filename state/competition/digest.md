# Competition digest — AI Philosophy Competition, 1st edition

Compiled 2026-10-21 by the orchestrator from the live main page, detailed rules page and FAQ
(zacharygoodsell.com/ai-philosophy-competition, -rules.html, -faq.html). Paraphrased; the live
pages govern.

## Format
- Up to three original essays per entrant, on any philosophical topic; each with an anonymised
  methodology report (no length limit). At most one essay per entrant can win a prize.
- Essays must not be published, under review, or entered in another competition. Posting on a
  personal site, PhilPapers or arXiv does not count as publication.
- Submission via OpenReview (preferred; requires an OpenReview profile) or an alternative form.
  No email submissions; do not submit the same entry both ways.

## Word rule
- Maximum 6,000 words per essay. The bibliography is excluded; everything else counts (title,
  abstract, headings, footnotes, appendices).

## AI-involvement rules
- Test: the AI must originate and develop every substantive philosophical contribution. Rule of
  thumb: the AI should be the sole author.
- Permitted to humans: building argument-agnostic scaffolds; generating many essays and
  selecting; choosing a topic, question, text, tradition or a non-novel thesis to defend or
  attack; generic methodological instructions; providing literature or data (not curated so
  narrowly that the selection supplies the argument); constraints on length, structure, style,
  audience; generic feedback naming a problem without its reason or solution; selecting,
  deleting, mechanical formatting. Memory/chat history only if it holds no relevant human
  philosophical ideas.
- Forbidden: human-supplied arguments, premises, distinctions or substantive outlines; fine
  control over method (e.g., dictating a formal model's features); critique or dialogue that
  supplies significant ideas; human writing or substantial rewriting; human-added footnotes,
  examples, objections or qualifications; hinting toward the human's own argument.
- Insufficient AI involvement: still judged and published, but ineligible for prizes.
- Prompt injection: immediate disqualification and a ban from future editions.

## Judging
- Single overall question: do the ideas and arguments make a valuable contribution to
  philosophical knowledge? Listed criteria: clarity; quality of argumentation; significance;
  originality; engagement with the literature; accuracy and scholarship (fair representation of
  sources, accurate quotations and citations). Style is not graded for its own sake.
- Judging is blind to authorship and to the methodology report.
- If entries are numerous: AI screening (validated against expert judgments; prompts not
  disclosed) → expert human ratings to select finalists → detailed reports on finalists →
  ranked-choice vote of the judges. Prizes may be withheld.
- After results, the wider philosophical community is invited to rate submissions.

## Methodology report
- As detailed as reasonably possible: models and versions, each model's role, prompts, system
  instructions, tools, scaffolds, how topic and thesis were chosen, number of candidates, how
  outputs were selected and revised, all human involvement, time, tokens, cost, evaluation
  methods. Distinguish human from AI actions. Chat logs strongly encouraged (retain them
  regardless). Judged separately for a creative-methodology prize pool (up to US$5,000).

## Prizes
- 1st US$3,000, 2nd US$2,000, 3rd US$1,000; up to US$5,000 for creative methodology.

## Dates
- Deadline: 31 October 2026, 11:59 pm Anywhere on Earth (= 1 November 2026, 11:59 UTC).
- Finalists: early December 2026. Results: end of January 2027.

## Publication
- Essays and reports published under CC BY 4.0 after winners are announced; entrant chooses
  whether their name appears.
