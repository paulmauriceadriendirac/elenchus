# Referee report — S2 Epistemic logician / knowledge-first epistemologist — P-cbf4

## Thesis and argument as I understand them (≤120 words)

The setting is Goodman and Salow's product ("multi-dimensional") structures. There, KM-normality is I-normality applied respect by respect (Theorem 1). So KM satisfies COMBINATION: any epistemically possible state of one respect is possible together with the actual states of the other respects. K-normality violates COMBINATION whenever one respect lies outside belief and another is better than the worst believed state (Prop. 2). At (0,2) in G&S's figure-4 model, K credits knowledge that if the thermometer is bizarre, the scale is ordinary. A new completion, meet-normality, applies K respect by respect. It keeps cliff-edge knowledge and KK and still satisfies COMBINATION. Granting COMBINATION, MARGINS is the same question with or without COMPARABILITY, so G&S's abductive link fails.

## The single most serious problem

**Location.** The problem spans four places:
- §4, where COMBINATION is motivated;
- the abstract, which calls it "a combination principle that the argument from orthogonal errors presupposes";
- the four "costs" in §6;
- the inference in §7 and §10 that G&S's abductive link "does not hold".

**Problem.** The essay's distinctive conclusions are three:
- K is excluded on the multi-dimensional branch.
- KK-defenders must move to meet-normality.
- MARGINS is independent of COMPARABILITY.

All three depend on COMBINATION's "no-addition" direction. G&S's argument does not presuppose that direction, and the essay does not establish it independently.

(i) **G&S's argument is explicitly anti-skeptical.** Denying independence is bad because "allowing errors of one kind to generate ignorance about independent subject matters threatens much of the knowledge we take ourselves to have" (117). G&S describe the multi-dimensional model as accommodating "the kind of epistemic independence that anti-skeptical considerations arguably motivate" (118). K delivers all of that.
- On a product structure with R_B non-empty, project K's R_K(w) onto respect d. The result is exactly B_d ∪ ↑w_d, which depends only on w_d. So K and meet-normality agree on every single-respect proposition.
- What COMBINATION forbids is cross-respect knowledge. A principle that only removes knowledge cannot draw on anti-skeptical motivation.
- The §4 gloss says "one's knowledge about the scale depends on the thermometer's actual condition". That is false of single-respect knowledge about the scale. Only joint knowledge depends on the thermometer.

(ii) **The §6 costs do not supply the missing support.**
- The first cost just restates COMBINATION.
- The second (Gettier shape) and third (knowledge of the conditional lost when its antecedent is learned) are generic. They hold of K's cliff-edge knowledge whenever actuality lies outside belief, in one dimension too. Take G&S's hypothesis (d) with actual error e ≥ c. K's knowledge "error ≤ e" is entailed by the false belief "error < c". It is true because of "c ≤ error ≤ e", which the agent does not know. With STATISM, it is lost on discovering "error ≥ c" whenever c ≤ e < 2c.
- G&S already present K's NO DEFEAT failures as a known and accepted feature (127–128, 131, n. 41).
- The fourth cost is false (Major problem 1).

(iii) **The essay's own exit reproduces the verdict.** §7 offers KK-defenders the option of keeping K with COMPARABILITY, "answering the orthogonality argument as the probabilist does". In the joint context, that view yields the very verdict the essay criticises (Major problem 1).

**Why it matters.** Without COMBINATION, the choice on the product branch is among K, KM and meet. G&S's simplicity premise favours K over both, and their abductive link survives. The thesis shrinks to a conditional whose antecedent is undefended. The essay half-sees a route that does not need COMBINATION (Top change 1), but as written its central claims overreach.

## Major problems

1. **§6 "Fourth", §8 ("No probabilist context rules out (1,2) at (0,2)") and §9.1: false as stated, and the essay runs two separate choices together.**
   - G&S's probabilism is an account of the normality relations. The choice of completion is a separate matter: G&S evaluate their probabilistically motivated models under both K and MARGINS (126–128).
   - Use the figure-5 parameters. The joint-question probabilities (×111²) are: (0,0) 10000; (0,1) and (1,0) 1000; (1,1), (0,2) and (2,0) 100; (1,2) and (2,1) 10; (2,2) 1.
   - Then (0,0) ≫ (1,1), since 12000/12321 ≈ .974. But (0,0) ≫̸ (0,1), since the ratio is ≈ .81. So the joint R_B is {(0,0),(0,1),(1,0)}, and (0,2) lies outside it.
   - K therefore gives R_K(0,2) = ↑(0,2) = {(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1),(0,2),(2,0)}. This excludes (1,2). The K-probabilist knows exactly "if the thermometer is bizarre, the scale is ordinary".
   - Separately, a question that partitions E into {(1,2)} and the rest rules out (1,2) at (0,2) even under I/KM (≈ .999 > .95). That is why G&S want a "reasonably constrained" account of questions (117).
   - §9.1 says "a K-theorist who wanted to rest the exclusion on aggregation would have to give up K-NORMALITY for a probabilist account". This treats the normality-structure choice and the completion choice as one.

   *Why it matters.* The claim that K's verdict is isolated in G&S's space fails. Worse, the verdict follows from K's general rationale (outside belief, and less normal than actuality) under a natural joint ordering. And §7 recommends K plus probabilism as one of the KK-defender's two exits.

   *Fix.* Restrict the claim to probabilists who respect MARGINS and to admissible questions. Confront the K-probabilist directly. Add the symmetric fact: the intersection of the K-probabilist's single-respect accessibility sets is meet-normality. This mirrors Corollary 4.

2. **§6 "Second" and "Third": these costs are generic, not specific to failures of COMBINATION.** As shown above, both features hold of K's cliff-edge knowledge in one dimension. The essay omits two passages of the source that bear directly on them.
   - At 113–114, G&S consider Williamson's (2015) diagnosis that disjunctive accounts of accessibility give rise to Gettier cases. They reject it in favour of a CONVEXITY diagnosis, citing the false prediction that "any disjunction of a justified false belief and an unknown but safely true proposition will be known".
   - At 127–128 and 131, they document K's NO DEFEAT failures.

   *Why it matters.* As written, a K-theorist can reply that these are costs she has already paid.

   *Fix.* Isolate what is distinctive. The known proposition is "nothing is bizarre or the scale is ordinary". Its two disjuncts concern unrelated subject matters, which is the form of Gettier's own Ford/Barcelona case. Then state the result: once COMPARABILITY fails, K satisfies CONVEXITY yet still credits knowledge of a disjunction of a justified false belief and an unknown truth about an unrelated matter. That is a counterexample to the sufficiency of G&S's CONVEXITY diagnosis, and it partly vindicates Williamson's. This is the essay's best route to significance.

3. **§1, §4, §9.3 and Prop. 3(c): COMBINATION versus FACTORIZATION, and scope.**
   - §9.3 says "Section 4 argued that [FACTORIZATION] is the content of the orthogonality claim". But §4 argued only for COMBINATION, and called FACTORIZATION "stronger". §9.1 even concedes that COMBINATION may need restriction where aggregate effects matter.
   - With three or more respects, the default characterisation of meet-normality in 3(c) needs FACTORIZATION. FACTORIZATION forbids all cross-respect knowledge. For example, it forbids knowing at (0,0) that two respects are not both odd when neither respect's oddness can be ruled out. §4 says COMBINATION leaves exactly that open.
   - Scope also matters. G&S hold that knowledge from sources bearing on one quantity does not combine conjunctively, and that inaccurate readings are "epistemically harmful" (129; n. 37; cf. Goldstein and Hawthorne 2022a). They contemplate multi-dimensional models for just such sources (n. 36).
   - So by G&S's lights, the §1 "natural thought" that "one source's failure neither removes nor adds knowledge along the others" is false for sources on the same subject.

   *Fix.* Restrict COMBINATION to respects with independent subject matters, and say what that independence is. Then either argue for FACTORIZATION or confine the default claim in 3(c) to two respects.

4. **Prop. 3(a)–(b): false without R_B ≠ ∅, and the proofs are incomplete.**
   - G&S do not assume that ≫ is well-founded (92 n. 4; cf. n. 20). Take a respect isomorphic to the integers, with x ≫ y iff x ≥ y+2. Then B_d = ∅, so R_B = ∅, and meet-normality gives R_K(w) = ∅. That violates FACTIVITY, whereas K gives ↑w.
   - So two claims fail: "coincides with K-NORMALITY given COMPARABILITY", and "satisfies the five principles … on every multi-dimensional structure". Machine checks on finite structures cannot detect this.
   - Props. 2–3 are given only as "proof sketches", yet the abstract says every formal claim is proved.
   - Meet-normality needs greatest lower bounds. Its behaviour under the discovery dynamics G&S use (124–128) is unexamined, and discoveries can yield cells that are not products.

   *Fix.* Assume R_B ≠ ∅ (finite or ≫-well-founded structures). Alternatively, define meet-normality as ↑w ∪ {v : ∃b ∈ R_B, v ≽ w∧b}, which changes nothing when R_B ≠ ∅. Give full proofs, and say what meet-normality does on cells that are not products.

5. **The target and the novelty claims are miscalibrated.**
   - In the source, the abductive link is heavily hedged: "some abductive support"; "these are only some of the many considerations … we think all other consistent combinations are worthy of serious consideration" (111). Refuting it on condition of a new principle is a small increment.
   - Corollary 4 follows at once from Theorem 1, once each respect's relations are stipulated to be the probabilist's.
   - The essay claims novelty for "the reduction of the probabilist dispute to agglomeration". This overstates: G&S already give the context-by-context reply (121) and place the dispute at agglomeration and the preface (122–123).

   *Fix.* Present Corollary 4 as a sharpening of G&S. Spend the words saved on motivating COMBINATION.

## Minor problems

- **§1, "the second question carries the framework's verdict on KK".** MARGINS settles KK negatively, but denying MARGINS does not secure KK. G&S leave KK's status open (107). They note that even under K, transitivity of R_E is neither necessary nor sufficient for transitivity of R_K (n. 17).
- **§6 "First".** The "single-dimension rationale" given for K is not how G&S motivate it. G&S motivate K as the minimal repair that respects INDUCTIVE LIMITS (104) and as the knowledge default (110). That rationale applies unchanged on products.
- **§4.** In the consumer-grade quotation (116) the respects are *not* orthogonal, since how normal an error is depends on the grade. It is a weak analogue for COMBINATION.
- **§9.1, "provided the knowledge does not involve the actual state of either".** This is opaque. State the condition exactly: COMBINATION allows ruling out (1,1) at (0,0), but forbids ruling out (1,2) at (0,2) once (1,1) or (1,0) is open.
- **The note under the table.** KM's entailment of MARGINS is at 108–110, not "(106, 111)".
- **§1.** G&S 2023 introduce KM. K and I come from earlier work, as the essay says later.
- **Meet-normality is not positioned against the other completions G&S discuss on the figure-4 model in n. 18.** These are the completion considered in G&S 2018 n. 10 and Carter and Goldstein's global characterisation.
- **"Must therefore adopt a completion outside their catalogue" is too strong.** Meet-normality works only on structures with meets (§9.4), so the choice is a trade-off.
- **A cleaner global statement is available.** Where meets exist, KM's second disjunct is {v : w ≫̸ w∧v}, the exact dual of meet-normality. The essay could use this in §9.2 and against G&S's simplicity premise.

## Top five changes

1. **Fix the foundation of the case against K.**
   - Lead with the point Theorem 1 already supports, which needs no COMBINATION. On products, KM is non-disjunctive: it is {v : w ≫̸ w∧v}, that is, I-normality respect by respect. K remains a union of two products.
   - That directly undercuts G&S's premise that "K-NORMALITY is arguably preferable on grounds of its comparative simplicity" (111). It does so for the very kind of incomparability that motivates rejecting COMPARABILITY.
   - Then argue for COMBINATION from the independence of subject matters, in a way that does not equally condemn one-dimensional cliff-edges. Restrict it to respects with independent subject matters, and confront G&S 129 and n. 37 and Goldstein and Hawthorne 2022a.
   - Drop "presupposes", or state the main thesis as a conditional.
2. **Correct the claims about which views agree with K, and confront the K-probabilist.**
   - K plus the probabilist relations, in the joint context, excludes (1,2) at (0,2).
   - In §§6, 8 and 9.1, keep the choice of normality structure separate from the choice of completion.
   - Restrict "no probabilist context" to admissible questions.
   - Add the symmetric corollary: meet-normality is the intersection of the K-probabilist's single-respect sets.
3. **Rebuild §6 around what is distinctive.**
   - Concede that the Gettier shape and the NO DEFEAT failure are generic to K (G&S 115, 127–131).
   - Show that K and meet-normality agree on every single-respect proposition and differ only on cross-respect ones.
   - Then argue the central point. K credits knowledge of a disjunction whose disjuncts concern unrelated subject matters. That is a counterexample to the sufficiency of G&S's CONVEXITY diagnosis of Gettier cases (113–114), and it partly vindicates Williamson's (2015) diagnosis in terms of disjunctive form.
4. **Repair the formal statements.**
   - Add R_B ≠ ∅, or redefine meet-normality as ↑w ∪ {…}.
   - Give full proofs of Props. 2–3.
   - Reconcile §9.3 with §4 on FACTORIZATION versus COMBINATION, or restrict 3(c) to two respects.
   - Say how meet-normality behaves on the cells that are not products, which G&S-style discovery produces.
   - Place meet-normality relative to the completions in G&S n. 18.
5. **Recalibrate the novelty claims and engage the literature.**
   - Present Corollary 4 as a sharpening of G&S 121–123, and acknowledge how hedged the abductive link is (111).
