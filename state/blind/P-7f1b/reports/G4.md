# Referee report — G4 Advocate (defender of knowledge-first decision theory in normality frameworks and of the conditional repair) — P-7f1b

## Thesis and argument as I understand them (≤120 words)

Take finite normality models that are statist and comparable, with K-normality. In these, expected utility conditioned on knowledge (KDT) gives a free question non-negative value in every decision problem exactly when no state compatible with one's knowledge has a post-answer ken that reopens something now excluded. If the "p_v" questions are available, every question is safe exactly when the ken is gapped. Any factive state that contains rational belief also needs gappedness, so on graded evidence only conditioning on evidence is safe. In Goodman–Salow's Bjorn model, KDT gives a free second weighing negative value (about −0.05 at ten-to-one). An expected-utility form of Salow's Conditional Knowledge Dominance conditions on "the options' payoffs differ". A tie-removing perturbation defeats it wherever KDT fails.

## The single most serious problem

**Location: §5 (Theorem 3) and the abstract, thesis box and §7.**

**What the essay tests.** The essay refutes one formalization of the conditional repair. CEU conditions on a single coarse hypothesis, D = "the two options' payoffs differ". Equivalently, it applies conditional knowledge cell by cell to the two-cell partition {D, E∖D}. Theorem 3 works only because a perturbation can make that partition trivial (D = E).

**The natural alternative.** On the essay's own report of Salow's Suitcase, the hypothesis Salow conditions on is something one would *discover* ("over 17.5 kg"). The faithful expected-utility version therefore compares looking with acting now answer by answer:

value(look vs a) = Σ_c w_c · E[u_α − u_a | K^c_E(x)].

Here K^c_E(x) = B(E∩c) ∪ {v∈c : v ⪰ x} is the essay's own conditional-knowledge formula with D := c, and the weights w_c ≥ 0 are arbitrary. This rule is provably safe in exactly the essay's model class, using the essay's own lemmas.

**Proof.**
- Let s ∈ K^c_E(x). Its post-answer ken K_{E∩c}(s) lies inside K^c_E(x):
  - if s ∈ B(E∩c), its ken is B(E∩c), by the chaining step in A.2;
  - if s ⪰ x, then {v ⪰ s} ⊆ {v ⪰ x}.
- These kens are reflexive, transitive and nested (Lemma 3 applied to E∩c).
- So Lemma 2 applies with M := K^c_E(x). It gives E[u_α|K^c] ≥ max_b E[u_b|K^c] ≥ E[u_a|K^c] for every cell.
- Every term is therefore non-negative.

**What follows.**
- The rule never prefers acting now to a free question. That holds for every decision problem and every question, whether or not the ken is gapped.
- When no question is on offer, it coincides with KDT.
- In Bjorn at normal situations, the conditional ken on each reading is B(E∩d). Every state in it has that same post-answer ken, so the rule bets when no scale is offered and looks when one is.
- The same holds in the perturbed Bjorn problem of §5.2. Conditional on a reading that leads to declining, the ken includes losing weights (the essay's own −0.63).

**Why it matters.** Three of the essay's claims hold only for the coarse partition, and for statist comparable models they are false:
- "An expected-utility form of … CKD does not help" (abstract);
- "The expected-utility form … does not escape" (§7);
- "The proposed conditional expected-utility repair has counterexamples wherever the unconditional rule does" (thesis box).

In statist, comparable, K-normality models, the conditional repair succeeds once it is indexed to answers. These are the models in which Theorems 1 and 3 live. This removes one of the three advertised contributions. It also leaves §5.3's non-statist case as the only live challenge to the repair. The choice of partition is a free parameter of any conditional expected-utility rule, and the essay never defends its choice.

## Major problems

1. **Encroachment (§6.3, §6.5).**
   - *Problem.* Theorems 1–3 hold one knowledge assignment fixed across all decision problems. Hawthorne (2004) and Stanley (2005), and Fantl and McGrath (2009), let knowledge vary with the practical situation; the first two are the authors of the reason–knowledge principle. Such views escape every theorem, as §6.5 itself concedes ("This does not show…"). §6.3 answers only the iterated-knowledge variant, which K-normality collapses.
   - *Why it matters.* This is the first reply KDT's own authors would give, and it is left standing.
   - *Fix.* Press the cost the essay disclaims. In Bjorn at ten-to-one, the encroachment criterion the essay reports (via Salow) says something specific: the mere offer of a free second scale destroys Bjorn's knowledge that his weight is within c of r. Yet the fallibilist would still bet. By Theorem 2, on graded evidence every proper knowledge state has some such offer-plus-bet. Argue that this ubiquity is worse than encroachment's usual commitments.

2. **The p_v assumption (Theorem 1(b), Theorem 2, Corollary (ii), end of §4).**
   - *Problem.* The "completion-independence" and "only evidence" results rest entirely on every question p_v being available, where p_v = "nothing sufficiently more normal than v obtains". In effect that is learning that one's situation is somewhat abnormal.
   - *A false sentence.* "Bjorn shows that the questions needed for (ii) can be as natural as a second reading" is false if it means the second reading does the work. A.5 shows that the second reading defeats K-normality kens, not every non-gapped state that contains rational belief.
   - *Counterexample in Bjorn.* Let M_E(x) = {|μ| ≤ 1000} for x inside it, and use K-normality kens after the second reading. M is factive, contains B(E), is a proper subset of E and is not gapped. But every post-answer ken is the interval |μ| ≤ max(c/√2, |μ_s|), which lies inside M. By Lemma 2, the second weighing is safe for M.
   - *Why it matters.* "No refinement of knowledge escapes" (§6.2) holds only relative to an idealized class of questions.
   - *Fix.* State the assumption in each result that uses it. Motivate it, for instance by testimony, as in Goodman and Salow's own §8 discoveries. Correct the sentence.

3. **§5.3.**
   - *Problem.* The "dilemma" requires the repair's defender to use BRN's notion of conditional belief. The defender needs only Salow's notion, which is constrained by post-discovery belief (Salow 2025, 33, as quoted). The essay concedes that the repair works on that reading in its own example. I checked the Flipping numbers: −0.53; D = q; conditional kens {s1} versus {s1, s5}.
   - *Why it matters.* What is shown is that two notions of conditional belief in the Goodman–Salow corpus diverge. That is not a failure of the repair.
   - *Fix.* Either find a non-statist model where the answer-indexed rule with post-discovery kens fails (enumeration is the obvious tool), or downgrade the claim.

4. **§6.6.**
   - *Problem.* The essay argues that Bjorn is not one of Ahmed and Salow's cases because the observation is partitional. But the source of the failure is that KDT's later self does not conditionalize. Knowledge is lost, so post-answer probabilities are not present probabilities conditioned on the reading. At normal situations the KDT plan is a plan that depends on the reading d, but it is not the d-dependent plan the present self would choose. Good's theorem guarantees only the latter.
   - *Why it matters.* Ahmed and Salow's diagnosis can recommend avoidance to an agent who foresees her later self's choices. Alternatively, it recommends the answer-indexed rule. Either way, §6.6's conclusion does not follow.
   - *Fix.* Rewrite §6.6 around updates that are not conditionalization. Relate them to the generalizations of the value-of-information theorem to such learning (see prior work).

5. **Scope of the §6.1 dilemma.**
   - *Problem.* Margins views (I- and KM-normality) get No Defeat in statist comparable models (Goodman and Salow 2023, 128). There, KDT's failures are the known costs of anti-luminosity. So the new phenomenon, evidence avoidance with KK, is confined to K-normality, and through Theorem 1(a) to statism plus comparability.
   - *A point in the essay's favour it misses.* Goodman and Salow (2023, n. 40) report that Goldstein and Hawthorne's (2022a) model of multiple observations predicts defeat even given Margins. So the Margins horn may be less clean than §6.1 concedes.
   - *Fix.* Discuss that model.

## Minor problems
- **Magnitudes depend on c.** I combined Goodman and Salow's definition of "sufficiently more normal" (2023, 121) with their independent-Gaussian assumptions (129), relative to the question of the scales' errors. This gives c² = −2 ln(1−t).
  - So c ≈ 1.96 corresponds to a belief threshold of about .85 on states. (It is the 95% value for the first scale's marginal alone.)
  - At t = .95, c ≈ 2.45. My rough integration then gives about −0.01 at ten-to-one, not −0.05.
  - Report this, and rest the qualitative claim on A.5.
- **Bjorn version of Theorem 3 (§5.2).** ζ must be positive only on decline states inside the ken. With L fixed at 10, the perturbation can flip choices in a sliver near |d| ≈ 1.12c, so "by 0.05" holds only as ε → 0.
- **ε.** A.6 says to pick ε "so small", while §5.2 says ε need not be small. Reconcile them: ε ≠ 1 suffices.
- **Terms and overstatement.** "Ken" is never defined. "Whichever theory of knowledge one adopts" (§1) overstates the result: Lemma 4 needs statism and rational belief defined from normality.
- **References.** Harman, Kripke (2011) and Williamson (the unmarked clock) are invoked but missing from the references.
- **Enumeration.** The figures (64,004; 3,306; about 240,000) are uncheckable; release the scripts.
- **Stakes.** The finite theorems use arbitrarily large L, exactly the stakes KDT's defenders already treat as special. Only the Bjorn computation supports "moderate stakes"; say so.

## Defender's replies

**(i) The view at its strongest.**
- KDT conditions the evidential prior on what one knows.
- Knowledge obeys the normality framework (Factivity, Evidential Knowledge, Inductive Knowledge, Inductive Limits, Convexity), completed by I-, K- or KM-normality.
- Goodman and Salow reject collapse and keep comparability for Bjorn. They accept statism in Bjorn and Flipping but expect it to fail in general (n. 47).
- The authors of the reason–knowledge principle are sympathetic to stakes-sensitive knowledge.
- Salow adds Conditional Knowledge Dominance, with conditional belief constrained by post-discovery belief.

**Replies, and whether they work:**

- **R1. Answer-indexed conditional repair — succeeds** in statist comparable K-normality models (proof above). Theorem 3 does not touch it.
  - Partition matters: conditioning on what one would *do* (act cells) is not safe.
  - My construction:
    - States a, s2, s1, v2, v1 with ranks 0, .2, .5, 1.15, 1.3. u ≫ w iff the rank gap exceeds 1.
    - x = a; Π = {{a}, {s1,v1}, {s2,v2}}.
    - Prior: a .67, s1 .30, v1 .01, s2 .01, v2 .01.
    - Theorem 3's acts, with N = 10, ε = .001, L = 30.4.
  - Every reopening post-answer ken declines. But the conditional ken on "I would decline" is B({s1,v1,s2,v2}) = {s1, s2, v2}: s2 dominates v1 across cells.
  - So the act-cell rule prefers betting now by about .008, while the answer-indexed rule prefers looking.
- **R2. Encroachment — succeeds** against Theorems 1–3 as stated (conceded in §6.5). Its residual cost, knowledge destroyed by a free offer at ten-to-one, is not argued.
- **R3. Collapse — succeeds formally** (Corollary (i)), at the abductive cost Goodman and Salow identify (2023, 102–104). The essay's result could be turned into an argument *for* collapse; the essay should weigh that.
- **R4. Margins — partly succeeds.**
  - There is no evidence-driven defeat in statist comparable models.
  - But KDT still gives the second weighing negative value through KK failure, so the defender must own the costs of anti-luminosity.
  - Goldstein and Hawthorne's model further threatens this escape.
- **R5. "The case violates the view's parameters" — fails.** Bjorn is Goodman and Salow's own statist, comparable, collapse-violating, Gaussian-derived model, and K-normality is one of their live options. Only the magnitudes are sensitive to c.
- **R6. Sophisticated choice (Ahmed and Salow) — partly succeeds.** It defeats §6.6, but it concedes predictable knowledge loss, which Salow's dissolution aimed to avoid.
- **R7. Quarantining large-L bets — partly succeeds.** It disarms the finite theorems as evidence about moderate stakes, but not the Bjorn case.

**Net.**
- What the argument forces: unrepaired KDT, with knowledge fixed across problems, fails Good's theorem at moderate stakes in Goodman and Salow's model under K-normality. I concede that.
- What it does not force: that the conditional repair fails. Outside non-statist models, it does not.
- The case against KDT therefore reduces to this: KDT needs encroachment, collapse, Margins, or answer-indexed evaluation of inquiry. The last is cheap.
