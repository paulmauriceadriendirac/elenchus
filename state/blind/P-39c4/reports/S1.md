# Referee report — S1 Formal epistemologist (Bayesian foundations) — P-39c4

## Thesis and argument as I understand them (≤120 words)

Meehan and Zhang (M&Z) show that, among globally evidentially constant rules, conditionalization maximizes weighted total expected accuracy iff the weighting's learning profile is extensional. The essay reads this condition as coarsening at random (CAR). It then argues three things.
1. A fully supported extensional weighting stays extensional under every small reweighting of its family iff every member is transparent. With opacity, the exact verdict is therefore knife-edge (Prop. 1).
2. Under a quadratic score, conditionalization's regret is a Z-weighted squared distance. It is second-order in perturbations that are small relative to each report's baseline weight (Props. 2–3).
3. Weightings that are equivariant under relabeling but depend on the prior need not be extensional.

Conclusion: an exact vindication needs a defended balance, while approximate accuracy survives imbalance.

## The single most serious problem

**§3 (the gloss on "locally exact") and §6. The headline contrast has no correct normative reading in the essay.**

The essay says local exactness "asks whether the exact recommendation survives uncertainty about how strongly to count the cases." But the benchmark T_λ(f) is *linear in λ*. An evaluator with credence μ over weightings maximizes E_μ T_λ(f) = T_λ̄(f), where λ̄ is the mean weighting. By Fact 11, conditionalization is then exactly optimal iff the *mean* profile is extensional.

Take the essay's own two-state case. Give even odds to tilting by η toward either of the two opaque functions. The mean has L_S(a) = L_S(b) = ½, and conditionalization stays exactly optimal.

So Prop. 1 does not model uncertainty, which averages out. Nor does it model a determinate alternative weighting: that is simply the non-Bayesian position M&Z already describe (pp. 337–338: importance depends on "how likely it is for one to learn p at s"). The instability bites only if the weights are *indeterminate* (imprecise), and the essay never introduces that. Under indeterminacy the upshot is that more than one rule is admissible, not that something is "fragile".

Without a correct account of what local exactness protects against, Prop. 1 is just Fact 11's "only if" plus M&Z's linear-closure footnote (fn. 38, p. 336), restated topologically. The thesis "exact optimality is fragile" then adds no consideration to the "bedrock" dispute M&Z already identify (p. 337). This matters because the essay's claim to a contribution rests on that thesis.

## Major problems

1. **§3, Prop. 1 and the two paragraphs after it.**
   - **Problem.** The increment over M&Z is thin, and the fragility is generic.
     - The "if" half of Prop. 1 is stated by M&Z: a weighting positive on all and only transparent situations is extensional (p. 336), and extensional weightings are closed under linear combinations (fn. 38).
     - The "only if" half follows in one line. If (1−η)λ₀ + ηδ_{E*} were extensional, then δ_{E*} would be extensional by fn. 38, contradicting Fact 12.
     - M&Z themselves call the weighting worry a worry about "the robustness of the explanation" (p. 335), and they call Fact 11 "limitative" (p. 337).
     - Every exact-maximization vindication is fragile in this sense. Strict propriety, on which Facts 10–11 and Greaves–Wallace rest, is a closed, nowhere-dense condition in the space of scoring rules. The essay raises this very analogy in §6 and then leaves it unused.
   - **Why it matters.** The essay's distinctive contribution must be something other than "the extensional set has empty interior".
   - **Fix.** Use the one feature of Prop. 1 that is not generic. Robust exactness under arbitrary positive reweighting holds *iff every weighted situation is transparent*. Aim this at M&Z's claim (pp. 338–339) that "the important question is not whether evidence internalism is true" but whether the weighting is extensional. Prop. 1 shows that internalism is exactly what makes extensionality free and stable, while externalism makes it a balance that needs defending. That is a pointed thesis. Then answer the best externalist reply: families constrained by symmetry, such as the three-state cyclic pair given under Minor problems.

2. **§4 (closing paragraphs) and §6 ("second project").**
   - **Problem.** Approximate optimality does not single out conditionalization. Take any rule f with ‖f(p) − b_p‖ ≤ ε′ at every report. Under Prop. 3's hypothesis and the quadratic score, f's regret at any perturbed λ is at most (ε/(1−ε) + ε′)², the same order as conditionalization's. So a small-regret standard admits a continuum of globally evidentially constant rivals, such as small mixtures with the dogmatic rule. It therefore cannot answer M&Z's explanatory question: why BCOND (Bayesian conditionalization) rather than its rivals? Also, the neighbourhood must be centred on an extensional weighting, so project 2 quietly inherits project 1's justificatory burden.
   - **Why it matters.** The essay's "qualified defense of Bayesian practice" does not defend Bayes in particular.
   - **Fix.** Either (a) prove a selection result, for example that conditionalization minimizes maximum regret over a principled neighbourhood, or show that no such result holds; or (b) accept the consequence. With an imprecise set Λ of weightings containing a neighbourhood as in Prop. 1, the E-admissible rules are {r^λ : λ ∈ Λ}. That set contains conditionalization but not only it, and Prop. 3 bounds its diameter. The result is a bounded permissivism about updating. It connects to M&Z's own remarks on permissive rules (pp. 318–319).

3. **§4, Prop. 3 and the paragraph after it.**
   - **Problem.** The second-order regime is exponentially narrow for M&Z's own benchmark.
     - Under M&Z's uniform weighting, every report has baseline profile c_p = 2^{−(n−1)} (cf. their α_S, p. 344).
     - Tilting mass η toward one opaque situation E gives ε = (2^{n−1} − 1)η. Prop. 3 therefore applies only when η ≪ 2^{1−n}.
     - Beyond that point, the optimal response on E's reports approaches π(·|[E = p]), i.e. META-COND (meta-conditionalization). The regret is then ≈ η·Δ_E, where Δ_E is META-COND's expected-accuracy advantage over BCOND in E. That is first order, not second. The one-shot phenomenon of M&Z's Fact 8 reappears inside the aggregate.
     - This is also a sensitivity to the choice of partition: refining S shrinks the quadratic regime.
   - **Why it matters.** The abstract's "the accuracy cost of a small imbalance is second order" is true only under a proviso that does nearly all the work for realistic state spaces.
   - **Fix.**
     - State the uniform bound R_λ(b) ≤ 2·sup|A|·‖λ − λ₀‖₁. It holds for any bounded score, by linearity in λ and the optimality of b at λ₀.
     - Characterize the crossover between the two regimes.
     - Present smallness to first order as the guarantee that actually operates.

4. **§4, Props. 2–3.**
   - **Problem.** The essay understates how far its results generalize beyond its chosen score, and misses the contrast with M&Z. M&Z's exact theorem holds for *every* strictly proper score. The essay quantifies with one score and then disclaims the rest ("especially near boundaries where a score can be unbounded"). But:
     - (a) On probability vectors, M&Z's own Brier score (summed over all propositions, p. 328) equals 2^{n−2} times the essay's atomic score. M&Z's Table 1 (p. 302) gives −2.25 and −2.08 at the 15 oz. state; the atomic score gives −1.125 and −1.04. So the results transfer exactly.
     - (b) Prop. 2 is the Brier instance of a general fact. For any strictly proper score, regret is the associated Bregman divergence, so R_λ(b) = Σ_p Z_p D(r_p, b_p).
     - (c) For the unbounded simple log score, D = KL ≤ χ², and χ²(r_p‖b_p) = Var_{b_p}(δ_p)/(1+δ̄_p)². Prop. 3's bound therefore holds verbatim; ε²/(1−ε²) in fact suffices for both scores.
   - **Why it matters.** The essay gives up generality its own hypothesis secures, which weakens the stability half of its thesis. It also misses a real asymmetry: M&Z's exact result is robust across scores but knife-edge across weights.
   - **Fix.** State the divergence form and the log-score corollary. Say where the constants depend on curvature.

5. **§5 (neutrality and the "tautology" demand).**
   - **Problem.** The essay attacks arguments no one is shown to hold. M&Z's defence of extensionality does not appeal to symmetry. It is that the importance of updating on p at s should depend "only on the truth of p" (p. 337). The product weighting λ_π makes importance depend on π(s), so it violates M&Z's stated rationale directly and poses no threat to it. The weaker "tautology" demand is likewise unattributed.
   - **Why it matters.** §5 takes about a fifth of the essay without engaging a real position.
   - **Fix.** Either locate a symmetry-based defence and show that the equivariance gap defeats it — the probability-kinematics tradition M&Z flag in fn. 17 (Hughes & van Fraassen 1984) is the obvious place — or cut §5 to one paragraph.

6. **Literature.**
   - **Problem.** Apart from M&Z and three statistics papers, the essay engages none of the work its claims bear on:
     - Schoenfield 2017: for a point mass, eq. (1) *is* META-COND, so r_p is "aggregate meta-conditionalization". This link joins the CAR reading to the externalist literature.
     - Carr 2021: the subjective reading of the weights that §2 discusses. M&Z contrast their objective reading with hers in fn. 35, p. 330.
     - Levinstein 2019: state-dependent importance, which §6 calls "another genuine alternative". M&Z cite it on p. 339.
     - Greaves & Wallace 2006: the "if" half of Prop. 1 is the aggregate form of their result.
     - Heitjan & Rubin 1991, which originated CAR.
     - Work on robust and imprecise Bayesian analysis, which is the natural home for "robustness to how strongly to count the cases".
   - **Why it matters.** The contribution cannot be located against its nearest neighbours.
   - **Fix.** See Top five changes, item 5.

## Minor problems

- **Abstract, last sentence.** "without confusing either with the rediscovery of coarsening at random" reads like a reply to an unseen referee. A judge will not know what it guards against.
- **§1 CAR paragraph and §3 "Nor is the broader contrast…".**
  - "Multicovers", "procedural robustness", "three-pair example" and "CAR mechanisms extend beyond mixtures of partitions" are never defined.
  - The section pinpoints (§3, §§2–4, §4) come without any statement of what those sections prove.
  - The three-pair structure is easy to exhibit and should be. On S = {1,2,3}, take the two cyclic opaque functions E_A = (1↦{1,2}, 2↦{2,3}, 3↦{1,3}) and E_B = (1↦{1,3}, 2↦{1,2}, 3↦{2,3}), each with weight ½. This gives c_{12} = c_{13} = c_{23} = ½: an extensional profile generated entirely by opaque functions, and not a mixture of partitions. §6 needs this example for its claim about profiles "generated entirely from opaque learning functions".
- **§4 two-state illustration.** A uniform weight over all four factive functions is, after normalization, M&Z's own Hi-TEA benchmark (p. 329). Say so: it is the dialectically decisive instance of Prop. 1.
- **Prop. 3, hypothesis and bound.**
  - "Positive constants c_p on all reports under consideration" should read "every report active under the perturbed weighting has c_p > 0".
  - The bound is loose. ‖r_p − b_p‖₂² ≤ max_s b_p(s)·χ²(r_p‖b_p) ≤ ε²/(1−ε²). In the two-state case the true value is η²/4.
- **"Fixed-direction perturbation" (§4)** is never defined. It should be λ₀ + ηd with Σd = 0.
- **"Modest impossibility consequence" (§3).** This only says that the argmax moves with λ; the label inflates it.
- **The figure 0.000025 has no scale.** On the same normalized benchmark, conditionalization scores −0.25 and the dogmatic rule −0.5. Report regret relative to gaps like that.
- **§2, last paragraph.** The sentence on "a non-partition CAR construction" has no visible antecedent in the essay. The point it seems to make — extensional profiles need not be mixtures of partitions, yet they select the same rule — needs the example above.
- **§6.** "The claim that mathematical fragility alone refutes the proposal" is attributed to no one.
- **Definition of regret.** Regret should be defined explicitly relative to the best *globally evidentially constant* rule. At present this is only implicit.
- **Length.** The essay uses about 2,900 of 6,000 words, and it is most compressed exactly where a reader needs help: the CAR material and the target of §5.

## What is genuinely good (be specific: revisions must preserve this)

- **The literal CAR reading.** L_p(s) is a reporting probability, eq. (1) is conditioning on receipt of a report, and extensionality is CAR. M&Z never mention CAR. For philosophers this connects Fact 11 to a mature statistical literature. Keep it, expand it, and link it to META-COND.
- **The exact form of Prop. 1.** Robust exactness under arbitrary positive reweighting holds iff every weighted situation is transparent. This is the most promising result in the essay, because it locates precisely where internalism re-enters M&Z's picture.

- **Two useful separations.** §2 separates "same maximizer" from "same ranking of rivals"; §4 separates stability of the maximizer from stability of accuracy. Both are real distinctions worth keeping.
- **§5's mathematics.** The distinction between equivariance and invariance is neat and correct, as is the closed form H(s) = 2^{n−2}(1+π(s)).
- **Calibrated tone.** The essay claims no more novelty than its proofs support.

## Proof check

Python was not available, so everything below was checked by hand.

- **Setup identities.**
  - Σ_{p∋s} L_p(s) = Σ_E λ(E) = 1 ✓, since each E delivers exactly one p at s, and p ∋ s by factivity.
  - The rearrangement T = Σ_p Σ_{s∈p} π(s)L_p(s)A(f(p),s) is correct ✓ (cf. M&Z p. 345).
  - Σ_p Z_p = 1 ✓.
- **Eq. (1).** T = Σ_p Z_p·E_{r_p}A(f(p),·), and strict propriety gives r_p as the unique maximizer at each active p ✓ (= M&Z Lemma 1).
- **Extensionality ⇔ r_p = b_p.**
  - If L_p ≡ c on p, then r_p = b_p.
  - Conversely, r_p = b_p forces L_p(s) = Z_p/π(p) on p. If L_p(t) = 0 for some t ∈ p, then r_p(t) = 0 < b_p(t), by regularity of the prior ✓.
- **Prop. 1.**
  - *"If":* a transparent E has an indicator profile that is constant on each delivered p, and mixtures preserve constancy ✓.
  - *"Only if":* if E* is opaque, there are s, t ∈ p = E*(s) with E*(t) ≠ p ✓. Then L^η_p(s) − L^η_p(t) = [(1−η)c_p + η] − (1−η)c_p = η ✓. Since Z_p ≥ η·π(s) > 0, p is active ✓, and strict propriety gives a strict loss ✓.
  - *Geometry:* the extensional set is the simplex cut by a linear subspace. It has nonempty relative interior iff it contains every vertex, i.e. iff every member is transparent ✓.
  - *Two-state case:* the extensional set is {λ(E₃) = λ(E₄)}, a two-dimensional slice of the 3-simplex ✓.
  - The "modest impossibility" claim is correct ✓.
- **Prop. 2.**
  - E_r A(q,·) = −‖q‖² + 2⟨r,q⟩ − 1, so E_rA(r) − E_rA(q) = ‖r−q‖² ✓. Weighting by Z_p and summing is correct ✓.
  - General form: for any strictly proper score with generalized entropy G, regret = Σ_p Z_p·D_G(r_p, b_p), where D_G is the Bregman divergence.
- **Two-state example.**
  - At λ₀ every true report has profile ½ ✓.
  - Under λ_η: L_S(a) = (1+η)/2, L_S(b) = (1−η)/2, L_{a}(a) = (1−η)/2, L_{b}(b) = (1+η)/2 ✓.
  - Z_S = ½, Z_{a} = (1−η)/4, Z_{b} = (1+η)/4; these sum to 1 ✓.
  - r_S = ((1+η)/2, (1−η)/2) ✓.
  - Regret = ½·(η²/4 + η²/4) = η²/4 ✓; at η = 0.01 this is 2.5×10⁻⁵ ✓.
  - For scale: T(b) = −¼ and the dogmatic rule scores −½.
  - Under the log score the regret is ½·KL ≈ η²/4 as well.
- **Prop. 3.**
  - r_p(s) = b_p(s)(1+δ_p(s))/(1+δ̄_p) ✓, which gives the difference formula ✓.
  - Mean absolute deviation ≤ SD ≤ ε ✓; 1+δ̄_p ≥ 1−ε ✓; ‖·‖₂ ≤ ‖·‖₁ ✓; Σ_p Z_p = 1 ✓. **The bound is valid.**
  - Sharper: χ²(r_p‖b_p) = Var_{b_p}(δ_p)/(1+δ̄_p)² ≤ (ε²−δ̄²)/(1+δ̄)² ≤ ε²/(1−ε²), and ‖r_p−b_p‖₂² ≤ max_s b_p(s)·χ².
  - Two-state check: ε = η gives the bound η²/(1−η)², against the true value η²/4 ✓.
  - **An extension the essay denies itself.** For the simple log score, regret = Σ_p Z_p·KL(r_p‖b_p) ≤ Σ_p Z_p·ln(1+χ²) ≤ ε²/(1−ε²). The same bound holds for an unbounded score.
- **"Order at most the square of its size."**
  - Correct when every report touched by the direction has c_p > 0, since then ε = η·max|D_p(s)|/c_p.
  - If a touched report has c_p = 0, then Z_p = O(η) and the posterior shift is O(1), so the regret is first order.
  - A uniform bound holds for any bounded score. Since |T_λ(f) − T_λ₀(f)| ≤ sup|A|·‖λ−λ₀‖₁ and T_λ₀(r^λ) ≤ T_λ₀(b), we get R_λ(b) ≤ 2·sup|A|·‖λ−λ₀‖₁.
- **M&Z's uniform benchmark.**
  - c_p = 2^{−(n−1)} for every p, since α_S/|ℰ| = (2^{n−1})^{n−1}/(2^{n−1})^n.
  - Tilting by η toward E gives δ ∈ {−η, (2^{n−1}−1)η}, so ε = (2^{n−1}−1)η.
  - When η/c → ∞, r_p → π(·|[E = p]) (META-COND) and R ≈ η·Δ_E.
  - **Worked case: M&Z's Dana/Noah prior space**, π = (0.2, 0.6, 0.2), tilting toward Dana's E.
    - Z_{15,16} = Z_{16,17} = 0.2 exactly.
    - r_{15,16} = (0.25+0.75η, 0.75−0.75η, 0).
    - r_S ∝ (0.2(1−η), 0.6(1+3η), 0.2(1−η)).
    - Regret = 0.45η² + 0.3456η²/(1+1.4η) ≈ 0.80η² in the essay's normalization, against Prop. 3's bound of about 9η² (ε = 3η). The two are consistent.
- **§5.**
  - *Invariance ⇒ extensionality:* the transposition σ = (s t) fixes p, and E ↦ σ·E (with (σ·E)(u) = σ(E(σ⁻¹u))) preserves factivity and maps {E : E(s) = p} onto {E : E(t) = p} ✓.
  - λ_π is equivariant ✓.
  - H(s) = 2^{n−1}π(s) + 2^{n−2}(1−π(s)) = 2^{n−2}(1+π(s)) ✓.
  - By the product structure, L_p(s) = π(p)/H(s), and these sum to 1 over p ∋ s ✓.
  - The posterior is ∝ π(s)/(1+π(s)) ✓. For the prior (4/5, 1/5): (0.8/1.8, 0.2/1.2) ∝ (4/9, 1/6), which normalizes to (8/11, 3/11) ✓.
  - Sanity check: a uniform prior gives an extensional profile ✓.
- **Tautology example** ✓. E never reports S, and {a,b} is delivered only at b.
- **§6, "generated entirely from opaque learning functions"** ✓. Witness: the cyclic pair E_A, E_B given under Minor problems. Every pair has profile ½ at both its states, and the profile is not a mixture of partitions, since any partition of {1,2,3} that contains a pair also contains a singleton.

**Result.** Every stated result holds and I found no counterexample. Two points are understated rather than wrong: the log-score extension and the uniform first-order bound. One proviso, Prop. 3's relative-smallness hypothesis, is far more restrictive for M&Z's benchmark than the essay admits.
