# Referee report — S5 (Philosopher of language / formal semanticist) — P-c865

## Thesis and argument as I understand them (≤120 words)

Dorr and Mandelkern (D&M) present Flattening, p>(pq>r) ↔ pq>r, as prima facie valid for both moods. The essay argues that it fails for counterfactuals. In the Reunion, the rule form with outer antecedent A∨B falsifies each half from (N), (J) and possibility premises, without using CEM. Theorem 1: in C2 plus either half, ¬(A>B), ¬(B>A) and X>(A∨B) entail (D), on which whichever of the two came would have depended counterfactually on the other's absence. (D) is false in the Reunion and Buttons cases and true in the Avoidance control. Diagnosis: Flattening amounts to cumulative imaging, which requires rankings to agree beyond a shared world, and world-fixed counterfactual rankings violate this. So a mood-neutral "if" should not validate Flattening.

## The single most serious problem

**Location.** §3 together with §§5.1–5.2: the step from the cases to "Flattening is invalid for counterfactuals".

**The problem.** The essay never shows that the two sides of a single Flattening instance diverge under one fixed interpretation of ">". That is what a counterexample to a validity claim needs. Everything turns on enriched, nested judgments:
- (J) is elicited with "still", with "even if Ann had come / had turned up", and with a "would" that is modally subordinated to the previous antecedent.
- The falsity of (D) is itself a judgment about a counterfactual nested inside a counterfactual.

The essay never states, let alone tests, the bare minimal pair that D&M's method calls for (D&M pp. 20–22):
- (a) "If one of them had come, then if Ann had come, Bob would have come."
- (b) "If Ann had come, Bob would have come."

That pair has a salient exclusive reading. "One of them" raises the question of which one came, and "if Ann had come" is then naturally heard as "if it had been Ann (who came)". On that reading (a) and (b) are both false, so on D&M's own test the pair passes. The essay gets its inclusive reading ("Ann came too") only through "even", "still" and "turned up". A defender of Flattening will say that this lexical material shifts the domain or the ordering.

The essay's replies to the two ways of relocating the divergence both assume the point at issue:
- Relocating it into the index (§5.1): the reply rests on "an index reached by supposition contains exactly what the scenario contains".
- Relocating it into the context (§5.2): the reply says a shift "concedes that the conditional as interpreted in context does not obey Flattening".

**Why it matters.** What the essay shows is that world-fixed orderings violate flatness. D&M already grant this for similarity orderings (pp. 19–20, 24). It has not yet shown that the English counterfactual violates Flattening, and that is the thesis.

## Major problems

1. **§5.2, context shift.**
   - *Problem.* The reply misidentifies the question. Flattening, like Contraction, is a claim about validity under a fixed interpretation. D&M explicitly treat felt inequivalences that come from re-indexing (tense, anaphora) as non-counterexamples, and they draw the parity with Contraction (pp. 20–21). Saying the sentences can be "asserted together without any felt shift" is no evidence: neither modal subordination nor domain shifts triggered by "even" are felt.
   - *Why it matters.* This is the defender's first move, and it has independent motivation in dynamic and contextualist accounts of counterfactual discourse. The essay engages none of them.
   - *Fix.*
     - Give single-sentence minimal pairs that vary only "even/still/too" and the inclusive versus exclusive construal of the inner antecedent.
     - Argue that the inclusive reading can be forced by additive particles that leave truth conditions unchanged ("if Ann had come too").
     - Show that any shift strong enough to save Flattening would overgenerate, for instance by predicting divergence in D&M's espresso and France pairs, or by failing Contraction-style controls.

2. **§5.1, the sequence theorist.**
   - *Problem: the index claim.* "An index reached by supposition contains exactly what the scenario contains" is exactly what sequence semantics denies. Its indices share categorical facts and differ on conditionals. D&M p. 13 describe this as a possible metaphysical gloss and credit the picture to Khoo 2022, whom the essay does not cite.
   - *Problem: (Independence).* The essay says it is accepted by "any non-backtracking theory". That is false: it fails on Lewis-1979-style metrics with chancy consequents, which the essay itself concedes in §5.5 (via Goodsell 2022). The Reunion's own premise (N) depends on a babysitter's cancellation, which is plausibly chancy.
   - *Problem: the interventionist argument.* This is the right argument but it is only gestured at. Structural-equation models do not treat a disjunctive antecedent as a single intervention, so they do not by themselves fix which "Bob scenario" "had one of them come" reaches. And the sequence theorist already rejects the supervenience of conditionals on categorical facts.
   - *Why it matters.* This is where the dispute is decided.
   - *Fix.*
     - Restrict Independence to deterministic, local cases and lead with the Buttons.
     - Engage the published sequence treatments D&M list in their fn. 4, especially the one on counterfactual probability (Schultheis 2022).
     - Make the causal point an argument with premises the sequence theorist must accept. For example, a fully specified causal model of a scenario fixes its dependence facts, so route-relative dependence would have that same model give different verdicts according to which supposition led to it.

3. **§6, and the closing claim of §4.1: the positive story overgenerates.**
   - *Problem.* The "intuitive semantics ... available for indicatives" consists of refinement of an information state, conditioning, and Kratzer-style restriction. All three validate Import-Export, not just Flattening: restricting by p and then by q is restricting by pq for any q, and Pr(·|Y)(·|X) = Pr(·|X∩Y) for any X. Given C2, Import-Export yields Materialism (D&M p. 21). So this story cannot be the explanation D&M's open problem asks for (p. 24). That explanation must separate the sequence "p, then pq" from the sequence "p, then q".
   - The essay also leans on Stalnaker's Thesis to motivate cumulative indicative supposition. But D&M §7 show that a strong form of the Thesis, (B>L>|B), holds in tree models that invalidate Flattening. There, Boolean instances can have probability below 1 (Thm 7.6, fn. 36).
   - *Fix.* Either supply an information-state semantics that validates Flattening but not Import-Export over C2 (for example, a forward search over states, analogous to tails), or withdraw the claim to have supplied D&M's intuitive semantics.

4. **§§1, 3, 4: the contribution claims are overstated.**
   - Theorem 1 takes a few lines: CEM plus the rule form, following D&M's derivation of H (pp. 18–19), as the essay acknowledges.
   - In C2 its "purely negative" premises are ordinary would-nots: ¬(A>B) ≡ A>¬B given ◇A, which the essay itself notes in §5.5. Its content is that Cautious Exportation carries the actual world's would-nots into the supposed scenario. That is a good gloss, not a new result.
   - "The next result needs neither [(P)] nor a judgment about a nested counterfactual" is false. Using the theorem against Flattening requires (D) to be false, and that is a nested judgment.
   - "A logic without Conditional Excluded Middle does no better" is true of Observation 2. It is not true of Theorem 1, whose proofs use CEM essentially.
   - Proposition 3 and Agreement are two-line restatements of (Flat).
     - D&M already state the metric form of (Flat) (p. 24).
     - Their linear-frame picture (p. 27: a "single master order", the "clearest semantic picture") is the essay's "carries forward the standpoint".
   - *Fix.* State the increment over D&M exactly. In my view it is the case type plus the (a)–(c) explanation of why their test pairs pass.

5. **§4.1: terminology and neighbouring literature.**
   - *Problem.* "Cumulative" has a fixed sense in conditional and nonmonotonic logic: Cautious Monotonicity plus Cut. C2 already validates that property, and D&M discuss it on p. 23 under the names Cautious Monotonicity and Cautious Transitivity. The essay means something else: invariance under iterated supposition. In iterated belief revision that is Darwiche and Pearl's postulate C1. The essay also never answers D&M's "cautious cousin" analogy (p. 23), which is the obvious argument for Flattening it needs to defeat.
   - *Fix.* Rename the property and relate it to C1 and to the update-versus-revision contrast. Answer the analogy, for example by noting that the cautious principles involve one supposition while Flattening involves iterated supposition.

## Minor problems

- **"Most cautious step."** The essay says "Flattening is the most cautious step one can take from C2 toward Import-Export" (§1) and calls it "the most cautious fragment" (§7). This is false. Each half of Flattening is strictly weaker, and so is Crashing Cautious Exportation, which is equivalent to 4 in C2 (D&M p. 18). Theorem 1 itself uses only one half.
- **"Requires" in §4.3.** The section says divergence "requires" (a)–(c). D&M's line example diverges without (b). These are conditions for robust, hearable divergence, not necessary conditions.
- **Mood in §4.3.** It applies a diagnosis of counterfactual supposition to D&M's conflagration pair (2), which is indicative.
- **Computational checks.** These cannot be verified as reported: the enumeration of 65,536 frames (the count, 16^4, is right), the Z3 runs up to five worlds, and the four-world "weighted departures" models. They also add nothing to results already proved. Release the code and models, or cut them.
- **"Counterfactual profile"** carries the thesis but is never defined.
- **"Instance."** Calling D&M's metric remark an "instance" of Agreement is loose. The remark states (Flat) for metrics; Agreement is a consequence of (Flat).
- **§5.4, the existential antecedent.** "The existential version has no disjunction to individuate" ignores that indefinites raise alternatives just as disjunction does, in the alternative and inquisitive frameworks the essay itself cites. The exclusive "which one?" reading is, if anything, more salient with "someone from their year".
- **Briggs 2012.** The essay cites it for structural-equation semantics. If I recall correctly (about 65%), Briggs reads disjunctive antecedents universally. By the essay's own §5.3, that makes (P) and (J) false. Acknowledge this.
- **Summary of D&M's case.** It omits their redundancy argument (p. 20: the outer antecedent "feels like it's doing nothing") and the cautious-cousin analogy (p. 23).
- **Which logic?** The essay never says which logic it proposes for each mood. Its cases leave the crashing halves untouched (Crashing Cautious Exportation is equivalent to 4; Crashing Cautious Importation yields H). So does it hold that counterfactuals validate C2 + 4 + H but not Flattening? D&M's corollary that C2.F is not axiomatizable by modal principles (p. 29) makes this a natural intermediate logic to discuss.

## Prior work this resembles (only if confident; author, year, title, confidence %)

- **Darwiche & Pearl, 1997, "On the logic of iterated belief revision", *Artificial Intelligence* 89 (90%).** Postulate C1 (if α ⊨ μ then (Ψ∘μ)∘α ≡ Ψ∘α) is the essay's "cumulativity", stated for iterated revision. Its semantic counterpart (revision preserves the prior ordering among μ-worlds) parallels flatness and Agreement.
- **Katsuno & Mendelzon, 1991, "On the difference between updating a knowledge base and revising it", KR '91 (85%).** Update works world by world and revision works globally. This is the essay's imaging-versus-conditioning and world-versus-information-state contrast.
- **Kraus, Lehmann & Magidor, 1990, "Nonmonotonic reasoning, preferential models and cumulative logics", *Artificial Intelligence* 44 (90%).** The standard sense of "cumulativity".
- **Joyce, 1999, *The Foundations of Causal Decision Theory* (85%).** Treats indicative supposition as conditioning and subjunctive supposition as imaging.
- **Stalnaker, 1975, "Indicative Conditionals", *Philosophia* 5 (90%).** Indicative selection is constrained by the context set. This is a classic statement of the essay's closing contrast between supposing from an information state and supposing from a world.
- **Context shift in counterfactual discourse, relevant to §5.2:**
  - von Fintel, 2001, "Counterfactuals in a Dynamic Context" (85%).
  - Gillies, 2007, "Counterfactual Scorekeeping", *Linguistics and Philosophy* 30 (85%).
  - Moss, 2012, "On the Pragmatics of Counterfactuals", *Noûs* 46 (80%).
- **Roberts, 1989, "Modal Subordination and Pronominal Anaphora in Discourse", *Linguistics and Philosophy* 12 (90%).** Bears on the continuation "he would have been there" in (J).
- **Lewis, 1973, "Causation", *Journal of Philosophy* 70 (95%).** Source of the definition of counterfactual dependence used in §3; not cited.
- **Kratzer & Shimoyama, 2002, "Indeterminate Pronouns: The View from Japanese" (80%).** Treats indefinites as raising alternatives.
- **Works cited by D&M that the essay omits.** I confirmed only that D&M cite them; I have not read them.
  - Schultheis 2022, "Counterfactual Probability", *J Phil*. D&M fn. 4 list it among sequence-semantics works.
  - Khoo 2022, *The Meaning of If*. D&M p. 13 credit the factual/non-factual picture to it.
  - Kaufmann 2023, "Bernoulli Semantics and Ordinal Semantics for Conditionals".
  - Mandelkern 2024, *Bounded Meaning*. D&M p. 22 say its error theory of Import-Export relies on Flattening.

## What is genuinely good (be specific: revisions must preserve this)

- **Precise targeting.** The essay goes after D&M's actual evidential claims: p. 20 ("checked many instances") and p. 22 ("appears valid for both"). Quotations and page references are exact.
- **A counterexample of a new type, in the right fragment.** It uses no metric. It stays within right-nesting with Boolean antecedents (L_B>), which is where D&M's test pairs live, so it sidesteps their left-nesting caveats (pp. 34–36). It falsifies both halves, and Observation 2 needs no CEM.
- **The Avoidance control.** It holds the negative premises fixed and varies only genuine dependence, and the verdict on (D) flips. This is the methodologically best part of the paper, and it answers the worry that the judgment is "an Import-Export intuition in disguise".
- **The Buttons.** The case is deterministic, spacelike-separated and symmetric, and Theorem 1 handles every tie-break. It is the cleanest case and should lead.
- **The (a)–(c) diagnosis.** It explains why D&M's espresso and France pairs pass and why their line example is hard to hear. The interventionist point adds why spot checks with conjunctive, variable-setting antecedents cannot find counterexamples. This is a real explanatory gain.
- **The consequence for the unity of "if"** is drawn correctly from D&M's own reasoning (p. 22).
- **Honest crediting** of the parallel with D&M's derivation of H, of their metric remark, and of their fn. 36.
