# Referee report — S2 (epistemic logician / knowledge-first epistemologist) — P-b4ea

## Thesis and argument as I understand them (≤120 words)

Carter and Hawthorne (C&H) answer Ex Ante Dogmatism with Transparency: further draws can give higher-order knowledge. The note makes two claims.

(1) Suppose each level's witness lengths are unbounded, draws are independent, and knowing^n entails knowing at every lower level. Then from any history, every finite level is almost surely available at arbitrarily late times. The proof uses a uniform 1/3 success bound at selected witness lengths.

(2) These conditions do not guarantee a finite expected time to reach level 2. In a stipulated hierarchy (K at red-proportion ≥1/2; K^n at length ≥n and proportion ≥2/3), the score 3r−2m is a centred walk, so optional stopping gives E[τ]=∞.

The note says explicitly that this does not refute C&H.

## Verdict
Prize: not finalist
Journal recommendation: reject

Short answers to the three questions I was asked:
- **Is the core result correct?** Yes. Both propositions and the numerical table check out (see Proof check).
- **Is it new relative to the source?** Only marginally.
  - Proposition 1 strengthens C&H's "you could come to know^n … by making some number of additional draws" (p. 12) to almost-sure recurrence. The step is elementary, and it supports C&H rather than challenging them.
  - The infinite-mean point is absent from C&H.
- **Does it bear on a live argument enough to carry an essay?** No, for three reasons.
  - C&H's reply needs only possibility (p. 7, clause (i)).
  - C&H already concede that the ideal agent draws indefinitely (p. 13).
  - The infinite mean is a knife-edge artifact. It reverses on the natural realization of C&H's own picture.

## The single most serious problem

**Location:** §3, the hierarchy behind the "Infinite-duration proposition", and the framing in the Finding and §5 ("a precise separation … within the transparency framework").

**The problem.** The note's only philosophically live claim rests on a hierarchy the target view's own motivating picture rules out. The hierarchy does two things:
- It turns C&H's deliberately weak *sufficient* condition (at least 2/3 red at the witness length) into a *necessary* condition for every level above the first.
- It puts that threshold exactly at the true chance of red. That makes the evidence score drift-free, and zero drift is the entire source of E[τ]=∞.

Both choices conflict with C&H's text:
- **p. 14.** Transparency "fits well with a picture on which the level of higher-order knowledge that p an agent possesses depends on the strength of her evidence for p". Agents at different levels "may only differ in the strength of evidence".
- **p. 16.** Transparency "is just the generalization of Anti-Skepticism to knowledge^n".
- **fn 2 (p. 2).** The anti-skeptic "will also want to accept" knowledge from "a sufficiently long sequence of draws on which x of the draws are red for some x such that 1/2 < x < 2/3". C&H use the 2/3 principle only as the "weaker" one that suffices for the dogmatist's point.

**How far the model departs from that picture.**
- In the note's model, RRR gives K^3 p, but 3,000 draws with 1,998 red give only K p. The likelihood ratio of RRB over RBB is 8 in the first case and 2^996 in the second.
- The starting history RRBB is credited with first-order knowledge at a likelihood ratio of exactly 1.

**The natural realization reverses the result.** I sketch it as a model (my construction, not the note's):
- **The model.**
  - Let ℓ(h) = (2r − m)·log 2, the log-likelihood ratio of RRB over RBB.
  - Worlds are triples (composition, draw sequence, θ), where θ is an unknown knowledge cutoff and δ > log 2 is a margin.
  - At time m, w accesses w′ iff w′ has the same history, |θ′ − θ(w)| ≤ δ, and w′ is an RRB-world whenever ℓ(h) ≥ θ(w); reflexivity is added.
  - At RRB-worlds this gives: K^n p iff ℓ(h) ≥ θ + (n−1)δ.
- **It satisfies every condition the note uses.**
  - Descent (factivity).
  - T, with k_n = ⌈3(θ+(n−1)δ)/log 2⌉.
  - Cofinal witness sets W_N, and F.
  - Independent draws.
  - Known outcomes.
  - Defeat.
  - The higher-order ignorance at first acquisition that C&H require (fn 16 and fn 18). Each draw moves ℓ by log 2 < δ, so the first draw giving K^n never gives K^{n+1}.
- **Its expected times are finite.** Under the true law ℓ has drift (1/3)·log 2 > 0. By Wald's identity, the expected time from any history h to level n is at most 3(θ+(n−1)δ−ℓ(h))/log 2 + 3. That is finite for every n and grows linearly in n.

**Why it matters.** The "separation" exists only at a boundary that C&H's own picture of evidential strength excludes. On that picture, every fixed level is affordable in expectation. Only the ideal (all-levels) aim is unaffordable, and C&H already say so (p. 13: such aims "will require you to continue to draw balls from the bag indefinitely"). So the note's increment is a non-entailment from thin formal conditions, not a finding about the transparency framework. The phrase "affordable inquiry" in the Finding overstates it.

## Major problems

1. **§§2–4: no epistemic model, and the obvious one collapses into KK.**
   - **What the note offers.** H_n is a stipulated function of the history. The §4 "chain" frames are standard non-transitive frames, built separately for each history and never linked to the draws.
   - **The lemma.** C&H treat outcomes as known (Minimality (i), p. 7; p. 16: "Upon that outcome occurring, you know the antecedent"). Given that, any model in which knowledge of p at RRB-worlds depends only on the history validates KK for p. Proof: if K p holds at w, every accessible world is an RRB-world with the same history, so K p holds there too; iterate.
   - **What follows.** The profile "H_1 without H_2 at RRBB" requires accessible RRB-worlds with the same history at which p is not known. That needs a hidden parameter (a cutoff, a normality ranking or a margin). The note never supplies one, and supplying it in the natural way (above) changes both the profile and the cost result.
   - **A further defect.** H_1 at proportion ≥1/2 credits knowledge at RRBB, and at every history with proportion exactly 1/2, where the evidence is equally likely under p and ¬p. No safety or normality account licenses that, and fn 2's strict "1/2 < x … sufficiently long" excludes it.
   - **Fix.** A dynamic model with explicit worlds, accessibility and a source of higher-order ignorance (C&H fn 18), with every verdict checked.

2. **§5: no premise of C&H's argument is engaged.**
   - **What C&H's reply needs.** The principle they rebut (p. 7, lines 303–307) says an agent must decline evidence if she "could not come to satisfy the aim of any inquiry". Their reply needs only that she *could* (pp. 12, 15). Almost-sure recurrence and infinite expected cost both leave that untouched.
   - **Where cost could bite.** C&H frame their reply in terms of epistemic permissibility. Value and risk enter only in §4.1.2 (p. 14: "how much value they assign to satisfying various norms and on the relative risks"), where non-ideal agents choose which requirements to aim at. Relatedly, §4.1.1 (p. 13) offers aiming at know^n for some finite n as the resource-sensitive fallback.
   - **Why the note's result does not reach it.** If even K^2 had infinite expected cost, that fallback would inherit the problem. The note does not make this connection, and on the natural model the fallback is in fact affordable.
   - **Fix.** Either target §§4.1.1–4.1.2, or recast Ex Ante Dogmatism in expected-value terms and defend a bridge from expected cost to permissibility or to the coherence of aims. C&H's fn 13 premise (one cannot aim to X if X is impossible) is a template.

3. **§3, the decision-theoretic gloss: whose expectation?**
   - "Expected net payoff −∞" is computed under the sampling law conditional on the true composition. §2 itself says this "is not automatically the inquirer's rational credence".
   - Under any credence that gives RBB positive weight, the claim is trivial and the random walk does no work. By factivity H_2 is never attained at RBB-worlds, so no policy is almost surely successful, and every success-insisting policy has infinite expected length.
   - The non-trivial reading needs knowledge-conditioned probabilities. That is the K1/normality territory of Hong's paper, which the note sets aside.
   - **Fix.** Fix the evaluative probability and defend the choice.

4. **§1: the "correction" is smaller than claimed, and Proposition 1 vindicates C&H.**
   - **F is derivable from the source.** The note says F "must remain explicit" because fns 16 and 18 do not state it. But fn 18 ends: "The same reasoning, we take it, will extend, mutatis mutandis, to ignorance of what is known^n, for any n." With factivity, that entails F:
     - Along the all-red sequence, let f_n be the first draw giving K^n.
     - Factivity gives f_n ≤ f_{n+1}.
     - The fn 18 extension gives f_{n+1} ≠ f_n.
     - So f_n is strictly increasing in n, and no finite all-red history supplies every level.
   - **The "bridge" is already there.** The note says the displayed formula needs a bridge to the local application on p. 15. The source supplies it: Transparency, factivity and fn 18 together give cofinal witness lengths.
   - **Proposition 1 then supports C&H.** Under independent sampling, it vindicates C&H's universal supposition on p. 12 ("whenever … you could come to know^n … by making some number of additional draws").
   - **Fix.** Present it that way. It is a modest, honest contribution, not a correction.

5. **Form and literature: this is not an essay, and the core literature is missing.**
   - **Form.** The text is an internal research log: "A6 v0.1", "A4", "adversarial review", "pipeline code", F used in §1 before it is defined in §2, and "K1" never defined. A prize reader cannot use it.
   - **Literature.** It cites C&H, Brown and Hong, but none of the iterated-knowledge work in C&H's own bibliography that bears directly on realization:
     - Williamson 2000;
     - Das & Salow 2018;
     - Goodman & Salow 2018;
     - Bacon 2014 on prior vs bonus knowledge;
     - Dorr, Goodman & Hawthorne 2014;
     - Goldstein 2022 on omega knowledge.
   - **Sequential analysis.** It ignores Wald's theory of how long to sample when deciding between two simple hypotheses, which is exactly this case. Its tests stop in finite expected time, which makes it the natural benchmark for "affordable inquiry".
   - **Fix.** Rewrite as an essay and engage this literature.

## Minor problems
- **Terms.** "Assumption F in A6" (§1) is used before F is defined (§2). "K1" (§5) is never defined. The Finding's "affordable inquiry" promises more than §3 delivers.
- **"Descent" is factivity.** Say so; it is not an extra assumption.
- **The success condition.** Inquiry into *whether* one knows^n can succeed through a negative answer, and its target is tense-indexed ("whether I know now"). The note, like C&H on p. 12, equates success with reaching K^n p at the stopping time. Say which norm is being tracked:
  - iterates of the Belief K-Norm require K^n p (pp. 9–11);
  - iterates of the Inquiry K-Norm concern knowing *whether* (p. 11).
- **A shorter proof of Proposition 1.** The event "S_k ≥ 0 for infinitely many k ∈ W_N" is exchangeable. By the CLT and reverse Fatou its probability is at least 1/2, so by the Hewitt–Savage 0–1 law it is 1. The CLT-plus-selection proof is correct but heavier than needed.
- **A missed illustration.** The model shows C&H's §4.1.2 risk point vividly, but the note never computes it. From RRBB, the next draw is black with probability 1/3, and that alone destroys H_1 (RRBBB has proportion 2/5).
- **"Transitions" (§4) is unclear.** Is it defeat, the definition of "coming to know" (p. 7), or something else?
- **§6.** The table is accurate but redundant once optional stopping is in hand; move it to a footnote.

## Claims that look false, misattributed or unverifiable
- **§1: "Thus assumption F in A6 must remain explicit."** Misleading. F follows from C&H fn 18's stated extension plus factivity (Major 4).
- **Finding and §5: "a precise separation … within the transparency framework"; "affordable inquiry".** Overstated. The separating model conflicts with the picture C&H attach to Transparency (p. 14, p. 16, fn 2). The natural model satisfying the note's conditions has finite expected times.
- **§4: the hierarchy "is compatible with factivity and ordinary modal closure at each individual history".** True for each history taken separately, but misleading. Once the histories are linked by known outcomes, history-determined knowledge cannot produce the stipulated profile (KK collapse, Major 1).
- **§3: "expected net payoff −∞".** This holds only under the true-composition law. Under credences that give RBB positive weight the class of almost-surely successful policies is empty, so the claim is trivial.

- **Calibration and candour.** The note:

- **A real, if small, exegetical observation.** C&H's displayed Transparency fixes one total length per level, counted from the first draw. Yet p. 15 uses it as "some number of additional draws" from wherever the agent now is. Seeing that the local use needs cofinal witness lengths is correct.
- **Clean proofs.**
  - The optional-stopping argument uses the walk's upward skip-freeness (steps up are exactly 1, so S_τ = 0) elegantly.
  - The selection argument with a uniform worst case (all-black past) is correct.
- **Distinctions worth keeping:**
  - availability vs belief vs recognized completion;

  - the "oracle" lower bound: recognition problems cannot push cost below τ.
- **A legitimate question.** The note asks what "you could come to know^n by further draws" delivers in terms of probability and cost. Answered inside a realized model, that question could sharpen C&H §§4.1.1–4.1.2.

## Proof check
1. **F gives cofinality.** The note asserts this without proof. It is correct. If W_N were bounded, then by descent every Transparency witness k_{N′} with N′ ≥ N would lie in a finite set, so some single length k* would witness every level. The all-red history of length k* would then supply every finite level, contradicting F. F itself follows from C&H fn 18 plus factivity (Major 4).
2. **Almost-sure recovery proposition.** Correct. Each step checks:
   - the threshold offset ⌈2(m+L)/3⌉ − 2L/3 = 2m/3 + O(1);
   - divided by √(2L/9) it tends to 0, so the CLT limit is 1/2;
   - the all-black past is the worst case uniformly over histories of length m;
   - the witness lengths are chosen deterministically, with conditional success probability ≥1/3 at each;
   - P(missing j in a row) ≤ (2/3)^j, so finitely many successes has probability 0;
   - intersect over the countably many N.

   A shorter route is via Hewitt–Savage (Minor problems). Note too that the bare *possibility* of recovery, which is all C&H's "could" requires, follows from cofinality with no probability at all: draw red up to a witness length k ≥ 3m.
3. **The hierarchy's conditions.** Descent ✓. T ✓, with W_N = {k ≥ N} for N ≥ 2. F ✓: a history of length m with proportion ≥2/3 has depth exactly m.
4. **Infinite-duration proposition.** Correct.
   - S = 3r − 2m has increments +1 (probability 2/3) and −2 (probability 1/3), mean 0.
   - S_0 = −2 at RRBB.
   - For m ≥ 2, H_2 holds iff S ≥ 0.
   - Upward steps are exactly 1, so S_τ = 0.
   - Increments are bounded, so if E[τ] were finite, optional stopping would give E[S_τ] = −2. Contradiction.
5. **Policy bound.** σ ≥ τ pathwise, so E[σ] = ∞; with linear cost and bounded reward the expected payoff is −∞. Correct under the true-composition law (see Major 3 for the credence reading).
6. **Table.**
   - **Checked by hand.** At T = 2: 4/9 and 2 ✓. At T = 10:
     - P(τ=2) = 4/9;
     - P(τ=5) = 2·(2/3)^4·(1/3) = 32/243;
     - P(τ=8) = 7·(2/3)^6·(1/3)^2 = 448/6561, from the 7 admissible placements of the two black draws.

     These sum to 0.644414, and E[min(τ,10)] = 5.649444. Both match the table.
   - **Large-T rows.** They fit the expected T^{-1/2} tail. The formula P(τ>T) ≈ 2/√(πT) gives 0.11284, 0.035682 and 0.011284, against the table's 0.11282, 0.035682 and 0.011284.
7. **§4 chain frames.** Correct but trivial. They do not link histories; see the KK-collapse lemma (Major 1).
8. **My counter-model** (the log-likelihood-ratio cutoff model in the single most serious problem). It satisfies every condition the note uses. By Wald's identity (overshoot ≤ log 2, drift (1/3)·log 2), E[τ_n] ≤ 3(θ+(n−1)δ−ℓ(h))/log 2 + 3 < ∞. So the infinite mean is not robust even within natural models of the note's own conditions.

## Top five changes (for a full 6,000-word version)
1. **Replace the level-function with an explicit dynamic model of the bag case.**
   - Worlds: composition × draw sequence × a hidden parameter, with the accessibility relation stated.
   - Verify Anti-Skepticism, Transparency, defeat, known outcomes, fn 2 and the p. 14 strength-of-evidence picture.
   - Locate the source of higher-order ignorance in C&H's fn 18 mechanism.
   - Check every verdict in the model.
2. **Characterize exactly when the separation holds, then argue where C&H fall.** State a theorem:
   - the expected time to a fixed level is finite when its evidential threshold has positive drift under the truth (likelihood-ratio thresholds, or proportion thresholds strictly below 2/3);
   - it is infinite at the zero-drift boundary (proportion thresholds of 2/3 − O(1/m)).

   Then argue which side C&H's epistemology lands on. If it lands on the finite side, as I expect, the honest thesis is the converse of the present one: every fixed level is affordable and cost grows linearly in level, which vindicates the structure of C&H §4.1.1.
3. **Aim at a premise someone holds.** Either of these would be a result C&H's readers would update on:
   - Quantify C&H's §4.1.2 risk trade-off: the gambler's-ruin probability of losing level k before reaching level n, and the cost-optimal target level, in the realized model. Use Wald's sequential analysis.
   - Recast Ex Ante Dogmatism in value-of-evidence terms (Good 1967; Ahmed & Salow on Good's theorem without partitional evidence), and show whether C&H's reply survives.
4. **Fix the evaluative probability and the success condition.**
   - Choose between the objective law, the agent's credence and knowledge-conditioned probabilities. Note that the credence reading makes the result trivial, and engage Hong if K1 is used.
   - Distinguish inquiry into *whether* one knows^n (which a negative answer can settle, and which is tense-indexed) from attaining K^n p.
   - Tie each to the matching C&H norm: iterates of the Belief K-Norm or of the Inquiry K-Norm (pp. 9–11).
5. **Rewrite as an essay.**
   - State the thesis and the exact increment over C&H in the first paragraph.
   - Delete the pipeline material.
   - Derive F from fn 18 rather than assuming it, and present Proposition 1 as shoring up C&H's p. 12 supposition.
   - Engage the iterated-knowledge literature: Williamson 2000, Das & Salow 2018, Goodman & Salow 2018, Bacon 2014, Dorr–Goodman–Hawthorne 2014, Goldstein 2022.
   - Engage the checking literature: Friedman 2019a, Woodard (forthcoming), Beddor (forthcoming).
   - Cite Wald on sequential sampling, and move the table to a footnote.
