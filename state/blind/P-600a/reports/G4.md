# Referee report — G4 Advocate (defender of Flattening and sequence semantics) — P-600a

## Thesis and argument as I understand them (≤120 words)

Nested counterfactuals have a "scenario-relative" reading. On it, the inner supposition is evaluated by the dependencies of the scenario the outer supposition selects, and Flattening fails. The vehicle is a bare rule-form instance, (1) (A∨B)>(A>B) vs (2) A>B, in two stories (Reunion, Avoidance) that share their first-degree counterfactuals about who came. The author judges (2) false in both and (1) true only in Reunion. A control shows that no Boolean construal of the inner antecedent predicts this contrast. Buttons and Theorem 1 rest the inner verdict on Locality applied at the selected scenario. §6 explains why Dorr and Mandelkern's test pairs pass. §7 extends their Import-Export reasoning to Flattening. The essay concedes that the decisive judgments are untested.

## The single most serious problem

**Location:** §3, premise (P2) in its Reunion half, and §5.1.

**The problem.** The whole case rests on one judgment: that (1) is true in the Reunion. The essay supports that judgment only circularly. It also gives nothing that distinguishes its "semantic" explanation of the judgment from the defender's contextual one.

(a) **The support is circular.** The only argument for (P2) runs as follows. A C2.F proponent must accept the CEM dual, "If at least one of them had come, then if Ann had come, Bob would not have been there". This is supposedly absurd, because "that says that … Ann's arrival from Australia would have kept him away". But that gloss reads the dual on the scenario-relative reading, which is the very reading in dispute. On the case-split reading the dual is natural and true: "had at least one come: if it had been Ann, Bob wouldn't have been there; his sitter cancelled".

(b) **An equally precise rival fits all the data.** Take a flat conditional whose base world can be shifted locally to the selected scenario. It predicts:
- (1) true in the Reunion;
- (1) false in Avoidance;
- the Contraction row patterning with (2), because centering survives any re-centring;
- the "But if Ann had come…" discourse being coherent.

The essay's one diagnostic only tests a *persistent* shift, which no one proposes. Its 2×2 design cannot separate the two hypotheses, because both predict the interaction.

**Why it matters.** What is left is a conditional: *if* there is a stable scenario-relative reading, Flattening fails on it. Given D&M's Theorem 4.3, that adds little to the bare fact that a non-flat ordering invalidates Flattening. D&M already give the schematic reason that world-relative orderings break Flattening (p. 19, stated for similarity, though the reason is general). So increment (i), "a counterexample of a different kind", shrinks to "a reading on which Flattening fails".

## Major problems

1. **§3, the reading of (1).**
   - *Problem.* The natural reading of an underspecified outer antecedent followed by an inner antecedent that names one disjunct is case-split (Kratzer-style restriction). On that reading (1) ≡ (2).
   - *Telling sign.* The essay's own support for truth in the Reunion ("Ann's turning up *as well* would have changed nothing for him") quietly adds an additive particle. The control eliminates additivity only in its Boolean A∧B form. It does not eliminate a presuppositional "would *still*" construal, which delivers exactly the essay's pattern.
   - *Why it matters.* This is the only premise doing any work.
   - *Fix.* Test (1) against overt variants: "…would still have been there", "if it had been Ann instead", and a plain-disjunction version, "If Ann or Bob had come…". Show that the "true" verdict survives without a covert particle. Otherwise weaken (P2) to "(1) has a true reading" and accept the consequences.

2. **§3, "at least one of them" is an indefinite.**
   - *Problem.* Indefinites are the very category behind D&M's re-indexing pairs (7)–(8) (pp. 20–21). An indefinite outer antecedent introduces a scenario that the inner clause can be anchored to. That is the classic route to modal subordination (Roberts 1989, which the essay cites).
   - *The essay's reply fails.* It says the absence of overt anaphora makes re-indexing "less immediate". But the re-indexed item would be the conditional's own covert ordering parameter, which has no overt marker by its nature.
   - *Fix.* Top five changes, item 1, removes the indefinite.

3. **§4 and §5.2, Buttons and Locality.**
   - *Problem.* The "cost" argument presupposes that the conditional facts at a reached index supervene on that index's categorical facts and laws. Sequence semantics denies exactly this. That denial is built into van Fraassen's split between protoworlds and sequences, and into the "non-factual" gloss D&M report at p. 13 (Khoo 2022).
   - *"Nothing in the principle singles out the actual world" misdescribes the view.* On it, Locality constrains how the *head* of a sequence selects antecedent worlds. Tails do not select afresh; they inherit.
   - *"That asserts a counterfactual connection between spacelike-separated events" equivocates* between a conditional being true at a tail and influence within the tail-head's world.
   - *The metaphysics is not idle here.* The essay calls it idle; that is true for the rule-form instance but not for this step.
   - *Fix.* Argue independently that the truth of nested counterfactuals supervenes on the facts and laws of the reached scenario, for example by defending a Morgenbesser-style principle ("hold fixed the evaluation world's causally independent facts") for supposed worlds. Otherwise drop the cost rhetoric.

4. **§5.2, the SEM paragraph, and the Avoidance control.**
   - *Problem.* Under standard do-semantics the essay's own Avoidance verdict fails. do(B=1) clamps Bob. The later do(A=1) leaves the guest list untouched, because the list is not a descendant of A. So (1) comes out *true* in Avoidance too, contradicting (P2).
   - *What the essay needs.* Its verdicts require both antecedents to be realized upstream: the babysitter for Bob, and Ann's decision (and hence the list) for Ann. That is a backtracking-flavoured policy. A universal treatment of the disjunctive antecedent (the essay's own "universal reading") makes (1) false in both stories.
   - *Consequence.* "The verdict does not depend on how the model was reached" misses the point. The verdict depends on how each antecedent is *realized*.
   - *Fix.* State the realization policy and defend it. Also acknowledge that sticky interventions make SEM semantics non-flat anyway, so SEMs are not independent support for *this* contrast.

5. **§7, the unity of "if".**
   - *Problem.* D&M's case against Import-Export (IE) has two legs: the Dale/Gibbard collapse (pp. 21–22) and compelling subjunctive counterexamples (Etlin). The parity argument uses only the second.
   - *Without a collapse, the direction of the mood explanation is open for Flattening.* One option: the logic is non-flat and the indicative appeal comes from mood. The other: the logic is flat and the subjunctive failures come from a re-centring that only counterfactual selection allows. The essay's own §7 explains why indicative supposition does not select.
   - *Omission.* The essay also ignores Flattening's positive role reported at p. 22: an error theory of IE's apparent validity relies on it (Mandelkern 2024).
   - *Fix.* Address both legs, and that role.

6. **Increment (ii) and §5.1, the "explanatory burden".**
   - *Problem.* The base-world-shift account discharges the stated burden in one sentence (see R3 below).
   - *What is missing.* The essay never asks the question that actually hurts the defender: if re-centring is that easy, where does the flat reading ever surface?
   - *Fix.* Top five changes, item 2.

7. **§4, Theorem 1.**
   - *Problem.* It is a three-line corollary of the rule forms plus CEM. It does usefully remove dependence on the asymmetric tie-break. But using it against Flattening needs the same disputed Locality-at-the-reached-scenario judgment.
   - The Z3 verification of a result with a three-line proof is padding.
   - *Fix.* Compress it to a remark.

## Minor problems

- §1: the van Fraassen truth clause omits the vacuous case.
- §2: (Flat) is said to be stated by D&M "themselves for metric spaces (24)". What they actually say is that no ordinary metric space has the property.
- §1: the reconstruction of D&M's IE strand omits two things: the non-collapse point (ω-sequence models, p. 22) and the Mandelkern 2024 role. The account of their re-indexing reply also drops their hedge: "questions about the representation of context-sensitivity that are beyond our scope" (p. 21).
- §5.2: "so (1) is false there" should say that the *inner* conditional is false at the tail.
- §3: "then" in (1) invites a temporal construal (Bob arrives, then Ann). That construal also makes (1) true in the Reunion. Test the sentence without "then".
- §3: the falsity of (2) depends on holding fixed an independent, plausibly chancy outcome (the cancellation). That is Morgenbesser-type reasoning, but the essay flags chance only for Buttons.
- "The dependencies of a scenario" is never defined. D&M reject similarity (p. 7), so the essay must say which ordering notion it means.
- The four-world models are invoked but never displayed, yet Observation 2 depends on "the frame of the Reunion model". Print the order at every world.
- §6: D&M's cautious-cousin analogy is about collapse into Materialism, and on that point it still holds. The essay's contrast between a single ranking and iterated supposition is correct, but it answers a different question.
- "Flattening predicts no interaction" is true only under a fixed interpretation.
- The "checked exhaustively" asides attached to results with two-line proofs are padding.

## What is genuinely good (be specific: revisions must preserve this)

- **The matched control.** It holds the who-came structure fixed and varies only the dependency. That eliminates every Boolean strengthening of the inner antecedent (exclusive, additive-as-conjunction, universal) as the source of the contrast. This is a real methodological advance on one-off spot checks.
- **§6's conditions (a)–(c) and the interventionist observation.** The observation is that intervening on a set and then on a superset equals intervening on the superset directly. Together these show why D&M's test pairs *could not* have revealed a failure. They also show that the redundancy datum is predicted just as well by contextual equivalence. As advocate I must concede this: the test pairs do not discriminate.
- **It makes explicit what flatness demands.** Nested suppositions must keep the *base* world's ranking, not the selected scenario's: any two worlds that both reach v must agree on the pairs ranked after v. D&M never state this commitment, and defenders must now own it.
- **Observation 1 frames the evidence correctly.** Data about unnested conditionals are neutral.
- **Theorem 1 removes the dependence on the tie-break.**
- **Scholarship and calibration.** The source is used accurately with page references, and the conclusion is honestly calibrated.

## Defender's replies

**The view at its strongest.**
- Flattening is a law of "if" under a fixed interpretation, as Contraction is (D&M pp. 20–21).
- The semantic picture is a single master order, with each index's order obtained by deleting its predecessors (Thm 4.5, p. 27).
- Sequence semantics adds that conditional facts at an index need not supervene on its categorical facts (p. 13).
- D&M's conditions on a test:
  - the felt difference does not come from re-indexing (pp. 20–21);
  - it is not audible only "by doggedly holding in mind" a theory (pp. 19–20);
  - the interpretation is held fixed "throughout" (p. 36).

**Does the counterexample meet those conditions?**
- It is a valid formal instance: yes.
- It holds a single interpretation fixed: not shown, as the essay concedes.
- It is free of re-indexing: doubtful, given the indefinite and the covert ordering parameter.
- It is audible without theory: doubtful. The Reunion verdict requires resolving the outer supposition to Bob and then re-centring.

**R1. The case-split reading is the default, and on it (1) ≡ (2).**
- *Succeeds* against (P2) as stated.
- Does *not* show that no scenario-relative reading exists.

**R2. The CEM dual is true on the case-split reading.**
- *Succeeds.* The main support offered for (P2) is circular.

**R3. Base-world shift.**
- *The account.* Context fixes a base world, and each index's order is the base world's master order, truncated. The frame is linear, hence flat (p. 27). An outer counterfactual supposition may locally promote the selected scenario to be the base world.
- *Textual support in D&M.* This is a re-indexing of a covert parameter, the kind of context-sensitivity they call "delicate" (p. 21). For conditionals embedded in antecedents, they themselves float interpretation "differently from the conditional in which they are embedded" (p. 48, cf. Kaufmann 2023); the move extends naturally to consequents. They also allow their own Sequentiality counterexample to be resisted by an interpretive shift within the discourse (p. 36).
- *Predictions:*
  - Reunion (1) is true on the shifted reading;
  - Avoidance (1) is false, because the selected scenario's dependencies dislodge Bob;
  - the Contraction row equals (2), because centering survives;
  - the "But…" discourse is coherent, because the shift is local.
- *Succeeds* against §5.1's claim that the contextualist owes an explanation. Does *not* show that the shift account is better.
- *Forced concession:* D&M's Contraction-parity test (p. 21) cannot certify this re-indexing as harmless, because Contraction is immune to it.

**R4. Locality constrains heads only.**
- *Succeeds* against the "cost" argument.
- *Forced concession:* what is counterfactually true at an index depends on the route by which it is reached. The essay makes that unusual commitment vivid.

**R5. SEM realization.**
- *Partly succeeds:* it removes SEM support for the specific Avoidance contrast.
- *Forced concession:* sticky nested interventions are non-flat once disjunctive antecedents are realized selectionally. The defender must therefore deny that natural-language nested counterfactuals are nested interventions.

**R6. Parity.**
- *Mostly succeeds.* There is no collapse leg, so the direction of the mood story is not forced. The essay also does not address the role of the IE error theory.

**R7. Probability.**
- Under restricted forms of Stalnaker's Thesis, the two sides of a Flattening instance receive equal probability (p. 44 n. 36).
- The essay concedes the indicative case, so this remains Flattening's positive evidence there. It is neutral for subjunctives.

**The best repair, and whether the essay survives it.** The repair is context-anchored Flattening (R3).
- The essay's formal results and the §6 diagnosis survive it.
- Its anti-Flattening conclusion and §7 do not.

**The essay's best unused weapon.** The repair carries a price the essay never charges. Consider the triad:
- (A∨B)>B: "had at least one come, Bob would have been there";
- B>(A>B): "If Bob had come, then if Ann had come, Bob would have been there";
- ¬(A>B).

The triad is C2.F-inconsistent. Normality gives (A∨B)>B, Reciprocity then gives (A∨B)>(A>B), and the rule form gives A>B. So, given the essay's first-degree verdicts, every flat model makes the middle sentence false in the Reunion. Yet it is very hard to hear as false.

The middle sentence has no case-split reading. An Import-Export construal would make it trivially true in Avoidance as well, so the same control rules that out. If R3 has to be invoked even here, re-centring becomes near-obligatory after any specific counterfactual supposition. The flat reading would then surface only where (a)–(c) fail, or where a case-split construal is available. In both places a non-flat semantics with a restrictor reading predicts the same verdicts.

By D&M's own parsimony principle (p. 36), and their concession that the unconstrained semantics has "more prima facie philosophical plausibility" (pp. 27–28), Flattening would then have no positive support for counterfactuals.

**My verdict as advocate.** The essay *as written* forces three concessions:
- the test pairs do not discriminate;
- actuality-anchoring must be made explicit;
- SEM semantics is non-flat.

It does not force anyone to abandon Flattening.

## Top five changes

1. **Lead with the triad.** Open with the triad {(A∨B)>B, B>(A>B), ¬(A>B)} and its three-line C2.F derivation. Run the Reunion/Avoidance control on "If Bob had come, then if Ann had come, Bob would have been there". This one change removes R1, the indefinite/anaphora worry and the "then" confound. Keep (1) as a secondary bare instance.

2. **Replace the §5.1 "burden" paragraph.**
   - State the base-world-shift rival formally and concede that it fits all current data.
   - Then press the idleness dilemma, using D&M's parsimony principle (p. 36) and their concession at pp. 27–28.
   - Redesign the study so that it separates the hypotheses: overt "instead" and "still", "actually"-anaphora, ellipsis, probability ratings.
   - Demote the persistent-shift test to a footnote.

3. **Repair §4 and §5.2.**
   - Either argue for supervenience independently, or delete "nothing … singles out the actual world" and "spacelike connection".
   - Specify the upstream realization policy for both antecedents.
   - Concede that do(B=1) followed by do(A=1) makes (1) true in Avoidance, and cite Briggs 2012.
   - Recast SEMs as showing that the standard formal tool for nested counterfactuals is non-flat.

4. **Rewrite §7.**
   - Address the collapse leg and the Mandelkern 2024 role (p. 22).
   - Argue why the mood story must run your way; for instance, on your own §7 analysis the indicative evidence does not discriminate.
   - Cut increments (ii) and (iv) back to what is actually shown.

5. **Cut, and display.**
   - Shrink Theorem 1 to a remark: keep the tie-break point and drop Z3.
   - Drop the "checked exhaustively" asides.
   - Shorten the paragraph on imaging and Darwiche–Pearl, crediting DP's conditional-belief form and Katsuno–Mendelzon.
