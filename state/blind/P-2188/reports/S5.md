# Referee report — S5 (Philosopher of language / formal semanticist) — P-2188

## Thesis and argument as I understand them (≤120 words)

Flattening (P>(Q>R) ↔ Q>R whenever Q entails P) is what ordinal sequence semantics adds to C2 (Dorr & Mandelkern). The essay sets it against "locality": changing one of two independent controls preserves the other's setting, both at the actual world and at worlds reached by supposition. On the four-world square, locality fixes f(00,A)=10, f(00,B)=01, f(01,A)=11 and f(10,B)=11. Whichever single-control world realizes A∨B, nested selection diverges from direct selection, so Flattening fails. Its two directions both fail in an explicit frame that keeps 4, H, Contraction and conjunctive-setting Flattening. Because setting-overwrites compose, conjunctive test pairs (D&M's evidence) cannot detect the failure. A defender must deny locality at embedded scenarios ("route retention") or treat local readings as a context shift.

## The single most serious problem

**Location:** §4 (the (D)/(N) data). The problem carries into §5's burden-shift and the conditional conclusion of §8.

The formal incompatibility is, as the essay itself says, short. It also sits inside ground Dorr and Mandelkern have already mapped: their Figure 1 countermodel (p. 17), the metric counterpart of (1) (p. 24), and the line example (p. 19), which already has a *disjunctive* outer antecedent ("not strictly between 8–11", realized at 11). All the philosophical weight therefore rests on one linguistic premise: that the bare nested sentence (N) has a reading on which it is true while (D) is false.

The essay offers only two things for this premise: the stipulation (L), and the author's own report of how (N) sounds. It concedes that speakers may not hear (N) this way and that "too" helps. Its own opening gloss also leans on an additive: "If A had been on in that scenario *as well*".

Two standard analyses predict the verdict Flattening needs, and neither is discussed:
- **(i) Case-specification / restrictor construal.** Under a disjunctive outer antecedent, a right-nested if-clause naturally picks out one case of it. "If at least one had been on, then if A had been on…" is heard as "…if [the one that was on] had been A…", which is (A∨B)∧A, i.e. A.
- **(ii) Alternative-sensitive treatments.** On SDA-validating treatments of disjunctive antecedents, the outer antecedent quantifies over both realizations, and the 10-realization falsifies A>B. So (N) is false.

Beyond these, suppose the local reading needs "too/as well". A defender will then say that the additive presupposition is accommodated into the inner antecedent, giving A∧B. The Flattening instance is then (A∨B)>((A∧B)>B) ↔ (A∧B)>B, and both sides are true.

The burden-shift in §4 ("A claim that Flattening belongs to the logic of counterfactuals needs a reason to exclude that reading") gets the dialectic backwards. An interpretation that is coherent in the model theory is cheap: D&M concede that non-flat order functions exist and are coherent. Their actual question (§4.1, pp. 19–20) is whether *ordinary judgments about bare instances* diverge. The essay does not answer it. Its contribution therefore does not go beyond D&M's own acknowledgment that a closeness-style interpretation would falsify Flattening.

## Major problems

1. **§§4–5: The strongest rival readings are not addressed, and the main one is misdescribed.**
   - **Problem.** The essay calls the rival reading "exclusive" ("A *rather than B*"). But on the case/restrictor reading the inner antecedent is just A, not A∧¬B. It selects 10 only because direct A-selection from 00 selects 10. So the essay's "control" with an explicit A∧¬B antecedent does not test the rival at all. Two further rivals go unmentioned:
     - alternative-sensitive semantics for disjunctive antecedents, under which (N) comes out false;
     - accommodation of the additive's presupposition into the inner antecedent.
   - **Why it matters.** These are the first analyses a semanticist reaches for. All of them predict the Flattening verdicts. Until they are excluded, the case is not a counterexample to any claim D&M make.
   - **Fix.**
     - Build minimal-pair paradigms. Inner antecedent: bare / *too* / *instead* / "it had been A" / explicit "A and B". Outer antecedent: "at least one of A and B" / "one of the switches" / a specific realization. Also vary mood and causal structure (independent vs interlocked).
     - Show that the local reading survives with no additive particle.
     - Failing that, narrow the thesis to additive-marked sentences, and argue that these still instantiate the schema with inner antecedent A.

2. **§§3, 7: The formal result is stated for the wrong class and is weaker than it could be.**
   - **Problem.** The square identifies indices with categorical profiles. That invites the richer-indices reply, which §7 then concedes. The restriction is not needed. Here is the general claim:
     - Take any order model, sequence models included.
     - Suppose that at every index the A-selection preserves that index's b-value and the B-selection preserves its a-value.
     - Then Flattening fails at every index where both controls are off and both A- and B-worlds are accessible.

     The proof is the essay's own, word for word. Accessibility from the selected index, transitivity and universality are not needed, because if A is inaccessible from 01 the nested conditional is vacuously true and the failure persists.

     *Corollary.* In every sequence model, the first (A∨B)-tail of any both-off index violates locality. The result is also not an artefact of Stalnaker uniqueness. With Lewis-style ties (01 and 10 equally close), and on my reconstruction of Briggs-style interventionist semantics (which quantifies over the verifiers of A∨B), the exportation direction still fails: A>¬B is true at 00, but (A∨B)>(A>¬B) is false because of the 01-realization.
   - **Why it matters.** This turns §7's informal "price" into a theorem aimed at the sequence theorist's actual models. It also shows that conditional excluded middle (CEM) is not doing the work.
   - **Fix.** State and prove the general proposition and its corollary. This needs a few hundred words.

3. **§1, §6: The increment over D&M is under-specified and, in places, overstated.**
   - **Problem.**
     - The essay suggests that D&M's similarity case rests on "a numerical similarity metric" while its own does not. Yet the explicit frame in §3 is *exactly* the order induced by a weighted Hamming distance with w_A > w_B. "Ease of change" is the weight. So the frame falls under D&M's remark that "no ordinary metric space" satisfies the selection condition (p. 24).
     - §6 implies that D&M's support for Flattening is conjunctive. But D&M did test a disjunctive-outer case (the line, p. 19) and reported no clear divergence. Their (5) (France / south of France) is not obviously a conjunctive-settings case either.
   - **What is genuinely new.** Two things:
     - (a) Single-control locality at three indices is enough to force the failure, with no comparison across controls and whatever realization is chosen.
     - (b) The mechanism is different: an *independent co-variable's* setting persists through the inner change, rather than a single variable being pulled by a metric.

     The essay never states either as its increment, and never explains why its case escapes D&M's complaint about the line case: that divergence is detectable only "by doggedly holding in mind" a theory (pp. 19–20).
   - **Fix.** Add an explicit comparison with (1a)/(1b).

4. **Literature: the work most relevant to the essay's motivation is missing.**
   - **Problem.** The best argument for locality at selected worlds is that interventionist semantics composes nested interventions cumulatively: do(B=1) followed by do(A=1) is do(A=1, B=1). Citing this would turn "a coherent reading" into "the verdict of the leading formal theory of causal counterfactuals". The essay instead disclaims any link to how structural-equation models handle disjunctive antecedents and cites only Halpern 2000. Also missing:
     - the extensive literature on disjunctive antecedents (SDA; asymmetric cases of the McKay–van Inwagen type, which actually support the essay's unique-realization assumption);
     - the two-switch experimental literature;
     - the restrictor view;
     - the update/revision distinction. The essay's "inherit vs retain the route" choice *is* that distinction, and (1) is the counterfactual-logic analogue of Darwiche–Pearl's postulate C1.
   - **Fix.** See "Prior work" below. Rebuild §6 around these.

5. **§8: The conclusion is conditional and misses its strongest payoff.**
   - **Problem.** Suppose the case works for subjunctives, while indicative variants ("If at least one is on, then if A is on, B is on") get the case/restrictor reading and obey Flattening. Then Flattening behaves like Import-Export: plausible for indicatives, failing for subjunctives. That would undercut D&M's argument (p. 22) that Flattening, unlike IE, is backed by the desideratum of a unified 'if'.
   - **Fix.** Test the indicative versions, draw this implication explicitly, and calibrate the conclusion to it. That conclusion would be non-trivial; "if local readings are legitimate, Flattening fails" is close to trivial.

## Minor problems

- **Contraction is a theorem of C2** (D&M p. 21) and holds on every order frame. "The square validates Contraction for every proposition" and the abstract's "preserves Contraction" therefore describe no special feature of the construction. Recast this as a *linguistic* control: show that "If A had been on, then if A had been on, B would have been on" patterns with (D) under the same reading that makes (N) true.
- **(1) needs its conditions spelled out.** As stated ("for nonempty, accessible antecedents") it amounts to Cautious Importation, i.e. semi-flatness (Lemma 4.2), not flatness. The "appropriate convention for vacuous cases" carries two separate requirements:
  - X must stay accessible from f(w,Y) (Crashing Cautious Importation, fn 24);
  - transitivity, i.e. axiom 4 (Crashing Cautious Exportation, p. 18).

  Axiom 4 is D&M's chief worry, so say this explicitly. It also strengthens the point that the square satisfies 4.
- **§5 overreads D&M's cautious analogy.** D&M call Flattening a "plausible cautious cousin" of IE (p. 23); they do not claim the analogy makes the two kinds of constraint "equivalent". The intra-order vs inter-order point is correct; present it as showing that the analogy is weaker than it looks.
- **§1:** the opening gloss uses "as well", the very additive the essay later says the argument does not rely on.
- **§7, last paragraph** ("Nor does the result establish that Flattening is valid for indicatives") is confusing. Say what the account predicts for indicatives (see Major 5).
- **The importation-direction failure depends on a unique realization of A∨B** (CEM), which the essay secures with "B is easier". Note this. With symmetric controls, Stalnaker's supervaluation would leave (N) indeterminate. Cite asymmetric disjunctive antecedents as support.
- **Undefined terms.** "Complete two-control system" (all four profiles present and mutually accessible?) and "reading" (an LF? a value of a context parameter? a choice of order function?) should be defined. The §5 contrast between "change of context" and "evaluation at a different world" is really a metasemantic question: what fixes <x at an index reached by supposition? Framed that way it would be sharper.
- **(L) is stated only for turning a control on.** This is fine for the square, but the general statement should cover both directions.
- **Length.** About 2,800 of 6,000 words. The essay is underdeveloped exactly where its argument needs support.

## Proof check

- **(1) and flatness.** For all w and X ⊆ Y, Flattening is valid on a frame iff:
  - (a) if X∩R(w) ≠ ∅, then X∩R(f(w,Y)) ≠ ∅ and f(f(w,Y),X) = f(w,X); and
  - (b) if X∩R(w) = ∅ ≠ Y∩R(w), then X∩R(f(w,Y)) = ∅.

  Both are derived by choosing the consequent r freely: r = ∅, or r = the complement of {f(w,X)}. (a) is Cautious Importation, i.e. semi-flatness (Lemma 4.2). (b) holding for all X ⊆ Y is equivalent to transitivity: take Y = {y,z} and X = {z} with y ∈ R(w) and z ∈ R(y)\R(w). So (1) together with the stated conventions is equivalent to flatness, and the essay's claim holds. But the "convention" includes axiom 4 and should be stated.
- **Proposition 1. Valid.** It uses (L) only at 00 (for both A and B), at 01 (for A) and at 10 (for B). The step "f(00,A∨B) = min(f(00,A), f(00,B))" is right: every A∨B-world is at or after one of the two. It does not need 11 ∈ R(00). As shown under Major 2, it generalizes to arbitrary order models with locality at every index, and its exportation half survives ties and verifier-quantification.
- **Explicit frame.** Checked row by row.
  - All rows are centered, and all eight (L) values hold.
  - At 00: A>B is false (selection gives 10); (A∨B)>(A>B) is true (01, then 11); A>¬B is true; (A∨B)>(A>¬B) is false.
  - Both directions fail, and these are genuine rule-form instances, since a ⊢ a∨b.
  - The frame is exactly the order induced by d = w_A·|Δa| + w_B·|Δb| with w_A > w_B > 0 (each row is the ranking 0, w_B, w_A, w_A+w_B). It is therefore a Lewis-similarity frame (see Major 3).
- **Conjunctive settings in the explicit frame.** For every partial assignment over {A, B}, at all four worlds, selection equals the overwrite map. For example, ¬A: 10→00, 11→01. ¬B: 01→00, 11→10. Full assignments select their singleton. So Proposition 2 applies, and "Flattening for compatible conjunctive settings" holds there. Proposition 2 itself (T_b∘T_a = T_b when b extends a) is valid and is the composition property of interventions.
- **Exclusive-antecedent control.** {10} is selected from 00 and from 01, so there is no divergence. Correct, but it is not a test of the case-reading rival (Major 1).
- **Interlock control.** Correct as stated. If 11 is excluded, f(01,A) = 10 and (N) is false. How the interlocked system orders its remaining worlds is left open.
- **Modal claims.** With universal accessibility the defined □ is S5, so 4 and H hold. Correct.
- **Contraction, Cautious Monotonicity, Cautious Transitivity.** Correct, but they hold on *every* order frame (C2 theorems), so they say nothing specific about the square.
- **§7 claims about sequences.** Correct.

No stated result fails, so no countermodel is needed. The issues are triviality and scope, not error.
