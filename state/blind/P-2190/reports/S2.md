# Referee report — S2 (Epistemic logician / knowledge-first epistemologist) — P-2190

## Thesis and argument as I understand them (≤120 words)

On Goodman and Salow's multidimensional structures (finite products of total normality preorders), K-normality's accessibility set B ∪ U(w) projects exactly onto the single-source K-sets (Prop. 1), so all its surplus knowledge is joint. It is non-rectangular exactly when one coordinate lies outside its protected set and another lies strictly above the bottom of its own (Prop. 2). For example, at (0,2) the agent knows "if the thermometer is bizarre, the scale is ordinary". The essay argues that this cross-source knowledge is unlicensed when no joint constraint is modeled. It proposes the rectangularization ΠK, which satisfies the framework, is transitive, and is the least such rectangle (Prop. 3). It shows that KM factorizes as ∏I_i (Prop. 4), and concludes that margins and cross-source correlation are separate disputes.

## Verdict
Prize: not finalist
Journal recommendation: reject

Every formal result checks out, and Props. 2 and 4 are, as far as I know, new. But the essay misdescribes its own choice point, and its argument for the repair begs the question. A reframed short note built on Prop. 4 and Lemma M (below) could be publishable.

## The single most serious problem

**Where:** §3 (the gloss after Prop. 2), §4 (the "explanatory demand"), §§6–7 and the abstract (the "separation" of two disputes).

**What goes wrong.** The essay never notices that the world K excludes in its central case is a *margin world*.
- At actuality (0,2): (0,2) ⪰ (1,2); (1,2) is not ⪰ (0,2); and ¬((0,2) ≫ (1,2)), because neither 0≫1 nor 2≫2 holds.
- So (1,2) is "insufficiently less normal" than actuality, and MARGINS (G&S p. 106) requires it to be accessible.
- K's "correlational knowledge" in the showcase example is therefore K's familiar cliff-edge knowledge. It shows up along coordinate 1, within the slice where coordinate 2 takes its actual value.

This generalizes (Lemma M, in the Proof check). For any v in R_ΠK(w) \ R_K(w), the meet u = w∧v is insufficiently less normal than w, and v ⪰ u. So MARGINS plus Convexity force v back in. On the essay's structures, therefore:

R_K ⊆ R_ΠK ⊆ every framework completion satisfying MARGINS (in particular R_KM) ⊆ R_I.

The essay's own meet formula says the same thing. R_ΠK(w) is the upward closure of {w∧b : b ∈ B}, and each w∧b is either equivalent to w or a margin world of w. In other words, ΠK is K plus margins in exactly the directions of the protected set.

**Consequences.**
- **(a) The two disputes are nested, not separate.** Every MARGINS theorist already rejects all of K's surplus joint knowledge. The cross-source question arises only for those who reject MARGINS.
- **(b) §4's key premise is false.** The premise is that "the global disjunction in K-normality generates [the exclusion] without representing any additional relation in the setup". In fact the exclusion is generated by two relations the multidimensional model supplies: (0,2) ⪰ (1,2) and (1,0) ≫ (1,2). K's explanation of it is its standard explanation of cliff-edge knowledge: the world is unprotected and lies below actuality. The demand for a feature that distinguishes "the excluded combination from its individually admissible components" assumes that admissibility is assessed coordinate by coordinate. That is exactly what K denies and what the essay has to establish.
- **(c) The thesis that K's surplus is an unexplained cost is not established.** §7 in effect concedes a standoff: "Neither default establishes its own constraints".

**Why it matters.** This is the essay's only argument that ΠK beats K, and it frames the whole contribution.

## Major problems

1. **§§4 and 7: rectangularity is given no independent support, and it conflicts with joint knowledge G&S endorse.**
   - **(i) Preface-type knowledge.** Knowing that not all of many independent claims hold (G&S pp. 122–123; Carter & Goldstein's second-order dimension, G&S n. 30) violates rectangularity *because of* independence (aggregate improbability), not because of a modeled joint constraint. §7 calls this a "cost" but offers no criterion separating such cases from K's.
   - **(ii) G&S's own two-scale model.** It treats the scales' errors as probabilistically independent (p. 129, second assumption). Yet its sum-of-squares normality licenses non-rectangular knowledge about the two errors: the readings will not differ by more than √2c (p. 126). And n. 37 (p. 129) rejects the product treatment outright.
   - So within G&S, independent error sources do not by themselves favour rectangular knowledge.
   - **Fix: a source-internal argument is available.**
     - Rectangularity is equivalent to a cross-source disjunction property: K(p∨q) → Kp ∨ Kq, for p and q about disjoint coordinates (proof below).
     - Under G&S's BELIEF (pp. 112–113), doxastic accessibility is B = {0,1}². So at (0,2) the agent justifiably but falsely believes that the thermometer is not bizarre.
     - The K-known disjunction "thermometer not bizarre ∨ scale ordinary" therefore joins a justified false belief to a true but unknown proposition about a *different* subject matter. That is Gettier's Ford-or-Barcelona form, which G&S say is "typically not known … for the same reason as in classic Gettier cases" (p. 114). They credit Convexity with blocking such knowledge.
     - On the product branch, R_K(0,2) is upward closed, so Convexity does not block it. ΠK never yields it.
     - Preface knowledge differs: the disjunction is believed while each disjunct is disbelieved, so it is not inherited from a false believed disjunct.

2. **§4, the "disclosure" diagnostic: it does not show what the essay claims.**
   - It is a failure of NO DEFEAT. G&S already show that K violates NO DEFEAT in single-order models (Bjorn's two scales, p. 127; Flipping for Heads, p. 131), and that KM does so under incomparability (n. 35, p. 128). ΠK inherits every single-factor failure.
   - The pattern (know p ⊃ q; discover p; know neither q nor p ⊃ q) is what a high-probability requirement predicts when p and q are *independent* and p is improbable. Take G&S's figure-5 parameters (p. 122: ratios of 10:1, independent dimensions, threshold .95). Then P(T bizarre ⊃ S ordinary) ≈ .9991 beforehand, and P(S ordinary | T bizarre) ≈ .90 afterwards. So the loss is no symptom of an "unmodeled connection".
   - The diagnostic assumes STATISM without saying so, and G&S argue that STATISM fails in general (§8; n. 47, p. 138).
   - By Prop. 1, K's knowledge of propositions not involving the discovered coordinate is also unchanged. So the phrase "eliminates a supposed constraint on the *scale's* error" misdescribes what is lost: it is a joint constraint.
   - **Fix.** State exactly what ΠK does under STATISM: the posterior is the product of the unchanged marginals with the single-source update on the discovered coordinate. Concede that ΠK inherits single-source defeat, and engage G&S §§6 and 8.

3. **Title, abstract, §§3–4: "correlational knowledge" is a misnomer.**
   - Non-rectangular knowledge is not knowledge of a correlation.
   - On G&S's parameters, ΠK at (0,2) leaves (1,2) open (probability ≈ .0008) while excluding (2,0), which is ten times as probable (≈ .008). K, by contrast, treats the equiprobable worlds (1,2) and (2,1) alike.
   - §2 disclaims probabilistic independence, but the motivating rhetoric is probabilistic.
   - **Fix.** Use "cross-source" or "non-modular" knowledge instead. Say that the critique lives on the multidimensional branch, between which and probabilism G&S stay neutral (p. 123). This also bounds the essay's significance.

4. **Scope: the result depends on how the product is factored.**
   - ΠK depends on the factorization. Take three {0,1,2} chains and w = (0,2,0). Grouping them as (X1×X2)×X3 gives R_K^{12}(0,2)×{0,1}, which excludes (1,2,0). The three-factor ΠK includes it.
   - The essay's normative vocabulary is "sources" ("separate mechanisms, calibration histories, and subject matters"). Sources need not coincide with order-theoretic chains, and n. 37 is a case where G&S reject the product treatment for two distinct instruments.
   - ΠK is undefined on non-product evidence cells, which are exactly the joint-constraint cases §4 sets aside.
   - **Fix.** Say what fixes the factors. Defend the finest factorization, and make the meet formula the official statement.

5. **§6: the strongest dialectical payoff is under-developed and under-credited.**
   - G&S's abductive case for COMPARABILITY ↔ MARGINS rests partly on K being simpler than KM when Comparability fails (p. 111).
   - On the product branch, which is exactly where Comparability fails (pp. 117–118), Prop. 4 shows that KM = {v : no coordinate of v is sufficiently less normal than w's}. That is non-disjunctive and factorizes. K is disjunctive and does not factorize. The simplicity consideration plausibly *reverses*.
   - The essay says only that it does "not refute their assessment of every theoretical virtue".
   - This dimension-wise construction is the form G&S attribute to Carter & Goldstein (n. 30, p. 123). Prop. 4 therefore shows that KM coincides with C&G's construction on products (minus the second-order dimension), and C&G should be credited for that.

6. **Literature.**
   - The essay has three references, two of which I cannot check from the packet.
   - It omits the parts of the main source that bear directly on its thesis:
     - Gettier and Convexity, pp. 113–115;
     - monotonicity, p. 116;
     - NO DEFEAT and STATISM, pp. 127–128 and 131, nn. 35–37;
     - the preface and the probabilist branch, pp. 119–123.
   - It also omits the cliff-edge and margins literature (Williamson 2013b; G&S 2018), and the belief-revision work on language splitting.

## Proof check

**Setting.** E = X_1×…×X_m. Each X_i is finite, carries a total preorder ⪰_i, and has a relation ≫_i satisfying (i)–(iii). The global relations are the product relations.

**(P0) Background claims: all hold.**
- *The product relations satisfy (i)–(iii).* For chaining: suppose a ⪰ b ≫ c ⪰ d with b_i ≫_i c_i. Then a_i ⪰ b_i ≫ c_i ⪰ d_i, which gives a_i ≫_i d_i, and so a ≫ d.
- *B = ∏B_i.* If v_i ∉ B_i because u_i ≫_i v_i, then v[i↦u_i] ≫ v. This step needs E to be the full product.
- *Each B_i is non-empty*, because ≫_i is a strict partial order on a finite set.

**(P1) Prop. 1: correct.** Projection distributes over unions of non-empty products.

**(P2) Prop. 2: correct.**
- (⇒) v = w[e↦b_e] lies in R_ΠK(w) \ R_K(w), because v_d ∉ B_d and b_e is not ⪰ w_e.
- (⇐) Totality plus upward closure give B_d ⊆ U_d(w_d), and the hypothesis gives B_e ⊆ U_e(w_e). So R_K(w) = U(w), or R_K(w) = B if w ∈ B.
- Totality is essential to (⇐).

**(P3) Prop. 3: every clause is correct.**
- *Factivity:* w_i ∈ U_i(w_i).
- *Inductive Limits:* ∏B_i ⊆ R_ΠK.
- *Convexity:* each factor B_i ∪ U_i(w_i) is upward closed.
- *Inductive Knowledge:* suppose w ≫ v via w_i ≫_i v_i. Then v_i ∉ B_i, and v_i ⪰ w_i would give v_i ≫ v_i.
- *Transitivity:* y ∈ B_i ∪ U_i(x) implies B_i ∪ U_i(y) ⊆ B_i ∪ U_i(x). This step does not need totality.
- *Minimality:* any rectangle satisfying the three principles contains B ∪ U(w), and hence the product of its projections.

**(P4) The meet formula: correct.**
- Choose b_i = v_i if v_i ∈ B_i, and any member of B_i otherwise.
- If min(w_i, b_i) = w_i, then v_i ∈ U_i(w_i). Otherwise v_i ∈ B_i by upward closure.
- The essay's warning about non-products is right. Counterexample: E = {w, b, m, v}, with w and b incomparable, m = w∧b, w ⪰ v ⪰ m, w ≫ v, w ≫ m. Then B = {w, b}, and the formula admits v even though w ≫ v.

**(P5) Prop. 4: correct.**
- (⊇) Set u_i = min(w_i, v_i).
- (⊆) Suppose w_i ≫_i v_i. Then v[i↦w_i] ≫ v, and w_i ≫_i u_i for every common lower bound u.
- The proof uses totality and the full product.

**(P6) The worked examples are all correct.**
- R_K(0,2) is the five worlds the essay lists.
- The material conditional is known, and "scale ordinary" is not.
- In the cell X_1×{2}, both (0,2) and (1,2) remain open.
- At (1,1), R_ΠK = {0,1}² and R_KM is all nine worlds.

**No stated result fails.** Only the informal glosses fail: the gloss on Prop. 2, "separation", and "no additional relation".

**Results the essay should add (all verified):**
- **Lemma M.** Let v ∈ R_ΠK(w) \ R_K(w) and u = w∧v. Then:
  - w ⪰ u, and v ⪰ u;
  - ¬(w ≫ u), because each u_i is either w_i or a member of B_i;
  - u is not ⪰ w, since otherwise v ∈ U(w).
  
  So MARGINS puts u into the set and Convexity puts v into it. Hence R_K ⊆ R_ΠK ⊆ R_KM ⊆ R_I.
- **Surplus worlds need not lie below actuality.** Take X_i = {0,1,2,3} with 0≫2, 0≫3, 1≫3. At w = (0,3) the surplus is {(1,2), (1,3)}, and (1,2) is incomparable with w.
- **Disjunction property.** R is rectangular iff, for every i, every p about coordinate i alone and every q about the remaining coordinates, R ⊆ p∪q implies R ⊆ p or R ⊆ q. For (⇐), use singleton splits and induct on the coordinates.
- **Partition dependence** is shown in Major 4.
- **Further properties of ΠK.** ΠK satisfies monotonicity (p. 116) and allows no purely veridical Gettier cases (p. 115). Under BELIEF, K at (0,2) yields knowledge of "T∈{0,1} ∨ S=0", and ΠK does not.
