# Referee report — S7 Philosopher of causation, chance and science — P-3b36

## Thesis and argument as I understand them (≤120 words)

Andreas and Günther (A&G 2024, §5) remove model-relativity by defining causation in the world over stable, "metaphysically direct" regularities. The essay argues, conditionally, that such a reduction needs a further existence condition: enough metaphysically direct regularities must exist to recover every causal connection in its domain. A dyadic relay shows the gap. Every finite refinement connects source to receiver, but the dense union has no covers, so the closure of globally direct links is empty. Hence persistence of verdicts under refinement cannot guarantee a direct basis. P1 (endpoint connection), P2 (endless relevant mediation) and P3 (recovery only from unmediated links) are jointly inconsistent. A defender must posit a causal grain, restrict the domain, or drop recovery from direct pieces.

## The single most serious problem

**Location.** The bridge from §3 to A&G, in §§4 and 6.

**Problem: the draft has no demonstrated victim.**
- What it proves is an elementary fact about orders: a dense order has no covers.
- It turns that fact against the inference "verdicts persist under refinement, so a final direct basis exists." Nobody is shown to draw that inference.
- A&G certainly do not draw it. Their §5 is definitional, with no argument from invariance (p. 26).
- The claim that would matter is never argued. That claim is that A&G's reduction fails, or needs an extra axiom, in a scenario they are committed to handling. The draft explicitly declines to argue it ("The present argument supplies none of those", §6), and P2 stays a stipulation about a graph.

**Why it matters.** Inside A&G's own formalism the missing result takes about a page (see Proof check 7a–d). A&G's stated standard of conceptual accuracy (pp. 7, 29) then makes it bite. As written, the essay is a correct remark, not a contribution to a live debate. The fix is not more hedging. It is to run the construction through A&G's definitions, then price the escape routes against A&G's own commitments.

## Major problems

1. **§3: the model is not the right one for the case.**
   - *Problem.* The relay graph is not an A&G causal model. A&G treat directness as a property of type-level Boolean regularities, defined by a specific subformula factorization clause (relative directness p. 6; metaphysical directness p. 26). It is not adjacency of stages.
   - The draft never writes down the regularities, the set F of particular facts, or the factorization. It never shows that "metaphysically direct" coincides with "cover" in its example.
   - It also slides between a token transmission ("a signal passing from a source to a receiver") and type-level regularities. A&G's regularities are type-level generalizations (pp. 3–5), so the relay must be given as recurrent stage types instantiated across cases.
   - *Why it matters.* Until this is done, P2 is a claim about a graph, not about A&G's set L of metaphysically direct regularities.
   - *Fix.* Formalize the relay in A&G's notation, then apply the §5 definition with conditions (1)–(5) (pp. 10, 13, 17, 19). Proof check 7a–b shows this can be done.

2. **§§3–4: the example sits in a case the source explicitly leaves open.**
   - *Problem.* A pure relay is a chain of single-cause biconditionals X_y ↔ X_x. A&G call such symmetric regularities "problematic limiting cases" (p. 8). They assume direction "can always be determined" (p. 9). They concede their direction strategy "does not work for scenarios where some type effect has only a single type cause" (p. 30).
   - *Why it matters.* A defender can bracket the example as territory A&G have already flagged.
   - *Fix (a).* Adopt A&G's own temporal option for direction (p. 8). Cite p. 7, where they say a theory should give right verdicts "in all conceptual possibilities – including a scenario where one event is the only cause of another". So they owe a verdict here.
   - *Fix (b).* Separately test the other symmetry-breaker, alternative causes (p. 9). I suspect a dense relay with an independent injector at every stage has no finite non-redundant regularity at all, because disjunctions are finite (fn. 3, p. 5). If so, that is a second, independent failure worth reporting.

3. **§5: the three responses are priced generically, not against the target's commitments.** The source supplies the levers the draft needs.
   - *(i) Conceptual accuracy.* A&G demand conceptual accuracy (p. 7; p. 29: "these scenarios seem to be conceptually possible. And so a conceptually accurate theory should still align…"). They also use the mere conceptual possibility of a simple metaphysically direct regularity against Blanchard and Schaffer (p. 27). By parity, a conceptually possible continuous process counts against them. So a merely contingent causal grain (response 1) and a domain restriction (response 2) both conflict with their stated method.
   - *(ii) Directness is load-bearing.* It is what gives the "full description" (p. 6). My check: if the stable but non-direct C∨A↔E were in L, the pre-empted a would satisfy Definition 1 with F′ = {¬B}, since A together with C∨A↔E entails E after the settings. So directness is necessary, though not sufficient (pp. 6–7), for A&G's pre-emption verdict (p. 11). Response 3 must therefore say what replaces directness without losing pre-emption.
   - *(iii) Uneven pricing of response 3.* The draft charges response 3 with inserting causation as a primitive ordering (§5). But A&G's aptness clause is itself defined by "dependences … stronger than causation" (p. 26). Yet they claim their theory "has no need for discerning apt from non-apt causal models" (p. 28).
   - *Why it matters.* These points turn a generic "burden" into a trilemma specific to A&G, which is what would carry a full essay.

4. **§§1 and 6: novelty and literature.**
   - *Problem.* §6 concedes the point may be "too familiar" but credits none of its history:
     - Russell on the absence of contiguous causes in a continuum;
     - Salmon's at-at theory, which is response 3 for processes;
     - Zeno causality (Benardete's gods, Hawthorne, Yablo), on effects with no first or direct cause;
     - the no-fundamental-level and causal-drainage debates (Schaffer, Block, Kim), the closest dialectical analogue of "no final layer".
   - It also ignores the nearest work on its own target. Fn. 9 (p. 30) announces "another challenge to the reductivity of Baumgartner's theory and ours" in A&G's sequel, "A Lewisian Regularity Theory". The essay must establish whether its point is that challenge.
   - Gallow is dismissed in one sentence. If, as I believe (about 70%), Gallow's theory is built so that verdicts agree across accurate models of different grain, it is a published version of response 3 and must be engaged.

5. **Throughout: the "existence condition" is never stated.**
   - *Problem.* In A&G's framework it can be stated exactly. Entailment is classical (p. 8), hence compact. So if the variable E occurs in no metaphysically direct regularity, nothing causes e (Proof check 7b).
   - The required condition is therefore: every caused variable occurs in some metaphysically direct regularity. For a single process, every caused stage must have an immediate predecessor.
   - The ω+1 case (7c) shows this is stronger than "there is an ultimate grain". It also exhibits the draft's claim in §2, never shown there, that direct elements may "exist but be too sparse".
   - *Why it matters.* Stating the condition exactly turns a vague burden into a crisp, contestable thesis.

## Minor problems

- **No page references.** Every claim about the source concerns §5, pp. 25–28.
- **"Extremely fine grained" (§2).** The source says "very fine-grained" (p. 26).
- **§1 overstates A&G's definition.** "Requiring directness under every admissible enlargement" is stronger than what A&G define. Their notion is non-factorizability through *stable* regularities (p. 26). Indirectness that arises only through non-stable regularities does not defeat metaphysical directness.
- **Only half of model-relativity is treated.** A&G's model-relativity has two halves (p. 26, citing Baumgartner 2013, pp. 93–94). Extension can make direct regularities indirect, and it can make non-redundant ones redundant. The essay treats only the first and should say so.
- **§3 undersells its own point.** "Allowing infinitely many direct steps does not repair this particular example" is true, but more holds. By compactness, infinitely many steps can never help in A&G's framework.
- **§5 gives the aptness crux one paragraph.** Temporal versus causal divisibility deserves more.
  - Refinement by sub-intervals makes the new variables parts of the coarser ones. That is a mereological dependence, so it is inapt.
  - The argument therefore needs instantaneous-stage variables, and it must defend treating them as events.
- **§6 concedes too much.** It spends about a quarter of the text on concessions. Most can go once the formal result is in place.
- **Three key terms are never related.** "Admissible" (refinements), "apt" (A&G) and "relevant" (P2) should be unified or explicitly linked.
- **Say that the relay is deterministic.** A&G set indeterminism aside (fn. 1, p. 3), so one sentence saying so heads off a distraction.

## Claims that look false, misattributed or unverifiable

- **Abstract: "invariance of a verdict cannot supply the missing existence condition."** This is true, but the inference it blocks is attributed to no one. A&G do not argue from invariance of verdicts (p. 26). At best it pre-empts a defence nobody has offered, so it risks attacking a strawman.
- **§1: "directness under every admissible enlargement".** This overstates A&G's definition (p. 26).
- **§2: "extremely fine grained".** The source says "very fine-grained" (p. 26).
- **§2: the conditional "concerning having those regularities available".** This is accurate (p. 28: "if we had the metaphysically direct regularities at our disposal"). But "at our disposal" reads epistemically, so the existence reading should be flagged as the essay's interpretation.
- **§1: Gallow 2021 as a "project of model invariance".** The bibliographic data match the source's reference list. The substance cannot be verified from the packet, and the pgrim.org URL is unverifiable here.
- **Implicit: the relay lies within A&G's settled domain.** This conflicts with pp. 8–9 and 30 (Major 2).

## What is genuinely good (be specific: revisions must preserve this)

- **The quantifier-order diagnosis (§3)** is exactly right and is the paper's best asset. "Every model has a witness to connectivity" differs from "connectivity has a witness composed of globally direct links".
- **The two-limits contrast (§3).** The union of the finite models' reachability relations recovers the order, while the closure of globally adjacent pairs is empty.
- **Stability versus directness (§2).** The draft correctly separates persistence of non-redundancy from mediation, and correctly notes that neither entails the other.
- **The diagnosis in §4.** Finite models guarantee adjacency only through the selection of variables, and the move to model-independence removes exactly that guarantee.
- **Fairness.** The draft never overclaims. It anticipates the aptness objection (§4) and flags temporal versus causal divisibility (§5).
- **The three-way choice** of grain, restriction, or recovery without a direct basis is the right skeleton for a full paper.

## Proof check

**Bottom line.** Everything the draft proves is correct and elementary. The philosophically important step is not proved, although it can be.

1. **D_n and G_n.** Each G_n is finite with a path 0 → … → 1, and refinement preserves order. Correct.
2. **Edge splitting.** The edge j/2^n → (j+1)/2^n becomes 2j/2^{n+1} → (2j+1)/2^{n+1} → (2j+2)/2^{n+1}. Reachability in G_{n+1}, restricted to D_n, equals reachability in G_n. Correct.
3. **No covers.** If x = a/2^m and y = b/2^m, then (x+y)/2 = (a+b)/2^{m+1} lies in D ∩ (x,y). So there are no covers, J = ∅, and J's transitive closure is ∅. Correct.
4. **Quantifier order.** "∀n ∃ a path in G_n" differs from "∃ a path whose edges are direct in every G_m containing them". Each edge is direct in exactly one G_n. So the lim inf of the edge sets is ∅, while ⋃_n Reach(G_n) = <_D. Correct, and well put.
5. **"Infinitely many direct steps".** Correct for this example, since J = ∅. In A&G's framework it holds in general: ⊨ is classical (p. 8) and compact, so any derivation uses only finitely many regularities.
6. **P1–P3.** Valid, on two readings:
   - P3 says: if there is a connection, it is recoverable from globally unmediated connections within the process;
   - P2 entails there are no such connections.
   The validity is shallow. All the weight is on P2 as applied to A&G, which the draft leaves unestablished.
7. **What the draft should add.** I checked each step against the source's definitions.
   - **(a) The relay inside A&G's framework.** Use types X_d (d ∈ D) that co-occur across cases, with direction fixed by time (p. 8).
     - For x < y, X_y ↔ X_x is true and non-redundant: {X_x} is minimally sufficient, and the one-disjunct disjunction is minimally necessary because ⊥ ↔ X_y is false.
     - It stays so under every extension that preserves the co-occurrence pattern, so it is stable, granted aptness.
     - For x < z < y, the stable regularities X_z ↔ X_x and X_y ↔ X_z meet the p. 26 factorization clause. X_x occurs in the antecedents for both X_y and X_z, and X_z occurs in a regularity for X_y.
     - So X_y ↔ X_x is not metaphysically direct, and no relay regularity is in L.
   - **(b) Lemma.** If E occurs in no member of L, then no c ≠ e causes e.
     - Proof. By condition (2), E ∉ F′. By Definition 1 (p. 10), F ⊨ L, so the actual valuation satisfies every L′ ⊆ L, every F′ ⊆ F, and C. Flip E to false. E occurs in none of these formulas, so the flipped valuation still satisfies the set obtained after the settings in condition (3), which is a subset of them. Hence condition (3) fails.
     - Combined with (a): in the dense relay, no stage causes any other.
   - **(c) The ω+1 relay.** Take stages a_n = 1 − 2^{−n}, plus the stage 1.
     - Each X_{a_{n+1}} ↔ X_{a_n} is metaphysically direct, because no stage lies between them.
     - Every regularity for X_1 factors through a later a_k, so X_1 occurs in no member of L.
     - By (b), the final stage is uncaused although L is infinite. This is the case of direct elements that exist but are too sparse.
     - So response 1 must demand an immediate predecessor at every caused stage, not merely "a grain".
   - **(d) Contrast with the model-relative verdict.** Take any finite V ⊆ D containing 0 and 1.
     - L relative to V is the chain of consecutive pairs.
     - Definition 2 + (5), with L′ = L and F′ = ∅, gives that X_0 causes X_1:
       - (2) holds because every stage entails X_1;
       - (3) holds by forward chaining;
       - (4) holds because X_0 is the only root and is deviant;
       - (5) holds trivially.
     - So every finite model says yes while §5 says no. That is the precise form of the draft's slogan.
   - **Caveat.** Step 7a assumes the stage-variable extensions are apt and that direction is temporal. Those are exactly the two premises the essay must defend.

## Top five changes (for a 6,000-word version)

1. **Prove the result instead of stating a burden (about 1,500 words).**
   - Put Proof check 7a–d in the body, in A&G's notation and with conditions (1)–(5).
   - State the existence condition exactly: every caused variable occurs in a metaphysically direct regularity.
   - Present the dense and ω+1 processes as conceptually possible scenarios. In each, every finite model says the source causes the receiver, while §5 says nothing does.
   - This is the paper's centrepiece.

2. **Make it A&G's trilemma (about 1,200 words).**
   - *Grain.* Given conceptual accuracy (pp. 7, 29), a contingent grain is not enough. They would need the conceptual necessity of an immediate-predecessor structure, which Russell and the Zeno-causality cases contest.
   - *Restriction.* This contradicts the "all conceptual possibilities" aim. It also sits badly with their own appeal to conceptual possibility against Blanchard and Schaffer (p. 27).
   - *Dropping directness.* Show what keeps the pre-emption verdict (pp. 6–7, 11), and the short-circuit and switch verdicts (pp. 19–20, 24–25).
   - Exploit the aptness tension (p. 26 versus p. 28).

3. **Benchmarks and realism (about 1,200 words).**
   - Re-model late pre-emption with continuous trajectories; it is untreated in the source. Re-model the switch the same way. Report the §5 verdict next to the finite-model verdict.
   - Defend instantaneous-stage variables as apt: unlike interval refinements, they introduce no mereological overlap. Say what they are events of.
   - Specify recurrent stage types; this settles the type/token issue.
   - Run the relay with alternative causes against the finite-disjunction requirement (fn. 3, p. 5).
   - Replace bare dyadic labels with a physically familiar case, such as a classical signal in a continuous medium.

4. **Literature and the exact increment (about 800 words).**
   - Engage Russell; Salmon; Benardete, Hawthorne and Yablo; Schaffer; Block and Kim; Woodward; Halpern and Hitchcock; Halpern 2016; Blanchard and Schaffer; Baumgartner 2013; Gallow 2021; Yablo and List & Menzies on proportionality.
   - Read A&G's sequel (fn. 9) before claiming novelty.
   - State the increment in one sentence. For example: "A&G's model-independence clause makes causation depend on an immediate-predecessor structure that their own conceptual-accuracy standard cannot assume."

5. **Build and test response 3 (about 1,300 words).**
   - Propose a stabilization definition: c causes e iff there is an apt V such that, for every apt V′ ⊇ V, c causes e relative to the direct non-redundant regularities of V′.
   - Prove it coincides with §5 whenever the existence condition holds. Proof check 7d already shows it gets the dense relay right.
   - Argue it is no less reductive than A&G's stability clause, which already quantifies over all apt extensions. This defuses the draft's own worry (§5) that response 3 must treat causation as primitive.
   - Check it against the verdict table (pp. 28–29) and against Gallow.
   - Cut most of §6's concessions to make room.

**Can this carry a full essay?** Not in its present form. With changes 1–3 it would be a solid short paper. With all five it could be a 6,000-word paper with a theorem, a trilemma tailored to A&G, and a positive proposal.
