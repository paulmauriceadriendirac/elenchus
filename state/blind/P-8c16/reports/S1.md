# Referee report — S1 Formal epistemologist (Bayesian foundations) — P-8c16

## Thesis and argument as I understand them (≤120 words)

Meehan and Zhang (MZ) vindicate conditionalization (BCOND) by total expected accuracy across updating problems. Their Fact 11 shows that the vindication holds exactly for extensional weightings. The essay argues that extensionality is:

(a) **Not disguised internalism.** MZ's uniform total ranks globally evidentially constant (GEC) rules exactly as a total over transparent yes/no problems does, so Fact 10 is Greaves–Wallace summed. But the uniform total over opaque problems alone is also extensional, and no transparent weighting is profile-equivalent to it.

(b) **Not bedrock.** Take product or additive weightings built from a state-independent importance h on propositions. For these, extensionality ⟺ constant H ⟺ the optimal rule obeys No News.

Via Lemma 1, any remaining non-extensional weighting builds a hypothesis about how one learns into the standard.

## The single most serious problem

**Location.** §3 (Propositions 1–3; the closing paragraph of §3.2; the abstract; §6), read against the essay's own gloss in §2 and its result in §4.2.

**The narrow precisification.** The claim that extensionality is "not disguised internalism" holds only under a narrow reading of both key terms:
- "internalism" means that the weighting is supported on transparent problems;
- "equivalent" means proportional learning profiles, i.e. an identical ranking of *all* GEC rules.

**Why "verdict" fails.** Hi-TEA is a claim about which rule is optimal. By Lemma 1, the optimal rule depends on each λ_p only up to a scale factor that may differ from proposition to proposition. On that measure, every extensional weighting whose profiles are positive on p selects exactly what the transparent yes/no weighting selects. That includes MZ's uniform weighting, λ°, and any other extensional mixture. So two claims are false on their natural reading:
- "no weighting of transparent problems reproduces that verdict" (end of §3.2);
- "that verdict has no internalist equivalent" (§6).

Proposition 3 shows only that λ° ranks *suboptimal* rules differently from any transparent weighting. The essay never argues that this matters for the vindication.

**Why the essay's own results tell against it.** Two of the essay's statements together identify extensionality with coarsening at random (CAR):
- the §2 gloss: "which true proposition one learns at s carries no information about s beyond the truth of that proposition";
- the §4.2 result: extensional weightings are exactly the learning hypotheses under which META-COND and BCOND agree for the representative agent.

CAR is the condition under which conditioning on the reported set equals conditioning on the event of the report. MZ's Fact 12 says that the point-mass (deterministic) extensional weightings are exactly the transparent problems. So extensionality is precisely transparency-in-distribution, imposed on the aggregate learning model built into the evaluator's weighting. Propositions 2–3 show only that the *support* of the weighting need not be transparent. MZ already say this ("one does not have to be an internalist to subscribe to extensionality", 338); the essay gives a better example of it.

**Why it matters.** This is the first half of the headline thesis, and it underwrites the claim to "strengthen" MZ. As stated, it either repeats MZ or is false in the sense that bears on the vindication.

**Fix.** Distinguish two notions of equivalence: ranking-equivalence and selection-equivalence (same optimal rule). Adopt the CAR characterization. Then restate the thesis: extensionality is a transparency condition on the evaluator's aggregate learning model, not on any agent's learning. That claim is true, sharper, and still worth publishing.

## Major problems

**1. Unacknowledged overlap with the coarsening-at-random literature (§3.2 polytope remark, Prop. 3, Cyclic Informants, §4.2).**
- **Problem.** Three of the essay's points have precedents it does not cite:
  - MZ's Lemma 2 is the classical result that conditioning on the observed set coincides with conditioning on the event of the observation iff the coarsening mechanism is CAR (Heitjan and Rubin 1991; Grünwald and Halpern 2003, "naive" vs "sophisticated" conditioning).
  - The §4.2 "representative agent" observation is sophisticated conditioning in Grünwald–Halpern's sense.
  - The polytope of extensional profiles (non-negative (c_p) with Σ_{p∋s} c_p = 1) is the CAR polytope. The fact that for n ≥ 3 it strictly exceeds the convex hull of partitions is, to my knowledge, the subject of Gill and Grünwald's (2008) geometric characterization, whose extreme points are uniform multicovers generalising partitions. Cyclic Informants, the three pairs covering each point twice, is the simplest such non-partition vertex. Proposition 3 is a special case.
- **Why it matters.** It overstates the originality of §§3.2 and 4.2, and a formal epistemologist will notice. It is also a missed opportunity: CAR as stochastic transparency is exactly what resolves the most serious problem above.
- **Fix.** Cite this literature. Recast Propositions 2–3 and the polytope as applications of known CAR geometry to MZ's dialectic, and claim only the application.

**2. Proposition 4: its content, its interpretation, and a dropped qualifier (§4.1, §4.2).**
- **(a) Content.** For *every* weighting, the optimal rule satisfies No News iff λ_S is constant on S. This follows from Lemma 1 and regularity; the essay uses it in its proof but never foregrounds it. So No News is just extensionality at the tautology S. Proposition 4's real content is that, in two families, extensionality at S spreads to every p. That is a nice but small point, and "extensionality is equivalent to No News" oversells it.
- **(b) Interpretation.** The essay does not show that the h-families model *importance*:
  - It describes the product form as "a random learning situation in which a true proposition is chosen independently at each state".
  - Its explanation of *Enough Butter* is a frequency claim: "learning nothing is relatively more common at 15".
  - So λ×_h is a model of how likely one is to learn each proposition, with state-independent propensities. That is a hypothesis about how one learns, the very thing §4.2 says weights should not encode.
  - Under MZ's own gloss of λ_p(s) as "the epistemic importance of updating accurately in response to learning proposition p at state s" (336), importance that "attaches to propositions" would simply *be* extensionality.
- **(c) Dropped qualifier.** MZ's non-Bayesian cares about "how epistemically important p is as a proposition *at s*" (337–338). The essay paraphrases this as "how important p is" (§4.2). State-relative importance, the Levinstein-style contingent importance MZ cite at 339, is exactly what Proposition 4 excludes by assumption.
- **(d) A false gloss.** The essay says state-independent factors make "the relative weight of two propositions … the same at every state where both are true". That is true of λ× but false of λ+, where λ+_p(s)/λ+_q(s) varies with s.
- **Fix.** Prove the general version. Whenever the profile's dependence on the state is common to all propositions (λ_p(s) = a(p)·g(s) or a(p) + g(s)), extensionality ⟺ g constant ⟺ No News.
  - This covers λ×, λ+ and further aggregations. I checked one: summing h over the *distinct* propositions in E's range gives an additively separable profile, so the result extends. Objection 1 therefore concedes too much.
  - Note also a structural fact. Each state's profile sums to the same total (Σ_{p∋s} λ_p(s) = Λ). So any state-independent relative weighting is forced into the λ× profile Λ·h(p)/H(s).
  - Then argue explicitly why this models importance rather than likelihood of learning, and engage state-relative importance.

**3. The argument for privileging extensional weightings is circular and incomplete (§4.2).**
- **Circularity.** The criterion ("hypotheses that would not change the rational response") is defined as agreement between META-COND and BCOND, so it presupposes BCOND as the baseline response.
- **No News is not special.** The accuracy rationale offered for No News (Noah's problem) is just the p = S instance of the yes/no argument behind MZ's Fact 7, which applies equally to every p. A non-Bayesian whose weighting already overrides conditionalization on {15,16} gets no new reason to treat S differently.
- **A definitional restatement.** "The remaining dispute concerns only weights that depend on the state at which a proposition is learned" (abstract) merely restates the definition of non-extensionality when read at the level of profiles. It says something substantive only about the factors h_s, and the abstract does not say so.
- **A missed consideration.** Assimilating all remaining state-dependence to "how likely it is to learn p at s" ignores state-relative importance (2c). For that, the essay's diagnosis that a learning hypothesis has been built into the standard does not obviously apply.
- **Fix.** Supply a criterion that does not presuppose BCOND. For example: any weighting invariant under all permutations of S is extensional (swapping two p-states leaves p fixed). This also proves the extensionality half of Proposition 2 in one line. Then ask whether a standard meant to hold "for any agent" (330 n. 35) should be neutral between states. Otherwise, scale "not bedrock" back to "reduced, within a family, to No News".

**4. Missing engagement with the closest philosophical work (§§1, 4.2, 5).**
- **Carr (2021, "A Modesty Proposal").** This is the source of the subjective reading of the weighted aggregate. MZ contrast it with their own reading in the very footnote the essay quotes (330 n. 35). The essay attributes the reading to MZ's "note" without naming Carr, and §4.2 must say what it adds to her.
- **Zendejas Medina (2024).** MZ flag this paper (287 n. 2). It vindicates BCOND by conditional expected accuracy, one proposition at a time. That is the same decomposition Proposition 1 and MZ's proof of Fact 10 exploit: Σ_p π(p)·E_{π(·|p)}[A(f(p),·)]. So the slogan "Fact 10 is Greaves–Wallace summed" should be set beside "Fact 10 is Medina-style conditional evaluation summed".
- **Levinstein (2019).** Cited but not engaged. As I recall it, his point is that world-varying importance weights make standard accuracy measures improper. That bears directly on Fact 11: on each p, a non-extensional profile acts as the effective score λ_p(s)·A(c,s), which is improper exactly when λ_p is not constant on p.
- **Fix.** Add a related-work paragraph and state the exact increment over each of these.

**5. Scope (whole essay).**
- **Problem.** This is a commentary on one paper. The increment over MZ is a set of easy (if correct) observations plus a relocation of the dispute that MZ largely anticipate ("how likely it is for one to learn p at s", 337). Nothing is said about what follows for the wider accuracy-first debate over externalism (Schoenfield, Das, Gallow, Isaacs–Russell).
- **Fix.** Draw the general moral. Any "accurate in general" vindication must fix an aggregate learning model, and BCOND is vindicated exactly when that model is CAR.

## Minor problems

- **Objection 4 checks only maximin,** yet it concludes that any vindication "must therefore aggregate by weights". Minimax regret should also be checked. Here regret in a problem is measured against the best GEC rule for that problem, whose value equals META-COND's. In Dana's prior space under Brier, minimax regret also favours the dogmatic rule:
  - BCOND's maximal regret is 1.188, attained in Dana's own problem, where META-COND is the truth rule.
  - The dogmatic rule's maximal regret is 1.12.

  Dominance-style standards also fail to single out BCOND, because the one-shot rule is undominated too (MZ n. 32). Adding these would strengthen the reply.
- **Shorten Proposition 2.** Its extensionality claim follows at once from permutation invariance. The Bell-number formula is needed only for Proposition 3.
- **Sensitivity to the state space.** In the h-families, the size of the No News violation depends on how finely S is divided. Suppose h = 1 on all 2^n − 1 propositions plus a fixed bump on one answer. Then H(s) = 2^{n−1} + const on that answer, so the tilt vanishes as S is refined. The large tilt in *Enough Butter* is partly an artefact of n = 3. Say whether h is meant to be invariant under refinement.
- **Abstract overclaims.** "I prove that extensionality is neither disguised internalism nor bedrock" goes too far. The "bedrock" half is argued, not proved, and §4.2 concedes that the argument is not conclusive.
- **Define "equivalent" before Proposition 3.** State that equivalence means proportional profiles, and say whether proportionality is also *necessary* for identical rankings. It is for the Brier score, by uniqueness of additively separable representations. Saying so would turn Proposition 3 into a statement about rankings, not just about profiles.
- **Footnote 35 is not MZ's reply.** The essay says "Their answer to agent-relative weighting is that total expected accuracy measures a rule's accuracy for any agent (330 n. 35)". That note contrasts MZ with Carr; MZ do not offer it as a reply in §5.3. Flag this as the essay's reconstruction.
- **Levinstein placement.** "compare Levinstein 2019" sits beside the p. 335 quotation. MZ cite Levinstein at 339, in a different role (an internalist who rejects extensionality). Make clear that the comparison is the essay's own.
- **Loose word.** "Proposition 1 gives the converse" is loose. "Fact 10 is derivable from Fact 1 summand by summand" is exact.
- **Computational checks.** The enumeration checks cannot be verified by a reader and are not needed, since each proposition has a short analytic proof. Move them to a footnote.

## What is genuinely good (be specific: revisions must preserve this)

- **Proposition 1 and its random-colouring reading.** A crisp, correct, deflationary redescription of MZ's headline result: the uniform total equals α_S times the yes/no total, and Fact 10 follows from Greaves–Wallace summand by summand. Readers of MZ should know it.
- **Proposition 2's dialectical point.** The vindication does not depend on transparent problems swamping opaque ones. With the observation that restricting to opaque problems preserves extensionality, this cleanly answers a natural worry.
- **The learning-profile bookkeeping.** In particular, each state's profile sums to the same total Λ (Σ_{p∋s} λ_p(s) = Λ). This is the right organizing device and makes every result easy to check.
- ***Enough Butter*.** Every number checks: H = (4, 8, 8); tilted prior (1/3, 1/2, 1/6); (2/5, 3/5, 0) on {15,16}; Noah −1.18 vs −1.12. The mechanism is intuitive. The corollary that importance placed on questions rather than on particular answers is harmless is a good one.
- **The representative-agent reading of Lemma 1.** It ties the selected rule to META-COND and hence to MZ's own reasons for noncontrastiveness. It is the essay's best philosophical idea, though it needs CAR and Carr credit.
- **Objection 4.** The dogmatic rule is worst-case optimal among GEC rules; the proof and the numbers (−1.188 vs −1.12) are correct.
- **Fidelity to the source.** It is exceptional: nearly every page reference and quotation checks out (see below). The hedging in §4.2 and §6 is honest.

## Top five changes

1. **Rebuild §3 on the CAR characterization.** Cite Heitjan and Rubin, Grünwald and Halpern, and Gill and Grünwald. Distinguish ranking-equivalence from selection-equivalence and withdraw the "verdict" sentences. Restate the thesis: extensionality is transparency-in-distribution of the evaluator's aggregate learning model, not internalism about agents. MZ's Fact 12 already says that the point-mass extensional weightings are exactly the transparent problems. Present Propositions 2–3 and Cyclic Informants as applications showing that this model need not be a mixture of transparent problems.
2. **Turn Proposition 4 into a general theorem.** Foreground that, for any weighting, No News ⟺ λ_S is constant. Prove the general version: whenever the profile's state-dependence is common to all propositions, extensionality ⟺ No News. Argue that the h-families model importance rather than likelihood of learning, and reconcile this with MZ's 336 gloss. Engage state-relative importance (MZ's "as a proposition at s"; Levinstein), possibly via the impropriety reading of non-extensional profiles.
3. **Give §4.2 a criterion that does not presuppose BCOND.** One option is state-neutrality: permutation invariance entails extensionality. Without such a criterion, downgrade "not bedrock". Acknowledge that the Noah-problem rationale for No News is the p = S case of the general yes/no argument, and say what makes S special.
4. **Engage Carr (2021) and Zendejas Medina (2024),** plus Bronfman, Gallow and Das where relevant. State the exact increment over each, and credit Carr for the subjective reading of the aggregate.
5. **Tighten the claims and the objections.**
   - Drop "prove" from the abstract and qualify "and nothing more" in Objection 2.
   - Extend Objection 4 to minimax regret (BCOND 1.188 vs dogmatic 1.12 in Dana's prior space) and to dominance (MZ n. 32).
   - Fix the λ+ gloss and restore "at s" in the paraphrase of MZ.
   - Address how the h-family results change when S is refined.
