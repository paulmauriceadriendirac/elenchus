# Research lanes (Phase 1b)

Chosen by the orchestrator on 2026-10-21 from `state/panel/lanes.md`, adjusted by
`state/calibration/brief.md` (prefer split questions; conditional, negative and structural
results; decisive cases or theorems). CONFIG: topic none, avoid none. The panel-analyst's most
crowded area (AI consciousness and moral status) is not mapped; all six lanes lie outside it.
Population ethics (panel rank 6) is dropped as saturated with impossibility results; interpreting
AI systems (rank 12) is dropped as crowded and hard to make decisive. Conditionals and modals
(rank 7) is added so that three lanes sit outside the decision-theory/ethics cluster.

## L1 — Decision theory under extreme stakes
Principles linking probability and value when utilities are unbounded or outcomes infinite:
fanaticism and anti-fanaticism, St Petersburg/Pasadena-type prospects, stochastic dominance and
background risk, symmetry and invariance principles, risk-weighted and other non-expected-utility
alternatives; which combinations of principles are consistent and what they entail.
*Why:* deepest specialist bench on the panel, provable results, committee-wide interest.

## L2 — Comparability, completeness and incommensurability of value and preference
Whether betterness, preference or credence must be complete; dominance and money-pump arguments
for and against incompleteness; sequential choice with incomplete preferences; the semantics of
comparatives as it bears on comparability; parity.
*Why:* an actively disputed question among several readers, crisp principles, links ethics,
decision theory and language.

## L3 — Knowledge, normality and inquiry
Higher-order knowledge (KK, omega knowledge, fragility); normality-based epistemic logic and
belief; safety and closure; knowledge–chance links; dogmatism puzzles, evidence avoidance and the
value of information; knowledge norms of assertion and action.
*Why:* low crowding, explicit models make countermodels checkable, a precise increment will be
recognised; a large share of the panel works in epistemology.

## L4 — Decision-theoretic questions about artificial agents (formal strand)
Formal questions about the preferences and choices of artificial agents: shutdown and
corrigibility results, incomplete or risk-averse preferences as safety properties, formal
definitions of deception and manipulation, instrumental convergence. Formal and conceptual
results only; not discursive alignment ethics.
*Why:* formal tractability plus relevance to AI; interests both decision theorists and
philosophers of AI. Extra care with attribution and originality (an organiser works here).

## L5 — Credence, updating and self-location
Bayesian foundations: accuracy-first arguments, conditionalization and its rivals, supposition,
deference and Reflection, opinion pooling, regularity and conditioning on measure-zero events,
and self-locating belief (Sleeping Beauty and relatives, where the profession is sharply split
and over half choose "other").
*Why:* very high verifiability, low crowding, a split question with a large "other" share.

## L6 — Conditionals, modals and probability
Triviality results and probabilities of conditionals; failures of modus ponens; epistemic modals
and their logic; free choice; learning conditional information; causal-model semantics for
counterfactuals.
*Why:* deep specialist bench, decisive semantic methods, low crowding; widens the essay pool
beyond the decision-theory cluster toward philosophy of language and logic.
