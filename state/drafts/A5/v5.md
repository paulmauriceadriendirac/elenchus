# Holistic Accuracy, Refinement, and the Lockean Thesis

**Abstract.** Dorst argues that maximizing expected accuracy yields Lockean thresholds for belief even when the accuracy of a belief state is not the sum of the accuracies of its members. I show that the derivation has two gaps and give a counterexample. A truth-directed score adds a premium when everything believed is true. For every positive premium and any conservative values shared by all propositions, it makes a proposition believed at one credence and withheld at a higher one, with values and context held fixed. The natural defense is that this score is language-dependent: splitting an improbable world removes the effect. I turn that defense into a theorem. Given truth-directedness and conservative increments, invariance of belief under all refinements of the space of possibilities entails fixed thresholds; without it they do not follow. Invariance under refinements that leave the question fixed is satisfied by a question-relative record score. Whether accuracy-first epistemology is Lockean therefore turns on a substantive question: do questions shape belief only through the values of individual propositions?

## 1. Introduction

Accuracy-first epistemology holds that rational doxastic states are those that best promote accuracy by the agent's own lights. With a separable score for outright belief, positive for true beliefs and negative for false ones, it yields a Lockean thesis: believe a proposition just in case your credence in it exceeds a threshold. Easwaran (2016) proved this, using expected score as a tool for showing such belief states non-dominated, and stated the analogue for values varying with the proposition, which yields proposition-specific thresholds; Dorst (2019) presents these results as his Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 and credits both to Easwaran (p. 183). Dorst's own further claim is about holism. Separability, the assumption that a state's accuracy is the sum of its members' accuracies, "seems to contradict epistemic holism" (p. 192, citing Joyce 2009, §5). Dorst argues that dropping it changes nothing: given a premise he calls Sequence-Irrelevance, fixed proposition-specific thresholds follow anyway (his Proposition 5).[^1]

My thesis is that Proposition 5 does not follow from its stated premises, and that the premise it needs is refinement invariance. Given truth-directedness and conservative values, this principle forces fixed thresholds by itself. A holist can reject it without rejecting accuracy maximization or order independence. I do not claim that Lockeanism or Separability is false, or that the score I use is the correct measure of epistemic value.

The main argument runs:

(P1) The record measure of Section 3 satisfies all of Proposition 5's premises, together with an explicit bridge from optimality to permission, and violates its conclusion (Theorem 1).

(C1) So Proposition 5 does not follow from its premises.

(P2) Given truth-directedness and conservative increments, refinement invariance entails fixed thresholds (Theorem 4).

(P3) Conversely, fixed thresholds that attach to propositions independently of how the space is described entail refinement invariance.

(C2) So any derivation of such thresholds from accuracy must establish refinement invariance, by whatever route.

(P4) Unrestricted refinement invariance requires that the question asked affect belief only through the values it assigns to individual propositions; its restricted form, invariance under refinements that leave the question fixed, is satisfied by a question-relative record measure (Section 6).

(C3) So that requirement, not accuracy maximization or order independence, decides whether accuracy-first epistemology is Lockean.

The argument has four steps. Section 3 gives a counterexample: a truth-directed, state-independent score that rewards an error-free belief state makes belief non-monotone in credence for every positive premium. Section 4 locates two gaps in the derivation and shows that, under the natural repair of the first, the derivation is valid exactly when the score is separable. Section 5 shows what the holistic score delivers instead: belief generated by a selected core hypothesis and a threshold. Section 6 takes up the strongest reply, that the counterexample is an artifact of how possibilities are individuated. I prove that invariance under all refinements, together with truth-directedness and conservative increments, entails fixed thresholds, and that the weaker invariance the reply actually motivates does not.

The closest prior work is of two kinds. Rothschild (2023) develops additive scoring representations of Lockean belief, and Wang (2025) studies expected-utility rules for deductively cogent belief states. Other bridge theories select a strongest accepted proposition. Levi (1967) accepts the consequences of the answer to a question that maximizes expected epistemic utility, which trades truth against informational value. Leitgeb (2014, 2017) ties rational belief to stably high credence, with belief fixed by a strongest believed proposition. Dorst and Mandelkern (2022) model guesses as maximizing expected answer-value, an accuracy-informativity tradeoff over answers to a question, and show that only filtered guesses do so, an observation they credit to Holguín. Holguín (2022) argues that rational thinking is non-monotone in credence and question-sensitive. None of these works offers an accuracy score on unrestricted belief states meeting the conditions of Dorst's §4.2, which is what bears on Proposition 5. The increments here are a counterexample to that proposition, a proof that the derivation is valid only for separable scores under its natural repair, a theorem identifying refinement invariance as the premise that does the work, and a proof that core selection with filtering, the structure of these rival theories, falls out of a purely alethic score.

For readers outside formal epistemology the stakes are these. If accuracy alone settled the bridge from credence to belief, the Lockean would win that debate by default. If the shape of the bridge depends on whether the question being asked can affect belief other than through the value of each answer, the debate over Lockean, guess-like and closed belief becomes a debate about questions and epistemic value. Accuracy-first epistemology is then a framework for conducting that debate, not a verdict on it.

## 2. Dorst's derivation

Let $W$ be a finite set of worlds, propositions subsets of $W$, and a belief state $B$ any set of propositions. Accuracy First leaves inconsistent, nonclosed and empty states among the options (Dorst 2019, p. 183). An accuracy measure assigns a real number $A(B,w)$ to each state at each world. Given a probability function $C$ on $W$, expected accuracy is $EA_C(B)=\sum_w C(w)A(B,w)$, and Maximize Expected Accuracy says that $B$ is rational only if no state has higher expected accuracy. I add an explicit bridge from optimization to permission:

(Permission) Every belief state that maximizes expected accuracy is rationally permitted.

Without it, Maximize Expected Accuracy states only necessary conditions, and no score could show any state rational; Section 7 returns to this.

Under Separability, $A(B,w)=\sum_{p\in B}v_p(w)$, where $v_p(w)=T_p>0$ if $w\in p$ and $v_p(w)=F_p<0$ otherwise. Then $EA_C(B)=\sum_{p\in B}e_p$ with
$$e_p = C(p)T_p+(1-C(p))F_p ,$$
so every optimum includes $p$ when $e_p>0$ and excludes it when $e_p<0$. The threshold is $\tau_p=-F_p/(T_p-F_p)$. Call the values conservative when $T_p<-F_p$; then $\tau_p>1/2$. This extends Dorst's Conservativeness, $|T|<|F|$ for uniform values (p. 185); his Variable Conservativeness for proposition-dependent values instead requires $|T_p|<|F_{\neg p}|$ and $|T_{\neg p}|<|F_p|$ (p. 191), and lets thresholds fall below $1/2$.

In §4.2 Dorst lets the measure depend on the agent's current state $B_0$, "to capture the sense in which it matters what other beliefs she holds" (p. 193). Adding $p$ to $B_0$ raises the score where $p$ is true and lowers it where $p$ is false; Dorst writes the increments as $T^{B_0}_p$ and $F^{B_0}_p$ and remarks that this "assumes only that $A_{B_0}$ is well-defined" (p. 193). Proposition 4 then gives a threshold for the last proposition added in a sequence. Sequence-Irrelevance says that "rational constraints on beliefs are not affected by the sequence (if any) in which they are formed" (p. 194), and Proposition 5 concludes that there are "belief-independent values $T_p$ and $F_p$" fixing a threshold for each proposition (p. 195).

Two features of Proposition 5's scope matter below. Its $T_p$ and $F_p$ are values of the measure in a context, not functions of the credences. The proof obtains them by setting $T_p$ equal to $T^{B^-}_p$ for a state $B^-$ (p. 207). And Dorst's reply to the worry that variable Lockeanism is empty depends on it: the thresholds "come from the (dis)values of true (false) beliefs" (p. 196). So a score under which, with values and context fixed, a proposition is believed at one credence and not at a higher one refutes the proposition. Second, I read Sequence-Irrelevance as Dorst states it, as a constraint on rationality: the order in which a final state was formed does not change its rational status. Section 3 shows that the counterexample respects it on that reading.

## 3. The counterexample

Define the *record measure* with premium $\beta>0$:
$$A_\beta(B,w)=\sum_{p\in B}v_p(w)+\beta\cdot[\,w\in{\textstyle\bigcap} B\,],$$
where $\bigcap\varnothing=W$ and $[\cdot]$ is 1 if the condition holds and 0 otherwise. Call $\bigcap B$ the *core* of $B$: the strongest proposition the state commits its holder to. The premium rewards the truth of that total commitment. A state whose core is true is one from which any multi-premise deduction yields only truths, and a body of belief can be valuable as a whole in this way, which is the holist's thought. Dorst motivates Separability by an exam scored question by question (p. 184); many exams also reward a perfect paper. The premium does not grow with the number of beliefs, because it rewards one proposition, the core; the informativity of a state is rewarded by the separable part. The empty state's core is $W$, whose truth is trivial, so it earns the premium everywhere, as an uninformative answer should. The measure is purely alethic: $A_\beta(B,w)$ depends only on $B$ and on which of its members are true at $w$.

It meets the conditions of §4.2. Adding a true $p$ raises $A_\beta$ by exactly $T_p$, since if every member of $B_0$ was true at $w$, all still are. Adding a false $p$ lowers it by $|F_p|$, or by $|F_p|+\beta$ if $B_0$ was error-free at $w$. So the measure is strictly truth-directed, and every increment is conservative when $T_p<-F_p$. It does not depend on the current state, so Maximize Expected Accuracy issues one verdict about final states however they are reached, and every belief state is an option.

*The case.* Let $W=\{w_1,w_2,w_3\}$, $T_p=1$ and $F_p=-2$ for every $p$ (so $\tau=2/3$), and $\beta=3$. Write $\mathbf 1=\{w_1\}$, $12=\{w_1,w_2\}$ and $13=\{w_1,w_3\}$. At $C=(0.70,0.15,0.15)$ the unique optimum among all $2^8$ states is $\{\mathbf 1,12,13,W\}$, with expected accuracy $4.30$. The runner-up, $\{12,13,W\}$ at $4.20$, has the same core and lacks only $\mathbf 1$, so the margin is $e_{\mathbf 1}=0.10$: here the separable term decides. At $C'=(0.75,0.01,0.24)$ the unique optimum is $\{13,W\}$, with $4.94$; the runner-up is $\{\mathbf 1,12,13,W\}$ at $4.75$. Here the premium decides: the core $13$ is nearly certain, and its premium outweighs what the more specific core adds. (The optimum at $C'$ also omits $12$, whose credence $0.76$ exceeds $\tau$.)[^2] So $\mathbf 1$ is believed at credence $0.70$ and not at $0.75$. By Permission both optima are rational, and Proposition 5 would require $\tau_{\mathbf 1}\le 0.70$ and $\tau_{\mathbf 1}\ge 0.75$.

The case is not a knife-edge.

**Theorem 1.** Let $0<T<-F$, $\beta>0$, $D=T-F$, $\tau=-F/D$, and
$$c_0=\frac{\beta-T-2F}{2D+\beta},\qquad c_1=\frac{\beta-2F}{2D+\beta}.$$
Then $\max(c_0,\tau)<c_1<1$, and for all $c<c'$ in $(\max(c_0,\tau),c_1)$, $\mathbf 1$ belongs to the unique optimum at $(c,\frac{1-c}{2},\frac{1-c}{2})$ and to no optimum at $(c',\varepsilon,1-c'-\varepsilon)$, for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon\ge 0$.

The proof is in the Appendix. For $0<\beta\le D$ the interval has length $\beta T/(D(2D+\beta))$: it narrows as the premium shrinks but never closes. This instantiates what Holguín calls Non-Monotonicity: an agent can be permitted to think $p$ at a lower credence and not at a higher one (2022, §5).

*Fixed context.* A defender may reply that variable Lockeanism already lets belief in $p$ depend on credences in alternatives. In Dorst's model of the question "Who do you think will win?", the question lowers the penalty for falsely believing answers of the form "x wins" "until it gets an answer" (p. 190). Whether one believes that Steve wins at credence $0.48$ then depends on whether a rival is more probable. But in that model the dependence enters through a change of values, driven by the demand that the question be answered, which is not itself an expected-accuracy calculation; within each context belief is monotone in credence. The record measure produces the dependence with values, question and context fixed, as a consequence of maximizing expected accuracy. This matters for Holguín's objection that sophisticated Lockeanism requires "brute stipulations about the coordination of the proposition-sensitive thresholds" and "remains powerless to account for non-monotonicity" (2022, §7.2). Here no coordination is stipulated.

*Order.* The record measure does make one procedure order-dependent. At $C$, an agent who starts from the empty state and considers $\mathbf 1$ first gains $e_{\mathbf 1}-\beta C(\{w_2,w_3\})=0.1-0.9=-0.8$, declines it, and ends greedily at $\{12,13,W\}$. Considering $\mathbf 1$ last, she gains $0.1$ and ends at the optimum. Dorst's example of designing a sequence to make belief in God rational (p. 194) might suggest a violation of Sequence-Irrelevance. It is not one. Proposition 4 is a necessary condition: a final state is rational only if it maximizes expected accuracy relative to its predecessor. A global optimum meets that condition however it is written as the last step of a sequence, so Proposition 4 applies to it in every order. The greedy outcome is not an optimum, so under Maximize Expected Accuracy it is not rational whatever sequence produced it. No sequence confers rationality on any state. What depends on order is a procedure that Maximize Expected Accuracy does not endorse; Dorst himself says the process of forming beliefs in sequence "may be purely imaginative" (p. 194).

## 4. Diagnosis

*The quantifier gap.* Truth-directedness gives, for each world where $p$ is true, some positive increment. The passage on p. 193 then names one number $T^{B_0}_p$ as "the value" of adding a true belief in $p$. That moves from "for every $w$ there is a $t$" to "there is a $t$ for every $w$." Under the record measure, adding the false $\mathbf 1$ to $\{13\}$ costs 2 at $w_2$ but 5 at $w_3$, since only at $w_3$ was the prior state error-free. In expectation,
$$EA_C(B)-EA_C(B\setminus\{p\})=e_p-\beta\,C\big({\textstyle\bigcap}(B\setminus\{p\})\setminus p\big),$$
a threshold on $C(p)$ that moves with the credence of the rest of the core outside $p$. The step needs:

(ME) *Marginal extensionality.* For every $B_0$ and $p\notin B_0$, $A(B_0\cup\{p\},w)-A(B_0,w)$ depends on $w$ only through whether $w\in p$.

ME is not a consequence of well-definedness. It says the marginal value of a belief depends on the world only through that belief's own truth value.

*The content gap.* Grant ME. Proposition 4 then yields thresholds $\tau^{B^-}_p$ indexed to $B^-$, the rest of the state, and the proof of Proposition 5 generalizes from one $B^-$ to all. That requires $\tau^{B^-}_p$ not to vary with $B^-$. Sequence-Irrelevance cannot supply this: $B^-$ is not a sequence but the content of the other beliefs, which is what the holist says matters. Dorst himself separates the two: "no holist ever thought that the insight of their position is that the order in which you consider propositions affects what you ought to believe" (p. 195). The natural strengthening is:

(BI) *Background-independence.* $T^{B_0}_p$ and $F^{B_0}_p$ do not depend on $B_0$, for states $B_0$ not containing the complement of $p$.

**Theorem 2.** Let $A$ be truth-directed, and consider states containing no proposition together with its complement.[^3] (a) $A$ satisfies ME iff $A(B,w)=A(\varnothing,w)+\kappa(B)+\sum_{p\in B}J_p\,[w\in p]$ for a world-independent $\kappa$ and constants $J_p>0$ (the sum over contingent $p\in B$). (b) $A$ satisfies ME and BI iff $\kappa$ is additive, that is, iff $A(B,w)=A(\varnothing,w)+\sum_{p\in B}v_p(w)$ for proposition-dependent values $v_p$.

The proof (Appendix) turns on one observation: adding $p$ and $q$ in either order gives the same total, and under ME one side of the resulting identity is affine in $[w\in p]$, the other in $[w\in q]$. Since $A(\varnothing,w)$ is common to all states, (b) returns us to the separable measures of Corollary 2.

The proof yields more than the theorem states. Under ME the gain $J^{B}_p=T^{B}_p-F^{B}_p$ is the same for every background $B$, so $\tau^{B}_p=-F^{B}_p/J_p$.

**Corollary.** Under ME, on the stated domain, the threshold for $p$ is background-independent iff BI holds. Hence the step from $\tau^{B^-}_p$ to a single $\tau_p$ in the proof of Proposition 5 is valid iff $A$ is separable on that domain.

So, under ME, reading Sequence-Irrelevance strongly, as requiring background-independent thresholds, is no weaker than BI. And the Corollary yields a dilemma. Under ME, the only holism left is the world-independent $\kappa$ of Theorem 2(a), so the belief-dependence Dorst explicitly permits must take that form, and an additive $\kappa$ is just separability. If he admits it, Proposition 5 fails: a small bonus for deductively closed states satisfies ME and truth-directedness and makes belief non-monotone.[^4] If he excludes it, his §4.2 measures are separable on the domain, and §4.2 adds nothing to Corollary 2. Without ME, the record measure is a counterexample. Theorem 2 concerns a single measure. Dorst's measures are indexed to the current state; for such a family, ME at each member's own state, together with BI read across the family (the increments $T^{B_0}_p$ and $F^{B_0}_p$ of $A_{B_0}$ at $B_0$ do not depend on $B_0$), yields fixed thresholds through Proposition 4 without collapsing the family into one separable measure. The counterexample is unaffected, since a state-independent measure is a constant family.

## 5. What holism delivers instead

The record measure's optima have an exact description. For each proposition $X$ let
$$\Phi(X)=\beta\,C(X)+\sum_{p\supseteq X}\max(e_p,0).$$

**Theorem 3.** For any finite $W$ and proposition-dependent values, $\max_B EA_C(B)=\max_X\Phi(X)$. A state $B$ is optimal iff its core maximizes $\Phi$, $B$ contains every $p\supseteq\bigcap B$ with $e_p>0$, and $B$ contains no $p$ with $e_p<0$. Call the case *generic* when $\Phi$ has a unique maximizer $X^*$ and no $C(p)$ equals a threshold. With uniform values, in the generic case the unique optimum is $B^*=\{p\supseteq X^*: C(p)>\tau\}$, and: (i) $B^*$ is consistent iff $X^*\neq\varnothing$, and it is closed under single-premise entailment; (ii) $X^*$ contains every world more probable than any world it contains; (iii) $B^*$ is deductively closed iff $C(X^*)>\tau$, in which case it consists of exactly the consequences of $X^*$.

Call this *core-relative Lockeanism*. The agent selects a working hypothesis $X^*$, trading its probability against the above-threshold content it entails, and believes the above-threshold propositions it entails. Lockean belief is the special case in which the best core is the intersection of all above-threshold propositions. Closure can fail: on four worlds with $C=(0.62,0.18,0.10,0.10)$ and the values of Section 3, the unique optimum believes $\{w_1,w_2\}$ and $\{w_1,w_3\}$ but not $\{w_1\}$.

The structure is that of the rival bridge theories. $\Phi$ adds the expected accuracy of the core to a world-independent measure of how much valuable content it entails: an accuracy-informativity tradeoff over candidate total answers. Dorst and Mandelkern's expected answer-value has the same two ingredients, combined multiplicatively. A guess's expected answer-value is its probability times its answer-value if true, which is a parameter (at least 1) measuring the value of informativity, raised to the proportion of the question's complete answers that the guess rules out; false guesses score zero. Part (ii) is the analogue for cores of their argument that only filtered guesses maximize expected answer-value. Levi (1967) likewise accepts a strongest answer chosen by expected epistemic utility. The differences are instructive. For Levi and Leitgeb the agent accepts all and only the consequences of the selected proposition. Here belief is the above-threshold part of the core's consequences, and by (iii) cogency holds exactly when the core itself clears the threshold. What is new is not the structure but its source. Selecting a filtered core falls out of maximizing a purely alethic, truth-directed score over unrestricted belief states, which is the setting of Dorst's derivation. Guess-like belief is thus available inside accuracy-first epistemology, not only as a rival to it.

Increasing the premium, with credences and values fixed, cannot lower the credence of the optimal core (add the optimality inequalities at the two premiums), so it favors more probable cores; when world probabilities are distinct, (ii) makes these broader cores, which entail fewer beliefs. When every world has positive credence, $\Phi(W)-\Phi(X)$ grows linearly in $\beta$ for every proper $X$, so a large enough premium leaves only $W$ believed. The score therefore requires a defended balance between acquiring truths and protecting the joint truth of what one believes. Calling both aims accuracy does not fix their relative weight.

## 6. Refinement

The strongest reply to the counterexample is that it depends on how possibilities are individuated. Split the 1% world of $C'$ into two worlds of credence $0.005$. On the refined space $\Phi(\mathbf 1)=7.25$ exceeds $\Phi(\{w_1,w_3\})=6.91$, so $\mathbf 1$ is believed at $0.75$; it is also believed at the correspondingly refined $C$. The non-monotonicity is gone. In general, splitting a world of credence zero into $k$ worlds multiplies the separable part of $\Phi$ for every core excluding it by $2^{k-1}$, since each pair of coarse supersets that differ only on that world becomes $2^k$ refined supersets of equal credence, and it leaves the premium term alone, so by Theorem 3 the core is chosen as if the premium were $\beta/2^{k-1}$, and for fixed $\beta$ the interval of Theorem 1 shrinks exponentially in $k$. The premium is paid once while the separable sum ranges over the whole algebra. Verdicts about a fixed question should not depend on how unrelated possibilities are counted, and separable scores meet that demand.

Two repairs are available. First, the culprit is the flat sum. Replace it with the mean over the algebra $\mathcal A$: $A(B,w)=|\mathcal A|^{-1}\sum_{p\in B}v_p(w)+b\,[w\in\bigcap B]$. On a fixed space this only rescales the premium, so Theorems 1 and 3 apply unchanged. Splitting a credence-zero world now leaves the $\Phi$-value of every core that excludes it, and so every verdict, exactly unchanged. In the case of Section 3 (with $b=3/8$), splitting $w_2$ into 2, 4 or 8 equiprobable worlds, at both $C$ and $C'$, leaves both verdicts in place. Second, Dorst's accuracy function already takes "a set of propositions" as an argument (p. 179). Let that set be the algebra generated by the question in context. Splitting worlds that the question does not distinguish then changes nothing.

Neither repair yields invariance under every refinement, and no truth-directed, conservative non-Lockean score can. A *coarsening* is a surjection $\pi:W\to W^*$; it carries a credence $C$ on $W$ to $C^*=C\circ\pi^{-1}$ and identifies $p^*\subseteq W^*$ with $\pi^{-1}(p^*)$. Suppose the context assigns a measure to each finite space.

(RI) *Refinement invariance.* For every coarsening $\pi$, credence $C$ and $p^*\subseteq W^*$, $\pi^{-1}(p^*)$ belongs to every (some) optimum at $C$ iff $p^*$ belongs to every (some) optimum at $C^*$.

(CI) *Conservative increments.* On every two-world space, adding a contingent $p$ to any state gains less at the world where $p$ is true than it loses at the world where $p$ is false.

**Theorem 4.** Suppose every measure is truth-directed and CI and RI hold. Then for each contingent proposition $p$ there is a $\tau_p\in(1/2,1)$, fixed by the measure on the two-world coarsening $\{p,W\setminus p\}$, such that on every space and at every credence, $p$ belongs to every optimum if $C(p)>\tau_p$ and to none if $C(p)<\tau_p$.

The proof (Appendix) is short because RI does the work. Applied to the coarsening that merges everything outside $p$, it says that how the remaining probability is distributed cannot matter. That is the localization the Lockean needs. On the two-world problem, truth-directedness and CI then supply a single threshold above $1/2$. Conversely, separable measures whose conservative values are preserved under coarsening satisfy RI, so given truth-directedness and CI, RI holds exactly when thresholds attach to propositions independently of the space, except at ties. Neither premise can be dropped. The record measure is truth-directed and satisfies CI but not RI: on the coarsening $\{\{w_1\},\{w_2,w_3\}\}$ its threshold for $\{w_1\}$ is $(\beta-F)/(D+\beta)=5/6$, so it withholds $\{w_1\}$ at credence $0.70$, which it believes at $C$ on three worlds. And CI cannot be dropped: the separable measure with $T_p=2$ and $F_p=-1$ for every $p$ is truth-directed and satisfies RI, since its values survive coarsening, but it violates CI, and at credence $1/2$ its unique optimum contains both $p$ and $\bar p$, so no threshold in $(1/2,1)$ exists.[^5] So Lockeanism follows from truth-directedness, CI and RI without Separability, Sequence-Irrelevance, ME or BI, and Theorem 1 shows it does not follow without RI.

The refinement objection therefore splits in two. Its restricted form, invariance under refinements that leave the question fixed, is met by the question-relative record measure. Its unrestricted form requires more. Merging $w_2$ and $w_3$ is also a coarsening, so RI requires the verdict on whether horse 1 wins to be the same whether the question is "Which horse wins?" or "Does horse 1 win?", unless the values change. That is a substantive thesis about belief: questions affect belief only through the values they assign to individual propositions. Dorst's model of questions in §4.1 is consistent with it. Holguín's view, on which rational thinking is question-sensitive and depends on whether salient alternatives are more probable (2022, §§7.2, 8.1), denies it. The Lockean can adopt RI and obtain fixed thresholds. But RI is then the premise doing the work, and it is a claim about how questions bear on belief, not a consequence of accuracy maximization or of order independence. Whether some invariance strictly between the two forms, such as invariance under splitting a world into equiprobable parts, already forces fixed thresholds is open; the normalized measure passes that test in the case above, but I have no general result.

## 7. Objections

*The premium is not accuracy; it double-counts the conjunction.* The premium is alethic: it depends only on which believed propositions are true. It is not a disguised belief in the core, since in the four-world case it accrues on $\{w_1\}$, which is not believed. And it respects strict truth-directedness, the constraint Dorst places on holistic measures. The objector may insist that genuine accuracy is local, so that a belief's contribution depends only on its own truth. That is ME. With BI it yields separability, but ME alone leaves the term $\kappa$ of Theorem 2(a), and Section 4's dilemma applies. Excluding the premium on these grounds denies the holism §4.2 set out to accommodate. Someone may still find the premium unmotivated. Then the dispute is about the value of an error-free body of belief, which is my claim.

*Maximize Expected Accuracy gives only a necessary condition; perhaps neither optimum is rational.* Then, under the record measure, nothing would be rational at $C$ or $C'$, and Accuracy First would yield a norm that no state meets. Permission is the minimal premise needed for the framework to permit anything. Proposition 5 itself is stated as a necessary condition, and it is refuted only by a rational state; Permission supplies one.

*The result is negative only; Dorst's picture survives.* In part, yes. Belief remains a function of credence and contextual values, and Dorst's gloss of beliefs as "best guesses" (p. 196) fits core-relative Lockeanism at least as well as Lockeanism. What does not survive is the claim that accuracy forces the Lockean form. That matters to Holguín's argument, which targets that form: non-monotonicity tells against sophisticated Lockeanism, not against accuracy-first epistemology.

## 8. Conclusion

Dorst's Proposition 5 does not follow from truth-directed accuracy, Accuracy First, Maximize Expected Accuracy and Sequence-Irrelevance. A record premium satisfies all four, with Permission, and makes belief non-monotone in credence for every positive premium. Under marginal extensionality, the derivation is valid exactly when the score is separable. The premise that does yield fixed thresholds is refinement invariance: with truth-directedness and conservative increments it suffices, and without it the thresholds do not follow. In its restricted form, which the language-dependence objection motivates, it is compatible with the record measure; in its unrestricted form it amounts to the thesis that questions shape belief only through the values of individual propositions. A defense of Lockeanism from accuracy must therefore defend that thesis. It cannot take order-independent accuracy maximization to have settled the matter.

## Appendix: Proofs

*Proof of Theorem 1.* Write $e_p=C(p)D+F$; note $\tau>1/2$. At $C_c=(c,\frac{1-c}{2},\frac{1-c}{2})$ with $c>\tau$, the positive terms are $e_{\mathbf 1}$, $e_{12}=e_{13}$ and $e_W=T$; all others are negative (their credences are at most $1-c<1/2$). Then $\Phi(\mathbf 1)-\Phi(W)=e_{\mathbf 1}+2e_{12}-\beta(1-c)=c(2D+\beta)+T+2F-\beta$, positive iff $c>c_0$. Also $\Phi(\mathbf 1)-\Phi(13)=e_{\mathbf 1}+e_{12}-\beta\frac{1-c}{2}=\frac12(\Phi(\mathbf 1)-\Phi(W))+\frac12e_{\mathbf 1}>0$. Next, $\Phi(\mathbf 1)-\Phi(\{w_2\})=e_{\mathbf 1}+e_{12}+\beta\frac{3c-1}{2}>0$ and $\Phi(\mathbf 1)-\Phi(\{w_2,w_3\})=e_{\mathbf 1}+2e_{12}+\beta(2c-1)>0$; the cores $12$ and $\{w_3\}$ are symmetric; and $\Phi(\mathbf 1)-\Phi(\varnothing)=\beta c>0$. By Theorem 3 the unique optimum has core $\mathbf 1$ and contains $\mathbf 1$. At $(c',0,1-c')$ with $\tau<c'$: $e_{\mathbf 1}=e_{12}=c'D+F>0$ and $e_{13}=e_W=T$, so $\Phi(13)-\Phi(\mathbf 1)=\beta(1-c')-2e_{\mathbf 1}$, positive iff $c'<c_1$, while $\Phi(\varnothing)=\Phi(\mathbf 1)-\beta c'$. A state containing $\mathbf 1$ has core $\mathbf 1$ or $\varnothing$, so no optimum contains $\mathbf 1$; these strict inequalities persist for small $\varepsilon$. Finally, $c_1-c_0=T/(2D+\beta)$, $c_1-\tau=\beta T/(D(2D+\beta))$ and $1-c_1=2T/(2D+\beta)$ are positive. ∎

*Proof of Theorem 2.* Write $\Delta_p^B(w)=A(B\cup\{p\},w)-A(B,w)$ for $p\notin B$. Under ME, $\Delta_p^B=F_p^B+J_p^B[w\in p]$ for contingent $p$, and $\Delta_p^B$ is constant for $p\in\{\varnothing,W\}$; truth-directedness gives $F_p^B<0<F_p^B+J_p^B$. (i) Let $p,q\notin B$ be distinct, $q$ not the complement of $p$. Computing $A(B\cup\{p,q\},\cdot)-A(B,\cdot)$ in both orders gives $\Delta_p^B-\Delta_p^{B\cup\{q\}}=\Delta_q^B-\Delta_q^{B\cup\{p\}}$. The left side is $\alpha+(J_p^B-J_p^{B\cup\{q\}})[w\in p]$, the right side $\gamma+\delta[w\in q]$, or constant if $q$ is trivial. If $p,q$ are contingent, distinct and not complementary, $1,[w\in p],[w\in q]$ are linearly independent, so $J_p^{B\cup\{q\}}=J_p^B$; if $q$ is trivial, the same follows. (ii) Building any $B$ that contains neither $p$ nor its complement from $\varnothing$ one member at a time, (i) gives $J_p^B=J_p^\varnothing=:J_p$. (iii) For $B$ without complementary pairs, add its members in any order: $A(B,w)-A(\varnothing,w)=\kappa(B)+\sum_{p\in B}J_p[w\in p]$, where $\kappa(B)$, the sum of the $F$-terms and constant increments, is world-independent, and order-independent because the total is. The converse of (a) is immediate. For (b), BI says $F^{B}_p=\kappa(B\cup\{p\})-\kappa(B)$ is independent of $B$, which holds iff $\kappa(B)=\kappa(\varnothing)+\sum_{p\in B}f_p$; then $v_p=f_p+J_p[w\in p]$. Separable measures satisfy ME and BI trivially. The Corollary follows from (ii): $\tau^B_p=-F^B_p/J_p$. ∎

*Proof of Theorem 3.* $EA_C(B)=\sum_{p\in B}e_p+\beta C(\bigcap B)$. Every member of $B$ includes $\bigcap B$, so $EA_C(B)\le\Phi(\bigcap B)$, with equality iff $B$ contains every $p\supseteq\bigcap B$ with $e_p>0$ and no $p$ with $e_p<0$. Conversely, given $X$, let $B_X=\{p\supseteq X:e_p>0\}$ and $X'=\bigcap B_X\supseteq X$. The members of $B_X$ are exactly the positive-$e$ propositions including $X'$, so $EA_C(B_X)=\Phi(X')\ge\Phi(X)$, because $C(X')\ge C(X)$. Hence $\max EA=\max\Phi$, and the optimality condition follows. For uniform values, $p\subseteq q$ implies $C(q)\ge C(p)$, which gives upward closure. For (ii): if $u\in X^*$, $v\notin X^*$ and $C(v)>C(u)$, swapping $u$ for $v$ maps the supersets of $X^*$ bijectively onto those of the new core without lowering any credence, and raises the core's credence, contradicting maximality. For (iii): $B^*$ is upward closed with $\bigcap B^*=X^*$, so it is closed under conjunction iff $X^*\in B^*$, iff $C(X^*)>\tau$; it is consistent iff $X^*\ne\varnothing$. ∎

*Proof of Theorem 4.* Let $\pi$ send $p$ to $u$ and $W\setminus p$ to $v$. By RI the verdict on $p$ at $C$ equals the verdict on $\{u\}$ in the two-world problem at credence $c=C(p)$. There, truth-directedness excludes every state containing $\varnothing$ (dropping it raises the score at both worlds) and every state lacking $\{u,v\}$ (adding it raises the score at both), leaving $\{W\}$, $\{W,p\}$, $\{W,\bar p\}$ and $\{W,p,\bar p\}$. If $\{W,p,\bar p\}$ were optimal at $c$, dropping $\bar p$ or $p$ could not help: by CI, $(1-c)g\ge c\,l$ with $g<l$, so $c<1/2$, and $c\,g'\ge(1-c)\,l'$ with $g'<l'$, so $c>1/2$. Let $t$ and $f$ be the gain and loss from adding $p$ to $\{W\}$. Then $EA(\{W,p\})-EA(\{W\})=ct-(1-c)f$, positive iff $c>\tau_p:=f/(t+f)$, and $\tau_p>1/2$ by CI; similarly $\{W,\bar p\}$ beats $\{W\}$ only if $c<1/2$. So for $c>\tau_p$ the unique optimum is $\{W,p\}$, and for $c<\tau_p$ no optimum contains $p$. ∎
