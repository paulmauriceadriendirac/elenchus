## 1. Prior-art findings

### Zendejas Medina 2024 (full text)
- **Content.** Plan-based arguments need a bridge from "best plan" to what is done after learning. Auto-epistemic admissibility (plans conditioned on L_E) and epistemic admissibility (plans conditioned on E) coincide for transparent evidence (§4.2). Hild and Schoenfield implicitly adopt the first (§5). Externalists should adopt the second: one isn't bound by a plan whose condition one hasn't learned (§6.1). Conditional plans are ranked by conditional expected value (conditional expectation lemma, §7.2), which yields the New Accuracy Argument and a Practical Argument for conditionalization on opaque evidence (§8). Gallow is compared in §8.4.
- **Does it contain v3's results?** No. It has no totals or weightings, no learning mechanisms or CAR, no knife-edge, no neutrality, no No News, and no Reflection or biased-inquiry discussion (grep confirms: no "Salow", "reflection" or "biased" in the text).
- **Does it contain v3's moral?** Partly in form, not in content. ZM already says the threat is not opacity as such but a premise: auto-epistemic admissibility. v3's moral ("what threatens conditionalization is not opaque evidence but non-ignorable learning") was **overstated**. On ZM's view, even non-ignorable learning does not threaten conditionalization's rationality. v4 withdraws the unqualified moral. It now says that non-ignorability is what produces (i) the first-order disagreement between BCOND and META-COND and (ii) the failure of Reflection. Opacity alone produces only the disagreement about one's evidence.
- **Relation to v3's thesis: complementary, and in one respect subsuming.** It complements v3: ZM locates the threat in the bridge premise, v4 in the learning model, and each fixes something the other doesn't. ZM's fix works for every mechanism but leaves Reflection failures. Ignorability removes both first-order troubles but not META-COND's certainty about one's evidence. It subsumes in one respect: on the importance reading, MZ's total collapses to ZM's conditional evaluation (new Prop 3), so the evaluative core is his. ZM is not a rival about MZ's extensionality; he predates MZ and doesn't consider aggregates.
- **Strongest objection supplied** (v4 §8, Obj. 1): plan coherence makes ignorability idle. The reply concedes that ignorability is not necessary for vindicating conditionalization if epistemic admissibility is granted. It denies idleness on three grounds: the MZ analysis; Reflection depends on the mechanism; and ZM's condition is an argument that the manner of learning may not be used, not that it is uninformative.

### Meehan & Zhang n. 2 (287–288)
They describe ZM's argument as "based on conditional expected accuracy rather than unconditional expected accuracy" and leave a puzzle about unconditional versus conditional plans in nontransparent problems. v4's Prop 3 answers this in their terms: a total respects conditional verdicts iff it is extensional (CAR).

### Das 2019 (AAM read)
- **Content.** In his framework, Theorem 1 says meta-conditionalizing plans uniquely maximize expected accuracy relative to the ur-prior. The Lemma says they coincide with conditionalizing plans iff evidence is partitional. Corollary 2 states the tension with externalism. In §5.3 he suggests characterizing when conditionalizing on a class of propositions maximizes expected accuracy.
- **Prior art?** No CAR, no stochastic mechanisms, no totals.
- **Use in v4.** Prop 7(a), which restates Grünwald–Halpern, answers his §5.3 question for first-order propositions. It is paraphrased, not quoted, because the AAM wording may differ from print.

### Schultheis 2025 (read)
- **Content.** She names the "Bayesian Dilemma". She argues the dilemma is genuine when rules are evaluated by the accuracy of *adopting* them (credal selection and guess functions), via the continuity argument.
- **Prior art?** No CAR, totals or Reflection.
- **Use in v4.** Cited only for the name of the dilemma. Her argument is not engaged in detail; this is a weak point.

### Gallow 2021 (abstract + ZM §8.4)
- **What I can attribute.** The abstract says that if externalism is correct, conditionalization and Reflection are inconsistent; that he recommends rejecting conditionalization; and that he defends a new rule via the expected accuracy of learning dispositions. ZM §8.4 notes that the rule collapses into a-e conditionalization for ideal agents who know they will follow their plan.
- **Use in v4.** Prop 7(b) locates his conflict exactly: it arises for some prior iff learning is non-ignorable. Whether the paper itself discusses a condition like this is unknown, since only the abstract was read. **Risk**: if it does, the "I have not found (b) stated" sentence needs revisiting. A fetch request is recommended.

### Salow 2018 (abstract only)
Only the abstract claims are attributed: that intentionally biased inquiry is impossible, and that this supports an access principle. v4 adds its own point: under ignorable but opaque learning, access fails yet a conditionalizer can't expect her credence to move on balance. It explicitly does not claim this blocks his argument.

### Carr 2021, Bronfman 2014: unread (see above)
Carr is attributed only via MZ 330 n. 35, as in v3. Bronfman is omitted.

### CAR literature (Heitjan & Rubin; Grünwald & Halpern; Gill & Grünwald)
Unchanged from v3 (G&G full text read on arXiv; H&R and G&H abstracts read). Prop 7(a) is credited to G&H. The "if" half of Prop 7(b) follows easily from it. The converse half (Reflection for every prior ⇒ CAR, by partial fractions) I have not found stated, and v4 says so. It is not claimed as surely new.

## 2. What changed and why
- **Thesis repositioned.** v3's thesis is kept: extensionality = CAR; robust only with transparent problems counted; knife-edge plus neutrality otherwise. v4 adds a bridge to ZM and splits the dialectic into an importance reading and a frequency reading.
- **New Prop 3 (merges v3 Prop 6).** For regular π: extensional ⇔ the total respects ZM's conditional verdicts (Pareto over CEA_p); with nonzero λ_p, also ⇔ the induced scores are strictly proper. This answers MZ n. 2. It also shows that on the importance reading the total argument shares ZM's evaluative core, differing only in the bridge (global evidential constancy vs epistemic admissibility). Checked in check.py §12.
- **New Prop 7 (replaces v3 §6's informal agent-level claim).** (a) BCOND and META-COND agree on S iff CAR (G&H restated; answers Das §5.3). (b) Under CAR, BCOND satisfies Reflection for every proposition about the enlarged space; conversely, Reflection about S for every prior forces CAR (proof by partial fractions; solution space checked numerically for n=3,4). (c) Under CAR, the agent is uncertain of her evidence. Checked in check.py §§13–14, including Here's a Hand (expected posterior in HAND 1/2 vs prior 1/3) and biased-coin Cyclic.
- **Moral narrowed and corrected.** Under ignorable learning, the BCOND/META-COND disagreement concerns only what one has learned. Non-ignorable learning brings in disagreement about the world and Reflection failure. ZM's remedy and ignorability are complementary. An externalist who adopts ZM's remedy keeps conditionalization at the price of Reflection in the paradigm (non-ignorable) cases.
- **Dropped.**
  - v3 Prop 3's ranking claim (no transparent weighting ranks like λ°) and its Bell formula. The checks remain in check.py §4; the essay keeps only that λ° is neutral.
  - v3 Objection 1 ("every weighting is a learning hypothesis"), folded into §6.
  - Footnotes 1–2 (granularity; the naive-space caveat, now in the body via Prop 7).
  - Levinstein 2019 (no longer needed).
- **Reordered.**
  - §4 (totals and conditional verdicts, Prop 3)
  - §5 (proposition-attached importance, Prop 4 = v3 Prop 7)
  - §6 (frequency reading: knife-edge Prop 5 = v3 Prop 4, neutrality Prop 6 = v3 Prop 5)

## 3. v4 thesis (one sentence)
Meehan and Zhang's extensionality is coarsening at random on the model of learning implicit in their weighting. It holds exactly when their total respects Zendejas Medina's conditional verdicts. So the total argument either shares his evaluative core (importance reading, forced by propriety) or rests on a knife-edge held by neutrality (frequency reading). More generally, opaque evidence costs conditionalization its first-order agreement with the rival rule and its satisfaction of Reflection exactly when learning is non-ignorable, a cost that plan coherence does not remove.

## 4. Increment
- **Over MZ:**
  - weightings = coarsening mechanisms, and extensionality = CAR
  - uniform total = random yes/no questions
  - extensionality ⇔ respect for conditional verdicts (answers their n. 2 puzzle and links their argument to ZM's)
  - the knife-edge
  - neutrality and propriety as grounds, each with a stated cost
  - the No News theorem for proposition-attached importance
  - the failure of maximin, minimax regret and dominance
- **Over ZM:**
  - the exact relation between his conditional evaluation and MZ's unconditional totals
  - the decomposition: under ignorable learning the dispute is purely about one's evidence; non-ignorable learning brings first-order disagreement and Reflection failure
  - the observation that his remedy leaves Reflection failures exactly in non-ignorable cases
- **Over the CAR literature:** no new statistics, apart from possibly the converse half of Prop 7(b). The contribution is the transfer and its consequences for the externalism debate (Gallow's conflict located; Das's question answered for first-order propositions).

## 5. Three weakest points
1. **Formal modesty and possible prior art for Prop 7(b).** Props 1, 3–6 are short consequences of MZ's lemmas, the budget identity and propriety. Prop 7(a) is G&H. The Reflection characterization might exist in the statistics (martingale under CAR) or in Gallow 2021's full text, which I did not read. A fetch request for Gallow 2021 is advisable before final submission.
2. **Thin engagement with Gallow, Salow, Schultheis, Carr and Bronfman.** Gallow and Salow are attributed from abstracts only. Schultheis is cited only for the dilemma's name, and her continuity argument (adopting vs following) is not assessed. Under ignorable learning her setting might still produce first-order differences through fallible guesses; that is not examined. Carr is via MZ's report; Bronfman is omitted.
3. **Objection 1's reply is partial by design.** If epistemic admissibility is accepted and Reflection is not treated as a norm, ignorability matters only to the analysis of MZ. The essay concedes this and does not argue that Reflection outranks plan coherence. The Here's a Hand model adds an assumption (without the experience, one learns that one lacks it) that ZM's text does not state explicitly.
