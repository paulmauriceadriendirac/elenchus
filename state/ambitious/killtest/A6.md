**Notation.** ⪰ means "at least as normal as". ≫ means "sufficiently more normal than". For evidence Y and a situation x ∈ Y:
- K(x) is the knowledge set ("ken");
- RB(Y) is the belief set;
- C(x) is the closure of {x} under "not ≫" steps: v ∈ C(x) iff there is a chain x = c0, …, ck = v in Y such that no c_i ≫ c_{i+1}.

"Gapped" means that every member of the ken is ≫ every evidential possibility outside it.

## 1. Steelman

**A6-01.**
1. Knowledge-first decision theory (KDT: expected utility conditioned on what one knows) is the most direct way to give knowledge a practical role. Goodman–Salow normality structures are the most developed formal theory of inductive knowledge. Putting the two together lets one ask exactly when acting on knowledge respects "look before you leap".
2. The answer is sharp. In statist structures, KDT gives every free binary question non-negative value only if the ken is ≫-gapped. Any ken that obeys INDUCTIVE KNOWLEDGE and contains belief is gapped only where it coincides with C(x), which is the ω-closure of I-knowledge. Globally, only ranked (modular) ≫ qualifies.
3. Goodman and Salow argue for graded, non-modular normality in measurement and chancy-sequence cases. So KDT built on their theory makes some free question harmful at every situation with inductive knowledge. This turns Salow's "relatively rare" failures of Preservation into a pervasive cost, and gives the KK/ω debate a decision-theoretic stake.

**A6-02.**
1. Goodman and Salow's own memoryless cases (Flipping for All Heads, Decay, Bus Driver) let an agent know q and p while knowing that discovering q will defeat p. Salow 2025 (n. 8) sets these cases aside as "highly unusual". In exactly these cases the Strong Dominance form of the dogmatism argument goes through, though Salow dismissed it (p. 25) on the ground that the belief combination it needs is irrational.
2. Salow's Conditional Dominance escapes only if conditional belief is read as belief after discovery, a reading BRN App. D.3 rejects for non-statist models. On BRN's own notion, Conditional Dominance also recommends not watching.
3. So inside the Goodman–Salow programme something must give: unrestricted Strong Dominance, the □− failures, or BRN's notion of conditional belief.

**A6-03.**
1. In BRN's simple probability structures, learning what one already believes can create new beliefs (□+ fails) but cannot destroy them (□− holds).
2. Expected utility conditioned on belief therefore disvalues some free confirmations of what one believes. Expected utility conditioned on K-normality knowledge never disvalues confirming what one knows, because factive knowledge never excludes the actual state.
3. Good's theorem thus discriminates knowledge from belief as the outright attitude fit to enter expected-utility reasoning.

**A6-04.**
1. Carter and Hawthorne's reply to Ex Ante Dogmatism needs, at a single situation, both knowledge that further evidence could defeat and a KK failure that further evidence could repair.
2. In statist, comparable normality structures these never co-occur on any of the three completions. K-normality allows defeat but validates KK. The MARGINS completions allow KK failures but validate NO DEFEAT.
3. So within that framework the puzzle arises only where the reply is unavailable, and the reply is available only where the puzzle does not arise.

**A6-05.**
1. Pragmatic encroachment is the obvious way for a knowledge-first decision theorist to keep both Good's theorem and inductive knowledge.
2. A6-01's lemma prices this exactly. On graded normality, an encroachment that restores Good must cut knowledge down to a ≫-gapped set (the evidence) whenever a free coarse-grained check is available.
3. Such checks are almost always available, so the price is total, not merely uncontained as Salow 2025 §6 says.

## 2. Objections

### 2.1 A6-01 (Good's theorem and the gap condition)

**What survives scrutiny (checked by hand).**
- **Lemma A** (relative defeat implies harm) holds under full-support priors. Its proof is the bet that pays 1 on K(x) and −L elsewhere.
- **Lemma B** holds. Let v ∉ K(x), let m ∈ K(x) with not m ≫ v, and let p = {u ∈ Y : not u ≫ v}. Nothing in Y ∩ p is ≫ v, so v ∈ RB(Y ∩ p) ⊆ the post-discovery ken at m.
- **The bridge.** A gapped M with x ∈ M is closed under "not ≫" steps, so M ⊇ C(x). Any ken that obeys INDUCTIVE KNOWLEDGE satisfies K(x) ⊆ {v : not x ≫ v} ⊆ C(x). Hence a Good-respecting ken of this kind equals C(x).
- **Lemma C.** "Not ≫" is total, because ≫ is asymmetric, so the reachability relation is a total preorder. The sets C(·) are therefore reflexive, transitive and nested. Under statism they only shrink on discovery. Geanakoplos's sufficient condition then applies.
- **The global I-equivalence** is negative transitivity of ≫. This is the Lehmann–Magidor condition that BRN App. D.2 and Prop. 24 transpose to normality structures.

**O1.1. Already known / scope of target: the conditional repair. Severity: serious, potentially fatal to the card's dilemma.**
- *Objection.* KDT entails Salow's Weak Knowledge Dominance* ("If you know that B's consequences would be at least as good as A's, and for all you know they would be better, you should choose B over A"). Salow says this principle "can support dogmatism even given Preservation" and rejects it in favour of Conditional Knowledge Dominance ("If you know that B's consequences would be better than A's if they're not equally good, you should choose B over A") (Salow 2025, p. 33). The card attacks a rule that the leading dissolution has already discarded. Its dilemma ("ranked normality or ω-knowledge") also ignores the obvious third option: an expected-utility analogue of Conditional Knowledge Dominance.
- *Hand check.* Take Lemma B's canonical case under statism. Evaluate "look" against "act now" by expected utility conditional on BRN conditional knowledge (Def. D.2), given D, the set of states where the two plans differ. With D = p, the conditional ken contains RB(Y ∩ p), which contains v, and there the "now" bet loses L. So the conditional rule prefers looking, and the canonical counterexample is defused.
- *Best reply.*
  - (a) The KDT actually defended in the literature (Hawthorne–Stanley; Hong's normality-based KDT) is unconditional.
  - (b) A conditional expected-utility rule is a new decision theory. Its coherence (acyclicity, menu-independence, Good) is unproved.
  - (c) In the non-statist □− cases (A6-02), the conditional rule with BRN's notion of conditional belief still recommends not watching.
- *Quality.* Partial. Point (a) holds, but on its own it makes the result an objection to a view Salow already rejects. Point (c) is promising but is not yet a theorem for the expected-utility version.
- *Repair.* Make the conditional repair a lemma of the essay, as set out in §5, item 2.

**O1.2. Trivial once made precise / already known. Severity: serious.**
- *Objection.* The three lemmas are already in print:
  - Lemma A is the bet construction attributed to Das 2023 n. 12 (not checked by me).
  - Lemma C is Geanakoplos (reflexive, transitive and nested information has non-negative value).
  - The modularity equivalence is Lehmann–Magidor, already transposed by BRN (Prop. 24, App. D.2).

  Dorst et al. 2021 characterize, for possibly non-introspective guides, when one should prefer "to make any decision using their opinions". Their abstract equates this with expecting the guide's estimates to be more accurate. That is the general theory of which the gap condition is a special case.
- *Best reply.* No text I read connects normality kens to the value of evidence: not BRN (read in full; it has no decision-theoretic content), not Salow 2025, not Goodman and Salow 2023, not Ahmed and Salow 2019, not Carter and Hawthorne 2024. Three things are new:
  - the bridge lemma (any ken that obeys INDUCTIVE KNOWLEDGE and contains belief is Good-respecting only if it equals C(x));
  - its global form;
  - the corollary that failures are ubiquitous on graded normality. Salow calls these failures "relatively rare counterexamples to Preservation" (p. 36).
- *Quality.* Partial. This is enough for a modest formal contribution if the known pieces are credited and the increment is stated exactly.
- *Repair.* Present the result as three known pieces plus one new bridge lemma, and put the weight on the bridge and its consequences.

**O1.3. Equivocation: "value of evidence" versus self-trust. Severity: serious.**
- *Objection.*
  - From the definitions, under I-normality the ken is gapped at x iff KK holds at x.
  - Where KK fails, the KDT value of the null question (learn nothing) is already negative. At any s ∈ K(x) with K(s) ⊄ K(x), the Lemma A bet is declined although no evidence arrives.
  - MARGINS, COMPARABILITY and STATISM together "entail NO DEFEAT" (Goodman and Salow 2023, p. 128). So for MARGINS theorists the card's "Good failure" is a fact about KDT without KK disvaluing deferral to itself, not a fact about evidence.
  - The card's gloss on Bjorn ("the answer 'no' widens his knowledge beyond what he knew") is false for I- and KM-normality.
  - The scripts count the trivial experiment: `check1.binary_experiments` returns p = Y. So some of the reported I-normality failures may be failures on the null question (the I_global count in check1; the "300 situations" in check6, part 2).
- *Best reply.* Lemma B's question is non-trivial, so the necessity direction stands for informative questions. The null-question failure is itself a cost for KDT without KK.
- *Quality.* Partial.

  - (i) Failures driven by defeat: K-normality. This is genuine evidence-avoidance, the dogmatism puzzle proper.
  - (ii) Failures driven by introspection: I- and KM-normality. These are present even for null questions. Credit known kin: Williamson 2000, pp. 230–7, and Ahmed and Salow 2019 n. 7 on non-partitional information.

**O1.4. A premise the target audience rejects: statism plus arbitrary discoverable questions. Severity: moderate.**
- *Objection.* BRN say "STATISM is the exception rather than the rule" (§7). It holds only when the possible bodies of evidence are circumscribed. App. A argues for NESTEDNESS (states settle evidential trajectories) and calls the §7 model with an added discovery "unrealistic" (n. 46). On their probabilistic account, adding Lemma B's question to Bjorn's evidence space may break statism.
- *Best reply (my derivation).* Lemma B does not need statism in simple probability structures (BRN Def. 6.2).
  1. Suppose p = {u ∈ E : not u ≫_E v} is a possible body of evidence.
  2. By chaining, the most probable member m0 of p satisfies {s : m0 ⪰ s} = E ∩ p.
  3. So "not m0 ≫_E v" says that Pr_{E∩p}(states more probable than v) < t.
  4. By BRN Prop. 5, this puts v in B(E∩p).

  The negative half therefore survives in the probabilistic models. Only the positive ω-half needs statism, and on graded evidence that half is trivial anyway.
- *Quality.* Convincing for the negative half, once written up.
- *Repair.*
  - Prove this claim in the essay.
  - Model the option to look with states that settle evidential trajectories (BRN App. A).
  - Say which ℰ is assumed.

### 2.3 A6-03 (factivity as a shield)

**O3.1. Straw target / significance. Severity: serious.**
No one defends expected utility conditioned on outright belief. Salow 2025's belief-based principles are qualitative, and the card itself concedes that Conditional Dominance registers nothing in □+ cases.

**O3.2. Trivial once made precise / already known. Severity: serious.**
- The belief–knowledge contrast is Geanakoplos's reflexivity condition. Treating a non-factive state as certain ("taking a signal at face-value", in Ahmed and Salow's 2019 n. 7 summary of Geanakoplos) can make information harmful. A reflexive, transitive and nested state cannot.
- Under statism, FRONTLOADING holds, which I checked from the definitions. So belief-guided Good fails exactly where ♦− fails, and the gain-side contrast exists only in non-statist models.

**O3.3. Model. Severity: moderate.**
- The key case is BRN's §7 model with E′ added. BRN App. A n. 46 calls this model "unrealistic" because it violates NESTEDNESS once flip-by-flip evidence is in ℰ.
- The script uses only {S, E′, S∖E′}, which is nested, so the toy result stands. The trajectory-splitting model would need rechecking.
- The numbers verify by hand: B(S) = {1..5}, B(E′) = {1..4}, and the belief-guided value of the information is −0.0023.

**O3.4. Overclaim. Severity: serious for the stated thesis.**
"Good discriminates knowledge-first from belief-first formulations" is contradicted by A6-01: knowledge-guided expected utility fails Good pervasively on graded normality. The discrimination holds only for confirmations of what is already known.

**Verdict on A6-03.** KILL (confidence 0.75). Salvage at most a footnote.

### 2.4 A6-04 (the Carter–Hawthorne reply is idle)

**O4.3. P5 is trivial under statism.** ω-I closures shrink on discovery by definition of reachability.

**Fidelity check.** The scripts encode defeat and KK failure correctly, and the counts are plausible. The result follows from the published theorems anyway.

**Verdict on A6-04.** KILL (confidence 0.80). At most a footnote: "in statist comparable normality models the derivative-norm reply has no work to do".

### 2.5 A6-05 (encroachment cannot rescue KDT)

**O5.1. Already known. Severity: serious.**
- Salow 2025 n. 3 reports Hawthorne 2004 p. 181 as suggesting that, "given Good's (1967) result, this naturally falls out of the proposal—developed by Fantl and McGrath (2002) and Weatherson (2005)—that pragmatic encroachment occurs whenever the difference between maximal and actual probability affects your preferences over available options". The options include the option of looking.
- Salow §6 already argues that such encroachment cannot be contained.
- Comesaña ("A note on knowledge-first decision theory and practical adequacy", in *Pragmatic Encroachment in Epistemology*, pp. 206–211; abstract only) argues that with inductive knowledge KDT rules out states one should not rule out. He proposes that a proposition cannot be known unless it is practically adequate.

**O5.2. Equivocation. Severity: moderate.**
For I- and KM-normality the failure is present even for null questions (O1.3). So availability of free re-checking is not the trigger there: encroachment would have to strip non-gapped knowledge always. Only for K-normality is availability the trigger.

**O5.3. A premise encroachers reject. Severity: moderate.**
Encroachers restrict encroachment to live or salient options.

**Verdict on A6-05.** KILL as a standalone essay (confidence 0.75). Keep one paragraph in the merged essay that engages Hawthorne 2004 (via Salow n. 3) and Comesaña.

## 6. The essay to write

**Card.** A merger. A6-01 is the spine. A6-02 is the section that answers the conditional-repair objection. A6-05 contributes one paragraph. A6-03 and A6-04 are dropped.

**Calibrated thesis, to be fixed after Repair 2.**
- *Main claim.* In Goodman–Salow normality models, expected utility conditioned on what one knows respects Good's theorem at a situation only if one's knowledge already contains every possibility reachable by steps to "not sufficiently less normal" possibilities. So on graded normality (measurement, chancy futures), knowledge-based decision theory makes some free question harmful at every situation with inductive knowledge:
  - by defeat, under K-normality;
  - by failures of KK, under the MARGINS completions.
- *The conditional repair.* The Salow-style repair, which conditions on what one would know if the options differed, [if Repair 2 confirms this: restores Good when conditional knowledge tracks post-discovery knowledge (statist models), but] fails in Goodman and Salow's own non-statist memoryless cases.
- *What it does not claim:*
  - that knowledge has no practical role;
  - that Good's theorem is sacrosanct;
  - anything about Goldstein's own ω-models;
  - anything about non-statist structures beyond the cases analysed;
  - that knowledge of discrete hypotheses after decisive evidence (gapped cases such as Heading for Heads) is unfit to guide choice.
- *Legible upshot for non-specialists.* Knowledge of laws after decisive evidence can enter expected-utility reasoning without making free checks harmful. Knowledge of magnitudes and chancy futures cannot.

**Closest prior work and the exact increment.**
- **Salow 2025.**
  - What he showed: Weak Dominance licenses dogmatism exactly where Preservation fails; Weak Knowledge Dominance* is rejected; Conditional (Knowledge) Dominance is proposed; failures of Preservation are "relatively rare"; the □− cases are set aside (n. 8).
  - Increment: an expected-utility version with an exact boundary; ubiquity on graded normality; the conditional repair tested in the cases he set aside.
- **BRN Props. 1–2 and 24, App. D.2–D.3.**
  - What they showed: ♦− ⟺ modularity under statism (via Lehmann–Magidor); conditional belief comes apart from post-discovery belief without statism.
  - Increment: the decision-theoretic counterpart (Good for KDT ⟺ gap), and the practical consequence of the D.3 split.
- **Geanakoplos 1989, Dorst et al. 2021, Das 2023.**
  - What they showed: general conditions on information or guides under which value is non-negative.
  - Increment: the normality-theoretic condition under which knowledge kens meet those conditions (the bridge lemma).
- **Hong 2024, 2025, 2026.**
  - What they did: normality-based KDT against fanaticism, then two arguments that normality knowledge is not action-guiding (unread).
  - Increment: a third, exact argument through Good's theorem. To be fixed after reading.
- **Hawthorne 2004 (via Salow n. 3) and Comesaña 2018/19.**
  - What they showed: encroachment and practical-adequacy constraints as KDT repairs.
  - Increment: a Good-restoring constraint must shrink knowledge to C(x), which on graded normality is the evidence.

**The three objections the essay must answer.**
1. **The conditional repair (Salow).** Show by proof and exhaustive check what the expected-utility version of Conditional Knowledge Dominance does under statism, and that it fails in the BRN §10 *de se* model. Defend the badness of the dogmatic verdict with Bus Driver.
2. **"Trivial and already known" (value-of-information theorists).** Credit Das, Geanakoplos and Lehmann–Magidor as BRN transpose them, and Dorst et al. Isolate the bridge lemma as the increment. Separate failures driven by defeat from failures driven by self-trust, and do not count the null question as evidence.
3. **"Wrong target and model class, and Hong already said it" (normality theorists and KDT theorists).**
   - Name the target: Hawthorne–Stanley/Hong-style KDT combined with the leading theory of inductive knowledge.
   - Show that Lemma B holds in the probabilistic, non-statist models.
   - Model the option to look in nested, trajectory-settling structures.
   - Give a moderate-stakes instance.
   - State exactly what goes beyond Hong 2026's two arguments.
