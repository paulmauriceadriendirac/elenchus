## 2. A5-01: Truth cannot be the aim of knowledge-aimed belief (split-invariance impossibility)

### Steelman
Closed belief rules come in two kinds:
- Guess-like rules (expected answer-value, as in Dorst & Mandelkern and in Holguín's norms) pick the believed answer by weighing each rejected cell.
- Knowledge-aimed rules look only at the total probability excluded, never at how it is split among the rejected answers. The two examples are the probabilist normality rule (believe the smallest top-set of answers with probability at least t; G&S 2023 p. 122: belief "is characterized by the highest posterior density region for the given threshold") and Leitgeb's strongest-stable-set rule.

The card proves that this split-invariance is incompatible with choosing the believed answer by maximising the expected value of any answer-dependent, truth-directed, informativity-respecting value. The proof covers any value that is a continuous, increasing function of the answer's probability, so it also covers risk-weighted evaluation. The premises are minimal anti-scepticism and caution. So whatever grounds knowledge-aimed belief, it is not the expected value of truth.

### Formal audit (by hand)
1. **The proof is valid.** Take the path P_x = (x, (1−x)/2, (1−x)/2) and let x* be where outright belief in a begins.
   - For x > x*, S moves all residual mass to b, so EU({a}) at x is at least T_ab (the value of a certain "a or b"). By continuity this holds at x*.
   - Just below x*, filtering leaves {a,b}, {a,c} or W. Each gives a contradiction. "a or b" at the even split is worth strictly less than T_ab. W ties only if T_W ≥ T_ab, which informativity rules out.
   - The card's quantitative check fits this: from the two critical points, the steepness needed is T_a − F_a ≥ (1−t)/(4ε). At t = 0.9 and ε = 10⁻⁴ that bound is 250; the card's z3 value is 2001/8 = 250.125.
2. **The card's G&S rule is correctly derived.** On G&S's §5 analysis, w is sufficiently more normal than v iff P(more normal than v | no more normal than w) is high. That ratio is largest when w is the most normal answer. So v is excluded iff P(answers strictly more probable than v) ≥ t, which is the card's rule (ties included together).
3. **C is stronger than needed, and is misapplied to Leitgeb.**
   - (P2) says Leitgeb's view "(r = 1/2 strictly)" accepts C. It does not: the strongest P-stable^{1/2} set is {a} exactly when P(a) > 1/2, so that rule never withholds belief in a cell above 1/2. It satisfies C only if a contextual Lockean threshold s > 1/2 is added. The card's own check (check_split_z3.py, `stable_strongest`) uses the rule without s.
   - The proof needs only x* ≥ 1/2, because the S-step needs only 1 − x < x for x > x*. So replace C with **C⁻: never believe outright a cell whose credence is below 1/2.**
   - Under C⁻ the theorem covers Leitgeb's rule. Just below 1/2 on the even path that rule believes W, and a tie between W and {a} contradicts informativity.
4. **Filtering (F) is derivable on three cells once AS and C⁻ hold for every cell. This is new and strengthens the result.**
   - Fix a cell i. Some answer G is chosen at points arbitrarily close below x*_i on its even path. G cannot be:
     - {i,j}: excluded by the proof;
     - W: excluded by informativity;
     - a singleton {j}: EU of {j} is strictly increasing in P(j), so just above x*_i, concentrating the residual on j makes {j} beat {i}, against S.
   - So G must be the complement {j,k}. Then (1−x*)T_jk + x*F_jk ≥ EU_i(x*) ≥ max(T_ij, T_ik). Since the left side is below T_jk, this gives T_jk > max(T_ij, T_ik).
   - Imposing this for all three cells is cyclic and impossible (T_bc > T_ac > T_bc).
   - **Strengthened theorem.** On a three-cell question, no rule that maximises expected answer-value is split-invariant, anti-sceptical and "majority-cautious" (C⁻), for every cell. Truth-directed and informative values are assumed throughout. Filtering is not needed.
5. **Minimality.**
   - Under the card's single-cell AS and C, F is independent; the card supplies no model for that, so here is one. The values are:

     | Answer | T (true) | F (false) |
     |---|---|---|
     | {a} | 1 | −1 |
     | {b}, {c} | 1 | −10 |
     | {a,b}, {a,c} | 0.7 | −10 |
     | {b,c} | 8 | 0 |
     | W | 0.5 | — |

     The EU rule believes {a} iff P(a) > 0.9 and otherwise believes {b,c}. It satisfies S: belief in {a} depends only on P(a), and S is vacuous for two-cell beliefs on three cells. It also satisfies EU (truth-directed, informative), AS for a, and C and C⁻ for a. It violates F.
   - Under all-cell AS and C⁻, the minimal set is {S, EU (truth-directed, informative), AS, C⁻}. Independence models:
     - drop S: size-only values T = (1, 0.6, 0.5), F = 0;
     - drop EU: the G&S rule with t ∈ (1/2, 1);
     - drop AS: the card's top-two-or-nothing rule;
     - drop C⁻: size-only values T = (1, 0.5, 0.4), F = 0, which believes the top cell above 0.4;
     - drop truth-directedness or informativity: the card's models.

     These need re-running under all-cell AS.
6. **Convexity versus split-invariance: the card's best available increment, still unverified.**
   - Snippets of Wang 2025's abstract report that EU binarisation implies "convexity norms", which Leitgeb's stability theory and Lin–Kelly's tracking theory violate.
   - By hand, Leitgeb's strongest-stable-set rule has non-convex decision regions on open sets. On four cells, (0.41, 0.29, 0.20, 0.10) and (0.29, 0.41, 0.20, 0.10) both yield {a,b,c}, but their midpoint (0.35, 0.35, 0.20, 0.10) yields {a,b}.
   - The G&S coverage rule's regions are convex polytopes at generic credences: for each top-set A, all cells of A exceed all outside cells, P(A) ≥ t, and P(A∖{v}) < t for every v in A. I checked this by hand on 3 and 4 cells. Convexity fails only on measure-zero tie sets, because of the tie rule.
   - If Wang's norms are convexity norms, split-invariance is a further EU-necessary condition that catches a closed, filtering, convexity-respecting rule. That would be a real increment. It depends on Wang's text.

### Objections
**O1. Already known (in part). Serious.**
- *Objection:* "This is a known fact in philosophical dress. Bayes rules for set estimation under a fixed loss are cell-level cutoff rules. For example, with value 'truth − k·size' you include a cell iff its probability exceeds k. Fixed-coverage credible sets are not Bayes rules under any fixed linear loss. And Wang 2025 (as reported) already shows that Leitgeb's stability theory fails the norms EU-maximisation imposes. So 'stability cannot be accuracy-vindicated' is published."
- *Best reply:* The increment is threefold:
  - generality: any truth-valued answer value, including rank-dependent risk weighting;
  - a single named axiom with a minimal inconsistency, strengthened per audit point 4;
  - the probabilist normality rule, which appears to pass convexity yet fails split-invariance (audit point 6).
- *How good:* partial, pending Wang.
- *Repair:* Read Wang 2025 and Wang & Kim 2024. State the increment as "convexity-respecting but not EU-representable". Machine-check G&S regions against Wang's norms. Locate a citable statistics source for the fixed-coverage point, or state it impersonally.

**O2. Scope; false as stated. Serious.**
- *Objection:* The thesis claims "the accuracy-first bridge principles are all split-sensitive". The paradigm accuracy-first bridge principle, Dorst's and Easwaran's Lockeanism, is split-invariant: belief in a depends only on P(a). It escapes the theorem only because Lockean belief is not closed and has no strongest believed answer. The theorem presupposes closure, since it evaluates the believed answer as a whole. So the card's "it is not closure" is misleading.
- *Best reply:* Restate the result as a trilemma: closure; split-invariance with caution; truth-valued expected-value grounding — at most two can hold together.
  - Lockeans drop closure.
  - Guess theorists drop split-invariance.
  - Normality and stability theorists drop the grounding.
- *How good:* convincing once restated.
- *Repair:* Rewrite the thesis and §(ii) of "why it matters" accordingly.

**O3. Significance. Serious.**
- *Objection:* Nobody holds the inconsistent triad.
  - Goodman and Salow say their "belief" follows the tradition of belief aiming at knowledge, and that English "believe" "expresses something considerably weaker" (2023, n. 21). They never sought an expected-accuracy grounding.
  - Dorst and Holguín reject knowledge-aimed belief.
  - So the theorem costs no one. It formalises a distinction already drawn ("thinking" versus "being sure").
- *Best reply:* It turns an informal contrast into a theorem with a precise culprit. It blocks a natural project: an accuracy-first vindication of stability or normality belief, attractive to anyone who wants closure and accuracy-first foundations together. And it gives the thinking/being-sure dualism a value-theoretic basis.
- *How good:* partial. A demarcation result earns a section, not a whole essay.
- *Repair:* Present it as demarcation, not refutation. Pair it with a result that refutes a published claim (A5-03).

**O4. Scope of the value premise. Moderate.**
- *Objection:* EU here values only the strongest believed answer. State-level accuracy measures value the whole belief state; A5-03's perfect-record measure is an example. Under them, a state whose strongest belief is {a} can contain {a,b} but not {a,c}. Its expected value then depends on the split, and the proof's step "the value of believing a depends only on P(a)" fails.
- *Best reply:* On three cells, and whenever the optimal state is the full filter above its core, alethic state-level values reduce to answer-level ones. The perfect-record rule also violates S outright. With T = 1, F = 2, β = 3 (computed by hand):
  - at (0.7, 0.125, 0.175) its unique optimum believes {1} (4.30 against 4.25);
  - at (0.7, 0, 0.3) it believes only {1,3} (5.00 against 4.30).
- *How good:* partial.
- *Repair:* Either prove the no-go for alethic state-level measures under a stated truth-directedness condition, or restrict the claim to answer-level values and label the general case a conjecture.

**O5. Unnecessary premise and misattribution (C and Leitgeb). Moderate.** See audit point 3.
- *Repair:* Replace C with C⁻. Remove "Leitgeb's r = 1/2 strictly accepts C". The repaired theorem then covers Leitgeb's rule without a Lockean threshold.
- *How good:* convincing, and the theorem gets stronger.

**O6. Minimality incomplete. Moderate.** QUALITY §1.2 requires minimality, and F had no independence model.
- *Repair:* See audit points 4–5: either add the F-free model, or, better, adopt the stronger premise set {S, EU, AS, C⁻}, from which F follows.

**O7. Question-begging value premise. Moderate.**
- *Objection:* If an answer's value may depend on whether it is known at w, a knowledge-valued utility makes the normality rule optimal trivially. The theorem then shows only that knowledge-aimed belief is not truth-aimed, which knowledge-firsters already assert.
- *Best reply:* That is the intended reading: veritism against knowledge-centred value. The theorem makes precise a slogan that was previously only asserted.
- *How good:* convincing if the thesis is calibrated. It also reinforces O3.

**O8. Oversold "test for every camp". Moderate.**
- *Objection:* The case (0.91, 0.09, 0) against (0.91, 0.045, 0.045) predicts only that EU rules switch later when the residual is concentrated, not where. "Being sure" intuitions are likely flat (not sure) in both. And the alternative-outcomes effect is already used for "think" by Dorst (Cases 1–2, explained by context-shifted values), following Windschitl and Wells.
- *Repair:* Present the case as a structural prediction, and drop the claim of new testability.

**O9. Unverified anchors. Moderate.** Several load-bearing claims rest on texts the card read only in abstract, or that I could not check:

The 2023 paper says G&S will "mostly adopt" a non-reductive approach, considering a probabilistic analysis in §5 (p. 98), and that they have "no settled view about which of the multi-dimensionalist and probabilist approaches is correct" (p. 123). Call it "the probabilist normality rule", not "Goodman and Salow's rule".

## 3. A5-02: Dr Truthlove's conjecture is true

### Steelman
Easwaran's belief-first programme needs a purely accuracy-based reason why rational belief sets must be representable by a probability at the Lockean threshold. He proved one direction and conjectured the converse (2016, App. G). The card proves the converse with a Ville/Farkas alternative: a belief set is representable iff no positive reweighting of propositions lets another belief set strictly dominate it. It adds an exact full-support version and an extension to proposition-dependent values, and so gives the preface a sharp criterion.

### Objections
**O1. Already known. Fatal.**
- *What Rothschild proves.* His Theorem 3 states that three conditions are equivalent: (a) there is no two-way Dutch Book on B; (b) there is no weight vector x on which B is strictly dominated; (c) B is Almost Lockean Complete. It is proved with a variant of Farkas's lemma.
- *The conjecture, named.* He then states: "Lemma 1 (Easwaran's Conjuncture). If for all positive weight vectors x, B is not weakly dominated by some B′, then B is Almost Lockean Complete" (preprint p. 13; "Conjuncture" sic). His introduction says the paper proves, "as a corollary of these results, the main conjecture of Easwaran (2016)" (p. 2).
- *The extension, covered.* §8.2 says "A simple extension of these results could handle cases in which the odds are allowed to vary for each proposition". Note 29 adds that introducing this complexity "does not alter any of the results above given the linearity of the system". That covers the card's R_p, W_p extension.
- *Bibliographic status.* Erkenntnis, DOI 10.1007/s10670-021-00438-1; 88(5): 1979–1995 per index records. Not checked on the publisher's page.
- *Best reply:* The exact full-support / strong-coherence equivalence (Motzkin/Stiemke) is not in Rothschild. He notes only that the converse of his Lemma 1 fails when the representing credence gives zero to some propositions (p. 14).
- *How good:* fails. This is a footnote-sized refinement.
- *Repair:* none that is worth an essay.

**O2. Trivial once made precise. Serious.** Once the gain functions are written down, it is a two-line LP duality.

Note: Rothschild's own open conjecture (§8.1) is that Almost Lockean Complete is equivalent to not being weakly dominated by any mixture of belief sets, via Wald's complete class theorem. That is a possible small target, but too slight for an essay.

### First thirty seconds
- *Easwaran-style belief-firster:* "Rothschild did this. He even calls it my conjecture."
- *Decision theorist:* "It's Ville's alternative."
- *Preface theorist:* "The payoff is already drawn in Rothschild §§1 and 7."

### Verdict and reasons
**KILL (0.95).** Anticipated in print, with the conjecture named. Cite Rothschild in any essay that touches Lockean coherence.

## 4. A5-03: Holists need not be Lockeans (counterexample to Dorst's Proposition 5)

### Steelman
Dorst (2019, §4.2) argues that dropping Separability "gains nothing". Given any belief-dependent accuracy measure, Accuracy First, Maximize Expected Accuracy and Sequence-Irrelevance still yield a variable Lockean thesis (Prop. 5), so the link between epistemic utility theory and Lockeanism is "robust".

The card exhibits a counter-measure. It is purely alethic: separable truth/falsity scores, plus a premium β for a belief state with no false member. It meets every stated premise, yet makes optimal belief non-monotone in credence: {1} is believed at credence 0.7 but not at 0.775. The diagnosis is that the proof turns "for each world there is an increment t" into "there is one increment T_p^{B0} for all worlds". That is separability in miniature, so epistemic utility theory without separability does not decide between Lockean and closed belief.

### Text audit (accepted manuscript)
- Accuracy First (p. 8): "there are no external requirements on B—it need not be consistent, or deductively closed, or nonempty."
- §4.2 (p. 15) grants the objector that "the value (accuracy) of any given belief-state B cannot be broken down into the value of the various propositions it contains."
- The key step (p. 15): "Since A_{B0} is a real-valued function, there must be some t > 0 such that A_{B0}(B1, P, w) = A_{B0}(B0, P, w) + t. Write t as T_p^{B0}". The text continues: "It's crucial to emphasize that this assumes only that A_{B0} is well-defined."
- Prop. 5 (p. 16): "there are belief-independent values T_p and F_p for each proposition such that B is rational only if" the threshold conditions hold. Then: "The connection between epistemic utility theory and the Lockean Thesis is robust." And p. 17: "Lockeanism looked inevitable".

The quantifier shift the card alleges (∀w ∃t ⇒ ∃t ∀w) is there in the text. These are my transcriptions from page images; quote only after checking them against the published Mind text.

### Formal audit (by hand, exact)
Parameters: T = 1, F = 2, β = 3, so EA(B) = Σ_{p∈B} (3C(p) − 2) + 3·C(∩B). For each candidate core X = ∩B, take the best B with that core.

| Credence | Best core and state | EA | Runner-up | EA | Is {1} believed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C = (7/10, 1/8, 7/40) | X = {1}: B = {1},{1,2},{1,3},W | 4.30 | X = {1,3} | 4.25 | yes, at C({1}) = 0.70 |
| C′ = (31/40, 0, 9/40) | X = {1,3}: B = {1,3},W | 5.000 | X = {1} | 4.975 | no, at C′({1}) = 0.775 |
| C″ = (0.775, 0.002, 0.223) (regular variant) | X = {1,3}: B = {1,3},W | 4.988 | X = {1} | 4.975 | no |

Other values at C: X = W gives 4.00 and X = {1,2} gives 3.95. Everything else is lower at all three credences.

- The regular variant C″ shows the zero-credence cell in C′ does no work.
- Since Prop. 5's thresholds are credence-independent, the threshold for {1} would have to be ≤ 0.70 and ≥ 0.775. Contradiction.
- Dorst's conditions (ii)–(iii) hold: a true addition adds exactly T; a false one subtracts F, or F + β if the prior state was error-free at w.
- Conservativeness holds: |T| < |F|.

**Caveat: the card overclaims closure.**
- The card says the optima "are deductively closed". That holds in its 3-cell cases but not in general.
- Counterexample on 4 cells, P = (0.62, 0.18, 0.10, 0.10), same T, F, β:
  - The unique optimum believes every superset of {1} except {1} itself, so it believes {1,2} and {1,3} but not their conjunction. EA = 3.58 + 1.86 = 5.44.
  - Rivals: 5.30 with {1} added; 5.20 for the {1,2}-filter; 4.48 for the {1,3}-filter; 4.00 for W.
- In general the optimum includes its own core X iff C(X) > F/(T + F).
- So the measure yields consistency, single-premise closure and non-monotonicity, but not multi-premise closure in general.

### Objections
**O1. Not an accuracy measure, or double counting. Serious.**
- *Objection:* "The premium is a disguised extra belief in ∩B with weight β, or a coherence bonus. Dorst's A was meant to be truth-local."
- *Best reply:*
  - The premium is alethic: A(B, w) depends only on which believed propositions are true at w.
  - It is not equivalent to believing ∩B: it accrues whether or not ∩B is believed, as the 4-cell case shows.
  - No separable measure reproduces it, since separable measures always yield thresholds (Dorst's Corollary 2).
  - It is the simplest formalisation of the holist's thought that a body of belief has value as a whole, as an error-free record. That is the view §4.2 set out to accommodate.
- *How good:* partial to convincing; it needs defending in the essay.
- *Repair:* Argue the measure's credentials. Show robustness over a parameter region. Test at least one other holistic measure, for example a premium tied to believing a true strongest proposition, and check Dorst's strict-increase condition for it.

**O2. The charitable reading of Dorst. Serious.**
- *Objection:* Dorst meant holism as belief-dependence (values may depend on what else is believed), not dependence on the other beliefs' truth values. So Prop. 5 concerns truth-local measures, and the counterexample misses.
- *Best reply:* The text says the derivation "assumes only that A_{B0} is well-defined". And on the charitable reading the premise is close to Separability itself.
- *Sketch (hand derivation, still to be proved carefully).*
  - Suppose that for every B0 and p ∉ B0, the increment from adding p depends on w only through p's truth value.
  - Define D(B, w) = A(B, w) − A(∅, w) − Σ_{p∈B} g_p(w), where g_p is the increment from adding p to ∅.
  - For any two distinct, non-complementary p, q in B, D(B, ·) is both a function of p's truth value and a function of q's. Two partitions of W are generated by p and by q; a function constant on the cells of both partitions is constant unless q = ¬p. So D(B, ·) is constant.
  - Hence A is separable up to a world-independent term κ(B). Complementary pairs need separate treatment.
- *The resulting dilemma for Dorst:* either the step assumes near-Separability, and §4.2 accommodates no holism; or Prop. 5 is false.
- *A further gap:* even with truth-local increments, nothing in Sequence-Irrelevance removes B0-dependent thresholds. A world-independent κ(B) that rewards closure would also break Prop. 5, although Dorst could call such a κ non-alethic.
- *How good:* convincing once the lemma is proved.
- *Repair:* Prove the lemma, including complementary pairs, and machine-check it on 3–4 worlds.

**O3. "Rational" is only a necessary condition. Moderate.**
- *Objection:* Both Maximize Expected Accuracy and Prop. 5 are "only if" claims. Perhaps the optimal states at C and C′ are not rational.
- *Best reply:* Accuracy First removes every external requirement, so nothing but expected accuracy can disqualify the unique maximiser. If it were not rational, no state would be rational at C.
- *How good:* convincing.
- *Repair:* State this sufficiency premise explicitly.

**O4. Closest prior art. Serious until checked.**
- *Objection:* Others already get closed or cogent belief from expected value or accuracy:
  - Levi-style answer-level value and Dorst & Mandelkern 2022 give closed, non-monotone belief.
  - Wang 2025 reportedly concludes that "deductively cogent expected accuracy maximizers are neither Lockean nor Humean", and compares this with Dorst.
  - Hewson 2022 argues that accurate believers are deductively cogent, using "belief-sensitive" accuracy.
- *Best reply:*
  - Answer-level value gives nothing for adding an entailed belief, so it breaks Dorst's strict-increase premise.
  - Wang (as reported) maximises over cogent sets, which clashes with Accuracy First's open option space.
  - Hewson (as reported) changes the evaluation itself.
  - The card's measure meets Dorst's own premises, so it alone refutes Prop. 5 on its own terms.
- *How good:* partial, because Wang and Hewson are unread.
- *Repair:* Fetch and read both. Also search the literature citing Dorst 2019 for "Sequence-Irrelevance".

**O5. Text version. Moderate.** The Mind version (128: 175–211) may differ from the ORA manuscript.
- *Repair:* Fetch the published version, and quote only from the copy read.

**O6. Significance: "a gap in a side-proposition". Moderate.**
- *Objection:* Dorst defends Separability anyway; Rothschild n. 18 reads him so.
- *Best reply:*
  - §4.2 is Dorst's answer to the holist (he cites Joyce 2009 §5), and it underwrites his "robust" claim.

## 5. A5-04: Cogent guesses are exactly the value-optimal answers

### Steelman
From optionality and permissivism among credal duplicates, Holguín infers that what one thinks is fixed by "certain kinds of pure acts of the will" and does not supervene on credence (§§11–12). The card proves that the cogent guesses are exactly the answers that uniquely maximise expected value for some admissible size-and-truth valuation. On that view optionality is variation in Jamesian epistemic values. Thinking supervenes on credence plus values, and the argument for choice loses its support.

### Objections
**O1. Already known (the formal part). Fatal.**
- Dorst & Mandelkern's one-parameter model, as Dorst summarises it, already does this:
  - a true guess is worth J^{Q_p}, where Q_p is the share of complete answers p rules out;
  - a false guess is worth 0;
  - "It's permissible for p to be your guess about Q iff, for some value of J≥1, p maximizes this quantity".
- *Derivation.* The k most probable cells have expected value S_k·J^{(n−k)/n}, where S_k is their total probability. With credences sorted, log S_k is strictly concave in k. So each k is the unique best size on an open interval of J ≥ 1. With a strict gap after the k-th cell, the top-k answer is the unique optimum. Hence, for regular credences, "uniquely optimal for some J" coincides with Holguín's cogency.
- The card's claimed increment ("not one parametric family") is void, and its admissible class is larger than needed.

**O2. Trivial once made precise. Serious.** The (⇒) direction uses gerrymandered size-specific error costs. False answers of one size are nearly free, and of every other size ruinous. That is revealed-preference triviality: any permission set can be rationalised so.

**O3. Invalid inference (the philosophical part). Fatal.**
- "Supervenes on credence plus values" does not entail "is not chosen".
- Holguín's own precedent is James. He writes: "I know of no author besides James (1956) whose view predicts doxastic control as widely and directly as doxastic choice does" (n. 32). On James, the passional nature decides, so a Jamesian-values reading is Holguín's home ground, not a rival to it.
- The card needs values that are fixed, unchosen states differing between credal duplicates. It does not supply them.

**O4. Counterexample at non-regular credences. Minor.** If some cell has credence 0, W is cogent but never the unique optimum while T strictly decreases.

### First thirty seconds
- *Dorst & Mandelkern-style guessing theorist:* "That's our Filtering plus Optionality."
- *Holguín:* "Values deciding through the passional nature is my Jamesian view."
- *Decision theorist:* "Revealed preference."

### Verdict and reasons
**KILL (0.85).** The formal half is anticipated; the philosophical half is a non sequitur. At most, a two-sentence remark in an essay that cites Dorst & Mandelkern.

## 6. A5-05: For continuous quantities the probabilist normality account is not purely probabilistic

### Steelman
In continuous questions, the probabilist normality account believes the highest-density region. That region depends on a measuring function fixed neither by the question nor by the credences, so with the function free, any interval of probability t can be believed. Central intervals, which are optimal under a proper interval score, are invariant under re-measurement and give the desired Decay verdict, but they abandon normality-as-likeliness. So the account must give up either "purely probabilistic" or its filtering structure.

### Objections
**O1. Already known. Serious.** Highest-density regions are not invariant under reparametrisation; that is textbook. By the card's own account, G&S 2021 (App. B–C, n. 23) already discuss the measuring function and asymmetric distributions.

**O2. A premise the target rejects. Serious.** G&S 2023 treat normality mostly non-reductively (p. 98). They have "no settled view" between the probabilist and multi-dimensionalist approaches (p. 123). And per the card, they motivate the log measure psychophysically. The "purely probabilistic analysis" phrase is unverified. The dilemma has no grip.

**O3. Equivocation on "accuracy". Moderate.** The interval score is distance-sensitive: it rewards near misses. The values in A5-01 are truth-valued. So "accuracy grounds central intervals" uses a different notion of accuracy from the rest of the A5 cards.

**O4. Overreach. Minor.** Non-monotone relabellings are not measuring functions of an ordered quantity.

*Checks:* the Decay figures are verified by hand (exponential, mean 1, t = 0.9):
- central interval: [0.0513, 2.9957];
- highest-density, identity measure: [0, 2.3026];
- highest-density in log-time: about [0.084, 3.932]. The density in log-time, e^{y−e^y}, is equal at both endpoints (y − e^y ≈ −2.561 vs −2.563), and the mass is ≈ 0.8998.

### First thirty seconds
- *Statistician:* "Textbook."
- *Normality theorist:* "We said so, and normality is not reduced to probability."
- *Accuracy theorist:* "The interval score isn't truth-valued."

### Verdict and reasons
**KILL (0.85)** as a stand-alone card. A footnote at most.

A5-03, by contrast, refutes a published proposition on its own premises (QUALITY §1, kind 1). It locates the error in the text (kind 4), and has a legible payoff.

**Thesis (calibrated).** Dorst's argument that expected-accuracy maximisation yields a variable Lockean thesis even without Separability (2019, §4.2, Prop. 5) fails, for two reasons:
- its key step assumes that a belief's marginal accuracy depends on the world only through that belief's truth value, which is near-Separability under any natural reading;
- a purely alethic measure that adds a premium for an error-free belief state satisfies all of Dorst's stated premises, yet makes optimal belief non-monotone in credence.

So epistemic utility theory does not by itself decide between Lockean and non-Lockean (consistent, often closed, non-monotone) belief. The choice turns on whether an error-free body of belief has value beyond its parts.

What the thesis does not claim:
- that Lockeanism or Separability is false;
- that holistic accuracy always yields closure (the 4-cell counterexample shows it need not);
- that holistic accuracy can ground knowledge-aimed belief. The closing section shows it cannot, at least for answer-level values: on three-cell questions, no expected-answer-value rule is split-invariant, anti-sceptical and majority-cautious.
