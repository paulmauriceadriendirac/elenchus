## A5-01 — Truth cannot be the aim of knowledge-aimed belief: a split-invariance impossibility
- operator: IMP, TRN, DIF
- thesis: No rule that picks the believed answer to a question by maximising expected epistemic value, where an answer's value depends only on which answer it is and whether it is true (and true answers beat false ones and beat the tautology), can satisfy "split-invariance" — the property, shared by Goodman and Salow's probabilist belief rule and by Leitgeb's stability rule, that how the improbability outside one's strongest belief is divided among the rejected answers never changes what one believes — given minimal anti-sceptical and cautious behaviour; so the knowledge-aimed bridge principles cannot be vindicated by accuracy, and the accuracy-first bridge principles are all split-sensitive.
- argument:
  (P1) Split-invariance (S): if the strongest believed answer is A, then redistributing P(not-A) among the cells outside A, keeping each below every cell of A, leaves the strongest belief unchanged. Goodman and Salow's rule (belief = minimal top-set of answers with probability at least t; 2023 §5, 2025 Prop. 9) satisfies S, as does Leitgeb-style stability (A is stable iff each of its cells outweighs P(not-A); 2025 §9.2): both look only at the total excluded mass. (Machine-checked on a 3-cell grid: zero violations for both.)
  (P2) Filtering (F): the believed answer never includes a cell while excluding a more probable one. Anti-scepticism (AS): one believes cell a outright when P(a) is close enough to 1. Caution (C): for some P with P(a) > 1/2 one does not believe a outright. All four camps' closure-respecting views accept F and AS; every view with a threshold above 1/2 (Goodman–Salow's THRESHOLD; Leitgeb's r = 1/2 strictly) accepts C.
  (P3) Value-maximisation (EU): the strongest belief maximises E_P[u(G,w)] with u(G,w) = T_G if w ∈ G, F_G otherwise (Dorst's retained half of Extensionality; Easwaran's truth-only valuation), T_G > F_G for the two-cell answers, and T_{ab}, T_{ac} > T_W (a true two-cell answer is worth more than the tautology). The proof uses only that an answer's value is a continuous, strictly increasing function of its probability of truth, so it covers risk-weighted (rank-dependent) evaluation as well.
  (L) Proof sketch. Along P_x = (x, (1−x)/2, (1−x)/2), let x* be where outright belief in a starts (AS gives x* < 1; C and S give x* > 1/2). For x > x*, S lets all residual mass move to b, so EU gives x·T_a + (1−x)·F_a ≥ T_{ab} for every x > x*, hence at x*. Just below x*, F leaves only {a,b}, {a,c} or W as the chosen answer, and by continuity it ties with {a} at x*. {a,b} at the even split is worth strictly less than T_{ab} ≤ value({a}) — contradiction; W would give T_W = value({a}) ≥ T_{ab}, contradicting informativity.
  (C) F, S, AS, C and EU are jointly inconsistent; dropping any one of S, EU, AS, C, truth-directedness or informativity yields a consistent set (models: a neutral expected-guess rule; the Goodman–Salow rule; a "top-two-or-nothing" EU rule; a "believe a iff P(a) > c, c < 1/2" EU rule; constant-valued two-cell answers; T_W ≥ T_{ab}).
- key case or result: the theorem; the discriminating case is (0.91, 0.09, 0) versus (0.91, 0.045, 0.045). Split-invariant views must give the same verdict on "a" in both; every truth-valuing view must, near its switching point, believe a when the residual is dispersed and hedge to "a or b" when it is concentrated on b. That is the direction of the alternative-outcomes effect (Windschitl & Wells 1998) that Dorst (2019, Cases 1–2) and Holguín (2022, §5) use for 'think'.
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Easwaran 2016 (§3.2, App. G) and Dorst 2019 (Thm 1, Cor. 2, Prop. 5) derive Lockean thresholds from separable truth-valued scoring; Levi 1967 and Dorst & Mandelkern 2022 ("Good Guesses", abstract only) give closed, filtering rules from answer-level value; Goodman & Salow 2025 §9 compare their rule with Lockean, stability, Lin–Kelly and Levi rules on dynamic principles only; Holguín 2022 §7.2 shows sophisticated Lockeanism cannot be non-monotonic. — difference: nobody has stated which feature of knowledge-aimed rules blocks every truth-valued expected-value vindication. It is not closure, not THRESHOLD (a THRESHOLD-satisfying EU rule exists), and not filtering; it is split-invariance. The result is a minimal inconsistency, with independence models.
- why it matters: (i) Veritists about belief (accuracy-first: Dorst, Easwaran, Pettigrew-style) can underwrite only split-sensitive, guess-like belief. They cannot underwrite Goodman–Salow or Leitgeb belief. (ii) Knowledge-aimed theorists must ground belief's norms in something other than the expected value of truth, such as knowledge or normality. That is a concrete cost or a confirmation, depending on the reader. (iii) It gives a formal argument for the dualism of Goodman & Holguín (2023, abstract): "thinking" (split-sensitive, value-maximising) and "being sure" (split-invariant) have different normative foundations. (iv) It yields a testable discriminating case for every camp.
- ambition: what changes in the debate if this is right: the four-camp map gets a value-theoretic fault line. Whether belief can be accuracy-grounded is settled negatively for the threshold/stability family, by one axiom that can be checked case by case.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors (sections/pages): Goodman & Salow 2023 in full (esp. §5, pp. 117–123; §8, pp. 134–139); Goodman & Salow 2021 TARK in full (§2 incl. n.10 SUFFICIENCY+, §5, App. B–C); Goodman & Salow 2025 §§6–9 (pp. 16–30, incl. Props 5, 9, 15–19); Holguín 2022 in full (§§4–8, 10); Dorst 2019 in full (ORA preprint: §§3–5, Appendix proofs of Props 4–5); Easwaran 2016 (§§2–3, Apps D, G); Hong 2023 §§1–2.3. None states a no-go for value-maximising vindication of split-invariant rules.
- prior-art risk: medium. In Bayesian statistics, credible sets of fixed coverage are known not to be Bayes rules for a fixed linear "size + miss" loss. That is a special case of the EU half and must be located and cited (candidate source unverified). Dorst & Mandelkern 2022 and Fang 2026 (both read only via abstract) may contain remarks on threshold views; fetch requests needed.
- kill test: a published statement that threshold/HPD or stability belief is not expected-epistemic-utility rationalisable for any truth-valued answer utility, or a truth-valued utility (meeting T_G > F_G and T_{ab} > T_W) under which the Goodman–Salow rule is EU-optimal on the whole simplex.
- verifiability: elementary proof (half a page); machine checks in scratchpad a5/check_split_z3.py (split-invariance of both rules: 0 violations on a 1/60 grid) and a5/check_limit.py (exact z3 optimisation: the steepness T_a − F_a that any representing utility needs grows without bound as the critical points approach the boundary — 2001/8 at ε = 10⁻⁴ for t = 0.9 — so no fixed utility represents the rule). Independence models to be checked the same way.
- main risk: "Of course an argmax of linear functionals is split-sensitive; so what?" The reply must show the result is not obvious and has teeth. THRESHOLD alone is EU-representable. Goodman and Salow's own footnoted variant SUFFICIENCY+ (2021 n.10) is split-sensitive, so the theorem also sorts their two rules. Second risk: extensionality may be rejected via verisimilitude. Reply: for nominal questions (horse races, lotteries), which their account covers, there is no closeness structure.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: high — one theorem, one case, three corollaries; the proof and independence models take about 1,200 words.

## A5-02 — Dr Truthlove's conjecture is true: Lockean representability is exactly robust coherence
- operator: REP, TRN, GEN
- thesis: Easwaran's open "Main Conjecture" holds (Easwaran 2016, App. G). A belief state is represented by some probability function (believing everything above the threshold W/(R+W) and nothing below it) if and only if no positive reweighting of the propositions' importance lets another belief state accuracy-dominate it. With full-support representation and strong coherence the equivalence is exact. It extends unchanged to proposition-specific values R_p, W_p, which settles the converse of his "Modified Main Result".
- argument:
  (P1) Easwaran's framework: a belief state A over a finite set of situations is scored, under positive weights w_p, as Σ_{p∈A} w_p(R·[s∈p] − W·[s∉p]). A is (strongly/weakly) coherent under w iff no state weakly/strongly dominates it; A is represented by P iff P(p) > t ⇒ p ∈ A and P(p) < t ⇒ p ∉ A, with t = W/(R+W) (Easwaran 2016, §§2.2, 3.2).
  (P2) Let h_p = R·1_p − W·1_{¬p} if p ∈ A and −(R·1_p − W·1_{¬p}) otherwise: the gain of A's attitude to p over the opposite attitude. A is represented by P iff E_P[h_p] ≥ 0 for every p.
  (P3) Minimax / Farkas alternative: either such a P exists, or there are λ_p ≥ 0, not all zero, with Σ_p λ_p h_p(s) < 0 at every situation s. A Motzkin/Stiemke version gives the full-support case with weak dominance.
  (P4) In the second case, let B be A with the attitude flipped on supp(λ), and weight those propositions by λ (any positive weight elsewhere). Then B scores strictly higher than A in every situation: A is not even weakly coherent under that weighting.
  (C) Coherence under every positive weighting implies representability; with Easwaran's G.1, the two are equivalent.
- key case or result: the theorem. It reproduces Easwaran's own Appendix F/G example exactly: the 4-situation state of his Figure 11 is coherent under equal weights but not representable when W = 8R/5, and the minimax witness returns weights in the ratio 1:1:1:2 on {1,2},{1,3},{1,4},{2,3,4} — the very reweighting he offered only as "evidence for this conjecture".
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Easwaran 2016 (Noûs 50: 816–853), Apps F–G (conjecture stated; one direction proved; evidence from one example); Easwaran & Fitelson 2015 (coherence for belief; not read here); de Finetti-style duality arguments for credence. — difference: proves the conjecture, in a stronger form (weak coherence suffices, and the proof is constructive), and extends it to proposition-dependent values R_p, W_p — the setting Dorst 2019 uses — where Easwaran "ha[s] not worked out the consequences".
- why it matters: Easwaran's belief-first programme takes belief as fundamental and treats credence as what represents a coherent belief state. It needed a purely accuracy-based reason why rational belief states must be representable at all. The theorem supplies one: representability is just immunity to dominance however the propositions' importance is weighed. It bears directly on the survey's 31/31 split over whether belief or credence is more fundamental: the belief-first side gains a theorem it lacked. It also gives the Lockean answer to the preface a sharp form: an inconsistent belief state (each claim plus "some claim is false") is robustly coherent iff it is Lockean for some credence.
- ambition: what changes in the debate if this is right: a ten-year-old conjecture by a central figure closes. The "coherence vs consistency" debate over the preface (Easwaran & Fitelson) gets an exact criterion: robust coherence = Lockean representability.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors (sections/pages): Easwaran 2016 in full text (philosophersannual.org copy: §§1–6, Apps A–G, esp. pp. 28–36); Dorst 2019 (cites Easwaran §§3.2, G.2 only; no conjecture); two web searches for a proof of the conjecture (none found).
- prior-art risk: medium — the proof is a standard duality move, so someone may have noted it (Easwaran later work; Easwaran & Fitelson 2015; Pettigrew's belief work). Easwaran & Fitelson 2015 and later citing literature must be checked.
- kill test: a published proof of the conjecture, or a counterexample (which the theorem rules out unless the definitions differ from App. G's).
- verifiability: proof by the minimax theorem (one paragraph). Machine check in scratchpad a5/check_easwaran.py (exact z3: all 256 states on 3 situations at W/R ∈ {6/5, 3/2, 2, 3}, plus 400 random states on 4 situations; every non-representable state, 959 in all, gets a strict-dominance witness under a constructed positive weighting) and a5/check_fig11.py (Easwaran's own example).
- main risk: too slight for a 6,000-word essay unless the philosophical payoff for belief-first reduction and the preface is developed. Specialists may find the proof routine once the duality is seen.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: high for the theorem (about 1,500 words). The rest goes on generalisations (three attitudes, App. C; proposition-dependent values; the infinite case left open) and the reduction and preface payoffs.

## A5-03 — Holists need not be Lockeans: a counterexample to Dorst's separability-free derivation
- operator: CX, HID, REV
- thesis: Dorst's Proposition 5 (2019, §4.2) is false as stated: there is a holistic accuracy measure that meets every constraint Dorst imposes but is not variable-Lockean. Rational belief in a proposition is then retained at credence 7/10 but abandoned at credence 31/40, so no credence-independent thresholds exist, and its expected-accuracy-optimal belief states are deductively closed. The derivation silently assumes that the accuracy gain of adding a belief depends on the world only through that belief's truth value.
- argument:
  (P1) Dorst's premises for Prop. 5 (§4.2): an accuracy measure A^{B0} exists for each prior state B0; adding a true belief strictly raises accuracy and adding a false one strictly lowers it; Accuracy First; Maximise Expected Accuracy; Sequence-Irrelevance.
  (P2) The perfect-record measure A(B,w) = Σ_{p∈B}(T·[w∈p] − F·[w∉p]) + β·[w ∈ ∩B] meets all of them: a true addition raises A by exactly T; a false one lowers it by F or F+β; evaluation is of whole states, so sequence is irrelevant.
  (P3) Dorst's Prop. 4 writes the gain from a false addition as a constant F_p^{B0} ("there must be some t … write t as T_p^{B0}"). Under (P2) the loss is F + β·[w ∈ ∩B0]: it depends on whether the rest of one's beliefs are true at w. The step needs a hidden premise, marginal extensionality: the value of adding p varies with the world only through p's truth value.
  (P4) Without that premise Prop. 5 fails. Exact enumeration on three situations with T = 1, F = 2, β = 3 finds, for the single-situation proposition {1}, that every optimal state believes it at C = (7/10, 1/8, 7/40) and no optimal state believes it at C' = (31/40, 0, 9/40). Credence-independent thresholds are impossible, and the optima are filters: {1} and its supersets, versus {1,3} and its supersets.
  (C) Epistemic utility theory does not entail Lockeanism once separability goes. A natural holistic value — a premium on a flawless record — yields closed, non-monotone belief instead.
- key case or result: the counterexample (exhaustively computed). It also shows which premise does the work: marginal extensionality is separability in miniature.
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Dorst 2019 (Mind 128), §4.2 and Appendix (Props 4–5); Easwaran 2016 App. D (concedes that dropping additivity is "a deeper challenge", unexplored); Dorst & Mandelkern 2022 and Levi 1967 (answer-level value, under which adding an entailed belief adds nothing — so they violate Dorst's strict-increase premise rather than refute Prop. 5); Holguín 2022 §7.2 (sophisticated Lockeanism is monotone, so it cannot fit non-monotonicity). — difference: shows that Prop. 5 is false even granting all its stated premises, isolates the hidden premise, and shows that a measure that rewards true belief strictly and piecemeal can still deliver closure and Holguín-style non-monotonicity.
- why it matters: Dorst's case for reducing belief to credence (§5.1) and his claim that Lockeanism is "robust" (§4.2, "Lockeanism looked inevitable") rest on Prop. 5. It also shows that the closure-versus-Lockean choice is a choice about the value of a flawless record, not something accuracy itself settles. With A5-01 this maps which camps accuracy can and cannot ground.
- ambition: what changes in the debate if this is right: the best-known derivation of Lockeanism from accuracy loses its holist extension. The accuracy framework becomes neutral between Lockean and closed belief, which shifts the burden onto arguments about epistemic value.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors (sections/pages): Dorst 2019 in full (ORA preprint, pp. 1–30, incl. the Appendix proofs of Props 4–5); Easwaran 2016 App. D; Holguín 2022 §7.2; Goodman & Salow 2025 §9.4 (Levi).
- prior-art risk: medium — a reply to Dorst 2019, or Dorst's own later work (Dorst & Mandelkern 2022, read via abstract only), may already flag the gap. Fetch Dorst & Mandelkern 2022 and search the citing literature for "Sequence-Irrelevance".
- kill test: a published refutation of Prop. 5 or an erratum. Or a reading of Dorst's text on which the world-independence of T_p^{B0}, F_p^{B0} is an explicit premise — then the card shrinks to "the explicit premise is separability in disguise".
- verifiability: exact enumeration (scratchpad a5/check_dorst.py: all 128 belief states over the 7 non-empty propositions of 3 situations, on a 1/40 credence grid; violations found for all 6 non-trivial propositions); the hidden-premise diagnosis is checkable against Dorst's text (Appendix, proof of Prop. 4).
- main risk: if the reading in "kill test" is right, the result is a clarification rather than a refutation, and too slight alone. Best used as the second half of an A5-01 essay rather than as a stand-alone essay.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: medium alone; high as a section of the A5-01 essay.

## A5-04 — Thinking supervenes on credence plus epistemic value: cogent guesses are exactly the value-optimal answers
- operator: REP, DIS, TRN
- thesis: Holguín's "cogent" guesses (2022, §10) are exactly the answers that uniquely maximise expected epistemic value for some admissible valuation. The valuation must depend only on an answer's size and truth, prefer true to false answers, and prefer more informative true answers. So the optionality and permissivism Holguín takes to show that what one thinks is a "choice" and "does not supervene on credence" (§11) are fully explained by variation in Jamesian epistemic values. His argument from credal duplicates therefore does not support doxastic voluntarism.
- argument:
  (P1) Holguín: a guess G (union of cells of Q) is cogent iff every cell in G is strictly more probable than every cell outside it; rational thinking = thinking one's cogent guess (Cogent Guess, §10). Credal duplicates can rationally think different things; Holguín infers that thinking is fixed by "pure acts of the will" (Doxastic Choice, §11).
  (P2) Theorem. G is cogent for P iff there are values T_1 > T_2 > … > T_n and F_k < T_k such that G is the unique maximiser of P(H)·T_{|H|} + (1 − P(H))·F_{|H|}. (⇐) For fixed size, expected value is maximised exactly by the most probable cells, and uniqueness excludes ties across the boundary. (⇒) Make false answers of every size other than |G| very costly and false answers of size |G| nearly costless, and keep T decreasing; only G survives.
  (P3) Hence for any two credal duplicates with different cogent thoughts there are admissible value profiles — exactly the "weighing of seeking truth against avoiding error" that James, and Easwaran (2016, §2) with his R and W, treat as the agent's "passional nature" — that make each one's thought uniquely optimal.
  (C) Holguín's data (optionality, the infelicity of "A or C" when B beats C, permissivism) are predicted by value-maximisation plus value pluralism. "Does not supervene on credence" is true but does not support "is a choice": thinking supervenes on credence together with epistemic value.
- key case or result: the representation theorem; Holguín's own horse race [a .35, b .30, c .20, d .15]. Each of his four permissible answers (A; A or B; A, B or C; no view) is the unique optimum for an explicit value profile, and the infelicitous "A or C" is optimal for none.
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Holguín 2022 (§§8–12); Dorst & Mandelkern 2022 ("Good Guesses", abstract only: guesses optimise an accuracy–informativity trade-off); Dorst 2019 §4.1 (thresholds shifted by contextual priorities); Easwaran 2016 §2 (R and W as James's "passional nature"); Levi 1967 (caution index). — difference: an exact characterisation of the whole cogency set by the class of admissible values (not one parametric family), and its use against the inference from optionality to doxastic choice.
- why it matters: it removes the main argument for the most radical thesis in the "belief is weak" camp (voluntarism and non-supervenience). It unifies Holguín's norm with the accuracy-first models of Dorst and Dorst & Mandelkern. With A5-01, it explains why permissive guess views escape the split-invariance no-go: they quantify over values.
- ambition: what changes in the debate if this is right: the guessing camp's metaphysics (choice) and its normative theory (value-maximisation) come apart. Any remaining case for doxastic choice must show that epistemic values are themselves chosen, which is a different and weaker thesis.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors (sections/pages): Holguín 2022 in full (esp. §§10–12 and nn. 27–34); Dorst 2019 §§4.1, 5; Goodman & Salow 2025 n. 41 (on "best guess" vs Dorst & Mandelkern).
- prior-art risk: medium-high — Dorst & Mandelkern 2022 may prove that every "filtered" guess is optimal for some value of their J parameter, which would anticipate (P2). That needs a fetch. (If their one-parameter family misses some cogent guesses, that is itself a discriminating result between Holguín and Dorst–Mandelkern.)
- kill test: Dorst & Mandelkern (or a reply to Holguín) stating the equivalence; or a reading of Holguín on which epistemic values are already counted as choices, so that the reply is anticipated.
- verifiability: proof (half a page); machine check in scratchpad a5/check_cogent.py (exact z3 LP: 900 credence/answer pairs on 4 cells, zero mismatches between cogency and unique optimality under some admissible value).
- main risk: philosophically thin unless the voluntarism argument is reconstructed precisely; the theorem itself is easy.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: medium — the theorem is short; the essay must carry the dialectic about choice and supervenience (Holguín §11) and engage Dorst & Mandelkern closely.

## A5-05 — For continuous quantities the probabilist normality account is not purely probabilistic: a freedom theorem and an accuracy-based rival
- operator: LIM, TRN, DIF
- thesis: For continuous questions, Goodman and Salow's probabilist account makes belief depend on a "measuring function" that the question and the credences do not fix (2021, App. B–C). With that function free, any interval of probability t can be made the strongest belief. The natural accuracy-based alternative — central credible intervals, which are optimal under the proper interval score — is invariant under re-measurement and yields their desired Decay verdict without the logarithmic measuring function, but violates normality-as-likeliness. So the account must give up either its "purely probabilistic analysis" or its filtering structure.
- argument:
  (P1) Goodman & Salow (2021, App. B; 2025, §6) order answers by probability density relative to a measuring function m and believe the highest-density region of probability t (2023, §5: "the highest posterior density region"). For Decay they reject the identity measure because it leaves immediate decay doxastically possible, and adopt the logarithm on psychophysical grounds (2021, App. C).
  (P2) Freedom lemma. For any non-atomic P on an ordered quantity and any interval I with P(I) = t, there is a monotone m whose density is at least c on I and below c outside it. Then the highest-density region at t is I. With non-monotone relabellings, any set of probability t qualifies.
  (P3) The interval score of Gneiting & Raftery (2007) is strictly proper for the central interval; expected-score-optimal interval belief is therefore the central interval, invariant under every monotone m. For Decay at t = 0.9: central [0.051, 2.996] mean-lives; highest-density with identity m [0, 2.303]; highest-density with log m [0.084, 3.932] — three different belief states (computed).
  (C) The account's continuous predictions are carried by m, so it is "probability + a measuring convention", not a purely probabilistic analysis. The only accuracy-grounded alternative abandons normality-as-likeliness.
- key case or result: the Decay figures above, and a skewed-error variant of Bjorn's scale, where the highest-density and central intervals disagree about whether a mild overestimate is ruled out.
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Goodman & Salow 2021 (App. B–C, n. 23, which notes criticism of minimum-likelihood intervals for asymmetric distributions and lacks space to answer it); Goodman & Salow 2025 §6; Gneiting & Raftery 2007 (proper interval score; to verify before citing). The non-invariance of highest-density regions is textbook statistics. — difference: turns their acknowledged parameter into a freedom result, supplies an accuracy-based rival that hits their Decay target invariantly, and states the resulting dilemma.
- why it matters: it locates exactly where the probabilist normality programme needs non-probabilistic input. It ties continuous belief to the accuracy debate (A5-01: in nominal questions no split-invariant rule is accuracy-grounded; here accuracy grounds central intervals, not normality).
- ambition: what changes in the debate if this is right: modest. It forces the normality theorist to defend measuring functions as part of a question's content, or to adopt quantile structure.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors (sections/pages): Goodman & Salow 2021 in full (App. B, C and n. 23); Goodman & Salow 2023 §§5–6; Goodman & Salow 2025 §§6, 8.
- prior-art risk: high for the statistical facts (well known); medium for the philosophical dilemma (partly acknowledged by the authors).
- kill test: Goodman & Salow (or later work) already defending measuring functions as part of the question's content and comparing them with central intervals.
- verifiability: lemma by construction; Decay figures computed (scratchpad a5/decay.py); the interval-score optimality is a cited theorem (verify the source).
- main risk: reads as "the authors already said this". It has the weakest increment of the five cards.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: medium — feasible but thin. Better as a section of the A5-01 essay than as a stand-alone essay.
