## A4-01 — Normal-backup preemption: background normality makes preemption depend on whether the preempted process was normal
- operator: CX, DIF, DIS
- thesis: In a preemption whose backup process is enabled only by normal absences, the normality-based accounts in the anchors deny that the preempting event is a cause. These are A&G 2024a (conditions (1)–(5)), A&G 2024b (Def. 2, (i)–(v)), and the HP definitions (original, updated, modified) with Halpern–Hitchcock witness-normality generated by their Default Rules 1–2 or by their §7.7 dependence ranking whenever the backup mechanism is the typical one. Their normality-free counterparts (A&G's own preliminary Def. 1 and Def. 2 without (v); HP without normality; Beckers's NESS and CNESS) count the event a cause. In every case the normality-based verdict flips back to "cause" when the only change is that the backup's trigger is a deviant event. So no account in the anchors gets both bogus prevention and normal-backup preemption right. The claim is exactly that, and no more: it is not claimed that normality is idle, nor that Gallow's endpoint-deviancy account fails here (as A&G report it, it should count the event a cause).
- argument:
  (P1) Locusts. It is the dry season. Without rain (R=0, normal) the maize will wilt and die within the week. That is D, with D = ¬R ∧ ¬Lo. On day 3 a locust swarm strips the field (Lo=1, deviant): E = Lo ∨ D. So Lo=1, R=0, D=0, E=1. The verdict that the locusts caused the crop's destruction is as firm as in any preemption case: the locusts' process ran to completion and the drought's never did. Nothing in the vignette makes the normality of the drought bear on whose process destroyed the crop.
  (P2) Proposition 1 (A&G, both versions). Let N be a set of actual normal root facts which, with the laws of the candidate's descendants alone, entail E. Then C is not an A&G cause of E.
  Proof. Conditions (5)/(iv) force those laws into L′. So by (2)/(iii) no admissible F′ contains all of N. Some n ∈ N therefore lies in F∖F′. That n is a root, is neither an ancestor nor a descendant of C, and is not more deviant than its negation, so it violates (4) and (v). In the locust case N = {¬R}: D's law and E's law are both laws of descendants of Lo, and ¬R makes E = Lo ∨ ¬Lo.
  (P3) Proposition 2 (HP + HH normality). Suppose that in the actual world every variable other than C takes its default value or obeys its equation, and that E occurs in the all-default solution. Then every HP witness for C (original, updated or modified) either sets some root to a deviant value or holds some variable contrary to its equation. Under HH's Default Rule 1 that variable is strictly less plausible than in the actual world. Under Default Rule 2 (incomparability across variables) the witness is therefore not ⪰ the actual world, so C is not (part of) a cause.
  (P4) The same accounts get bogus prevention right for the same structural reason, since survival is "normally inevitable". The control case (backup triggered by a deviant event, as in A&G's own preemption model) yields "cause" on every account. So the verdict tracks the normality of the preempted trigger, which the vignette makes causally idle.
  (C) Background normality, whether as a filter on excluded facts (A&G) or on witness worlds (HH), cannot tell an active normal alternative (bogus prevention: no poison ⇒ survival) from a preempted one (locusts: no rain ⇒ drought death, cut by Lo). Any account that handles bogus prevention this way is refuted by normal-backup preemption.
- key case or result: the locust model above, computed. A&G 2024a and 2024b: Lo not a cause. A&G prelim. Def. 1 and Def. 2 without deviancy: cause. HP orig/upd/mod: cause. HP orig/upd/mod with Default-Rule normality: not even part of a cause. With HH-§7.7 ranking (drought law most typical): not a cause. With value-only typicality: cause. NESS, CNESS: cause. BV: not a cause (BV fails all preemption, as Beckers notes). The result is robust to: a second normal absence (no irrigation); a deviant root shared by both routes ("planted", A&G still fail); a late-preemption form with an intermediate "field stripped" variable. It flips (all accounts say cause) when the backup's trigger is deviant, or when the backup route has its own deviant root.
- nearest prior work as far as I know:
  - Andreas & Günther 2024a (PPQ 105: 2–32) and 2024b (Phil Stud 181: 2145–2176): their deviancy conditions. Their preemption model has a deviant backup trigger. Difference: the case and Proposition 1 show that the verdict depends on the backup trigger's normality.
  - Halpern & Hitchcock 2015 (BJPS 66: 413–457), §7.7: they concede that their preemption treatment "involves an exception" to the default that endogenous variables obey their equations, and propose adding variables. Difference: for a normal backup no exception is principled. The backup mechanism is the typical one, so their own §7.7 ranking delivers the wrong verdict.
  - Harinen, "Normal Causes for Normal Effects" (not yet read; a search snippet says it argues HH fail when the effect takes a normal value). Must be read before drafting.
- why it matters: Normality is the leading answer to the isomorphism problem (the dossier flags it as unsolved). The card shows that the way it is currently used, as a constraint on the background or contrast, buys bogus prevention at the price of preemption of normal processes, which is ubiquitous (thirst, exposure, decay, drought). Any normality-based account must now say how it tells active alternatives from preempted ones.
- kill test: (a) a principled reading of A&G's deviancy (e.g., treating "no rain" as norm-violating) that is not ad hoc; (b) a text by HH or Halpern (e.g., the 2016 book) that treats preempted normal backups explicitly; (c) Harinen or another paper already giving this case or Proposition 2.
- verifiability: Propositions 1–2 are short proofs. Every verdict is computed exhaustively in `A4_models.py` (functions `ag_cause`, `hp_part_of_cause` with normality schemes, `cness_cause`).
- main risk: (i) Defenders may say the destruction was "normal" and so needs no cause. Reply: the preliminary theories, all counterfactual accounts and ordinary judgment say it has one. (ii) For HH, the verdict depends on the typicality ranking. The card claims failure only under their stated Default Rules or §7.7 ranking. Value-only typicality saves the case, but HH themselves reject it (§7.7: "a subtle mistake").
- feasibility in 6,000 words: high. One case, two short propositions, a computed table, one repair discussion.
- ambition: if right, normality no longer counts as a solved add-on to the isomorphism problem. Every normality-based account in print owes an account of the active/preempted distinction.

Beckers 2021 in full. HH 2015 §§6, 7.4, 7.7. HH compact-representations §4 (Default Rules). Halpern 2015 §§2–4. None of them considers a backup enabled only by normal absences.
- prior-art risk: medium. The structure is simple, so someone may have noticed it (Harinen's paper must be read). Halpern's 2016 book is unread.
