# A4 — Actual causation: five attack cards (ranked best first)

**Pressure points.** Read in full:
- the two anchors: Beckers 2021 (CNESS) and Andreas & Günther 2024a (PPQ 105: 2–32);
- A&G's open-access sequel, "A Lewisian Regularity Theory" (Phil Stud 181: 2145–2176, 2024b). It replaces 2024a's deviancy condition (4) with a new (v), adds the mentor case (fn. 2), and reports Gallow 2021 and Halpern 2016 in §5.3.

Also read:
- Halpern 2015 (arXiv 1505.00162: the modified HP definition);
- Halpern & Hitchcock 2015 (arXiv 1309.1226: normality, §§6–7);
- HH's companion "Compact Representations of Extended Causal Models" (arXiv 1309.1227: Default Rules 1–2).

Not reachable, with fetch requests filed or already existing: Gallow 2021, A&G 2021 (BJPS), Halpern 2016 (book), Harinen 2017. Nothing below attributes content to them beyond what A&G 2024b report, or what OpenAlex abstracts say (marked).

The pressure points:
1. Normality is the leading answer to the isomorph problem (bogus prevention vs. overdetermination), but it acts on backgrounds and contrasts. What happens when the preempted backup is itself normal?
2. HH's Default Rule 1 ("typical for variables to satisfy the equations"), and their own admission (2015, §7.7) that preemption needs an exception to it.
3. Witnesses for the modified HP definition only freeze actual values, so they must violate equations.
4. Halpern's normality-based treatment of Hall's switch, and A&G's dispute with Halpern–Hitchcock over whether the L/R switch model is apt.
5. A&G's maximality condition (iii) together with deviancy condition (v); Paul & Hall's MEDP as A&G's success case.

Every verdict below is computed in explicit binary structural-equation models. The code is `state/ambitious/cards/A4_models.py`; run `python3 A4_models.py --checks`. It reproduces every published verdict I could test:
- A&G 2024a table and 2024b fn. 2, including 2024a's own error on the mentor case;
- Beckers's Examples 1–2, Theorems 2–3 and the (C∧D)∨A example;
- Halpern 2015, Examples 3.1–3.4, 3.9 and 3.10.

## A4-01 — Locusts: background normality makes preemption depend on whether the preempted process was normal
- operator: CX, DIF, DIS
- thesis: When a deviant event preempts a backup process enabled only by normal absences, the normality-based accounts in the anchors deny that the event is a cause. These accounts are:
  - Andreas & Günther 2024a (conditions (1)–(5));
  - Andreas & Günther 2024b (Def. 2, (i)–(v));
  - every HP definition (original, updated, modified) with Halpern–Hitchcock witness-normality generated by their Default Rules 1–2, or by their §7.7 ranking when the backup mechanism is the typical one.
  The same accounts without their normality component count the event a cause: A&G's preliminary Def. 1; HP without normality; Beckers's NESS and CNESS. In every case the verdict returns to "cause" when the only change is that the backup's trigger is deviant, or that the idle absence is made norm-violating. So no account in the anchors gets both bogus prevention and normal-backup preemption right.
  Not claimed: that normality is idle; or that Gallow's account fails. As A&G 2024b (§5.3.1) report it, Gallow's account constrains only cause and effect, and should count the locusts a cause.
- argument:
  (P1) Locusts. It is the dry season. Without rain (R=0, normal) the maize would die within the week: D = ¬R ∧ ¬Lo. On day 3 a locust swarm (Lo=1, deviant) strips it: E = Lo ∨ D. The verdict that the locusts caused the destruction is as firm as in any preemption: the locusts' process ran to completion and the drought's never did.
  (P2) Proposition 1 (A&G, both versions). Let N be a set of actual normal root facts which, with only the laws of the candidate's descendants, entail E. Then C is not an A&G cause of E.
  Proof. (5)/(iv) put those laws in every admissible L′. So by (2)/(iii) no admissible F′ contains all of N. The excluded n ∈ N is a root, neither ancestor nor descendant of C, and not more deviant than its negation, so it violates (4) and (v). Here N = {¬R}: the laws of D and E, both descendants of Lo, make E = Lo ∨ ¬Lo.
  (P3) Proposition 2 (HP + HH normality). Suppose the actual world is typical in every variable except C, and E occurs in the all-default solution. Then every HP witness for C either gives some root a deviant value or makes some variable violate its equation. Under HH's Default Rule 1 that variable is strictly less typical than in the actual world. Under Default Rule 2 (incomparability across variables) the witness is therefore not ⪰ actual.
  (P4) Bogus prevention is handled by the same "normally inevitable" structure (survival). The control case, with a deviant backup trigger (A&G's own preemption model), gives "cause" everywhere. The verdict therefore tracks the normality of a trigger that the vignette makes causally idle.
  (C) Normality used as a filter on excluded facts (A&G) or on witness worlds (HH) cannot tell an active normal alternative (bogus prevention) from a preempted one (locusts).
- key case or result (`--checks` §2, §5):
  - NBP1: A&G prelim. Def. 1 says cause; 2024a and 2024b say not; HP (all versions) say cause; HP + Default-Rule normality says not; NESS and CNESS say cause.
  - The same pattern holds with a second normal absence; with a deviant root shared by both routes; and in a late-preemption form.
  - Every verdict becomes "cause" when the backup trigger is deviant (control), when the backup route has its own deviant root, or when ¬R is made norm-violating. Yet rain would not have changed E.
- nearest prior work as far as I know:
  - A&G 2024a/2024b: deviancy conditions; their preemption model has a deviant backup trigger.
  - HH 2015 §7.7: they concede that their preemption treatment "involves an exception to the default assumption that it is typical for endogenous variables to depend on one another in accordance with the structural equations".
  - Harinen, Erkenntnis 82 (2017): 1299–1320, "Normal Causes for Normal Effects". Abstracts only, via OpenAlex; full text not read; fetch filed. Harinen argues that HH's way of ruling out bogus prevention leaves their account open to counterexamples where the effect is normal, and proposes that normal effects are judged to have normal causes.
  - Difference: Propositions 1–2, the trigger-flip control, and coverage of both A&G versions. Harinen's hypothesis may predict weaker judgments for the abnormal locusts, and the essay must meet it.
- why it matters: Normality is the received fix for isomorphs. Normal backups (thirst, exposure, decay, drought) are everywhere. Every normality-based account must now say how it tells active alternatives from preempted ones.
- kill test: (a) a non-ad-hoc reading on which "no rain" counts as deviant for A&G; (b) Halpern 2016 or HH handling preempted normal backups explicitly; (c) Harinen already giving this case or Proposition 2.
- verifiability: two short proofs; exhaustive computation in `A4_models.py` (`ag_cause`, `hp_part_of_cause` with schemes, `cness_cause`).
- main risk: Defenders may answer that the destruction was "normally inevitable" and so needs no cause. Reply: every normality-free account and the theories' own preliminary versions say it has one. For HH the claim holds only under their stated Default Rules or §7.7 ranking; the licence rule of A4-02 saves the case.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: high. One vivid case, two short propositions, one table.
- ambition: normality stops counting as a solved add-on. The active/preempted distinction becomes the open problem that every normality-based account owes an answer to.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors: A&G 2024a in full (§§4.1–4.4, pp. 11–25; table pp. 28–29). A&G 2024b in full (§4.2 pp. 2156–2158 and fn. 2; §5.3 pp. 2169–2173). Beckers 2021 in full. HH 2015 §§6, 7.4, 7.7. HH compact §4. Halpern 2015 §§2–4. None considers a backup enabled only by normal absences.
- prior-art risk: medium. The structure is simple; Harinen 2017 and Halpern 2016 must be read.

## A4-02 — What normality must contribute: deviations need licences (a characterization within HH's framework)
- operator: IMP, GEN, DIS
- thesis: Call a normality scheme local and uniform when:
  - each variable's typicality in a world depends only on two things: whether its value is the variable's default, and whether the value obeys the variable's equation given its parents' values in that world;
  - the ranking of these categories is the same for all variables;
  - worlds are compared componentwise, as HH's Default Rule 2 prescribes.
  Of all 2,071 such schemes (weak orders on six categories, root default above root deviant), the ones that make the HP definitions (original, updated, modified alike) give the standard verdicts on a 24-query battery of canonical cases are exactly those with:
  - a deviant value licensed by its equation exactly as typical as a conforming default (E10 = E00);
  - a default value that violates its equation at least as typical (E01 ≥ E00);
  - unlicensed deviant values lowest (E11 < E00, E10, E01).
  In words: normality must penalize a value only when it is a deviant value that the variable's own mechanism did not produce. HH's Default Rule 1 and value-only typicality are both excluded. Every admissible scheme counts the flip in Hall's L/R switch as a cause, and the antidote in A&G's two-variable mentor model as part of a cause. The claim is relative to this class and battery, not to every conceivable ordering.
- argument:
  (P1) HH: X=x is a cause only if some witness world is ⪰ the actual world. Default Rule 1 says "it is typical for variables to satisfy the equations, unless we explicitly stipulate otherwise". Default Rule 2 makes values of distinct variables incomparable.
  (P2) Necessity: each constraint is forced by one canonical case.
  - E10 ≥ E00, by genuine prevention: the witness contains the poison's licensed death.
  - E00 ≥ E10, by the chain and overdetermination: the witness returns a licensed effect to default.
  - E01 ≥ E00, by preemption and by every endogenous cause. The modified-HP witness freezes the backup at its actual non-completion, and the candidate's own counterfactual value is an unlicensed default (the hiker not ducking although the boulder falls).
  - E11 < E00, by the short circuit (HH Ex. 4.2; Halpern 2015 Ex. 3.10): the witness keeps the poison in without the antidote, an unlicensed deviation.
  (P3) Sufficiency: an exhaustive check shows that every scheme meeting the constraints gets the whole battery right except the two listed cases.
  (P4) Corollaries:
  - Default Rule 1 violates E01 ≥ E00. It therefore fails five cases (the hiker's ducking; the double preventers in EDP and MEDP; the locusts; mentor), and eight under the modified definition.
  - Value-only typicality violates E10 ≥ E00 and fails genuine prevention.
  - The two remaining failures disappear in richer models: the mentor case with a neutralization variable (as in HH 2015, fn. 15), and the switch with blocked-track variables.
  (C) Within this class, what normality contributes is exactly a licence requirement on deviant values.
- key case or result (`--checks` §3):
  - 0 schemes correct on all queries; 38 correct on all but {SW, MENTOR}; the forced relations are printed by the check.
  - Minimal unsatisfiable pairs include {genuine prevention, mentor} and {locusts, L/R switch}.
- nearest prior work as far as I know: HH 2015 §6 guidelines and §7.7; HH compact representations, Default Rules 1–2. They propose symmetric equation-obedience; I show it is excluded and that the asymmetric licence rule is forced. Hall 2007 and Hitchcock 2007 are default/deviant accounts that I know only through the anchors' descriptions. Their asymmetry may coincide with the licence rule, which must be checked before drafting.
- why it matters: It answers the dossier's open question within the HP framework. It turns normality from a case-by-case dial into a forced rule, and identifies which verdicts normality cannot deliver.
- kill test: (a) a principled non-local or non-uniform scheme that HH endorse and that gets the battery right; (b) the licence rule proved necessary in Halpern 2016, Hall 2007 or Hitchcock 2007; (c) a canonical case outside the battery that the licence rule gets wrong.
- verifiability: Exhaustive enumeration (2,071 schemes × 3 definitions × 24 queries). Necessity also holds by hand from the forcing cases, and that half extends to partial preorders by the same cases; this extension is not machine-checked.
- main risk: The class restriction (HH allow "special considerations"), and the model choices for battery cases.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: medium-high. One table, four forcing cases, one theorem statement.
- ambition: In HP-style accounts, normality becomes a determinate rule rather than a free parameter. HH's own default is refuted, and two standard verdicts are moved from normality to aptness.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors: HH 2015 §§6–7. HH compact §4. Halpern 2015 §§2–4. A&G 2024b §5.3, for Halpern 2016 pp. 79–81 and 90–91. None states or proves a characterization of admissible typicality.
- prior-art risk: medium. Hall's and Hitchcock's 2007 default/deviant accounts (unread) are the likeliest overlap.

## A4-03 — The frozen-witness lemma: the modified HP definition plus HH's default normality collapses
- operator: HID, LIM
- thesis: Every modified-HP witness assigns some variable a value its own equation does not license, whenever it is a witness for a cause on which the effect does not counterfactually depend, or for an endogenous candidate (Lemma). Halpern (2015) writes that HH's normality extension "applies without change to the modified definition". With HH's Default Rule 1 in that extension, the modified definition counts as causes only minimal conjunctions of root-level but-for factors. The consequences:
  - in Suzy–Billy late preemption, Suzy's throw is only part of a cause, and Billy's preempted throw is part of it too;
  - no intermediate event is ever a cause.
  HH's own remedy for such exceptions (2015, §7.7: add variables so that witness worlds obey the equations) cannot work for the modified definition, because its witnesses never reset roots. Claimed about this stated combination only. I do not claim Halpern's book adopts it (book unread).
- argument:
  (P1) A modified-HP witness is [X←x′, W←w*], where w* are actual values.
  (P2) If no variable violates its equation there, the witness is the unique solution under [X←x′], so the effect would depend counterfactually on X. For an endogenous singleton X, its parents keep their actual values, so x′ violates X's equation.
  (P3) Under Default Rules 1–2, a violating variable is less typical than in the actual world (where it obeys its equation), so the witness is not ⪰ actual.
  (C) Only violation-free witnesses survive, that is, root-level conjunctive but-for causes. In late preemption ST ∧ BT is such a cause, and it is minimal because neither singleton survives. The Lemma is model-independent, so no extension helps. For the modified definition this answers negatively the question HH raise in §7.7, read as a question about causal verdicts.
- key case or result (`--checks` §4): Suzy–Billy: ST is part of a cause only via {ST, BT}; BT is part of a cause. Random models: 423 of 423 witnesses for non-but-for or endogenous candidates contain a violation.
- nearest prior work as far as I know: HH 2015 §7.7 (the exception, and the question whether adding variables can always remove it); Halpern 2015 (normality extension for the modified definition). Difference: a proof that for the modified definition the exception cannot be eliminated, and that dropping it collapses the definition.
- why it matters: Halpern's preferred definition can be combined with normality only under value-based (licence) typicality. This independently motivates A4-02.
- kill test: Halpern 2016 restricts normality for the modified definition (e.g., to exogenous values), so that frozen values are exempt.
- verifiability: a five-line proof plus the computational check.
- main risk: it may read as a technical footnote; the book may already handle it.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: high for the result, but slim for a whole essay. Best as a section of A4-02.
- ambition: shows that the most-cited definition and its authors' default normality rules cannot be combined.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors: Halpern 2015 §§2–5. HH 2015 §§6, 7.7. HH compact §4.
- prior-art risk: medium.

## A4-04 — The switch is not a normality problem
- operator: IMP, DIS
- thesis: No local uniform scheme lets any HP definition say both that the locusts caused the destruction and that flipping the switch did not cause the arrival in Hall's L/R model: {locusts, switch} is minimally unsatisfiable for all three definitions. For the modified definition, the switch also conflicts with the double preventer in EDP being a cause, and with Suzy-cause/Billy-not. Making arrival the default does not help: {switch, genuine prevention, late preemption, locusts} is still unsatisfiable.
  Consequences:
  - Halpern's normality-based switch verdict (2015 Ex. 3.9; 2016 pp. 79–81 and 90–91, as reported by A&G 2024b) conflicts with standard preemption verdicts.
  - It can be kept only by declaring the L/R model inapt, as Halpern & Hitchcock 2010 do (as reported by A&G).
  - A&G 2024b contest exactly that inaptness verdict (they say L and R are not logically dependent). If they are right, HP-style accounts must count the flip a cause, while A&G's condition (iv) does not.
- argument:
  (P1) The modified switch witness freezes LT=0 while F=0, an unlicensed default. The only blockers are a penalty on E01, or on the arrival or right-track values.
  (P2) The locusts need E01 ≥ E00 and E00 ≥ E10. Late preemption and endogenous causes need E01 ≥ E00. Genuine prevention needs E10 ≥ E00.
  (P3) Each blocker therefore breaks one of these.
  (C) The switch verdict must come from model choice, not normality.
- key case or result: `--checks` §3 (minimal sets; arrival-default-1 rows).
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Halpern 2015 Ex. 3.9 ("the world where the train does not go on the left track despite the switch being set to the left is less normal than the actual world"); A&G 2024b pp. 2170–2171 (the aptness dispute). Difference: a proof that the normality route is closed, so the dispute is about aptness only.
- why it matters: Switches are the paradigm of transitivity failure. The result relocates the problem for HP accounts and gives a discriminating point in favour of path-based accounts if L/R is apt. It cuts in A&G's favour, which balances A4-01 and A4-05.
- kill test: an independent, accepted aptness condition that L/R violates (the result then becomes a corollary); a principled scheme outside the class.
- verifiability: exhaustive check; hand proof from the four constraints.
- main risk: depends on the A4-02 apparatus and on the L/R model.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: medium (best combined with A4-02).
- ambition: settles that, for HP accounts, the switch is an aptness question.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors: Halpern 2015 Ex. 3.9; A&G 2024b §5.3; HH 2015 §7.7.
- prior-art risk: medium-low.

## A4-05 — A&G's deviancy condition makes verdicts hostage to idle absences
- operator: TEN, REP, CX
- thesis: In A&G's theory (both versions), whether c caused e flips merely because an absence ¬X changes its normative status, even when ¬X is idle: had X occurred, e would still have occurred, everything else as actual.
  - (a) With the locusts, Lo becomes a cause iff "no rain" is norm-violating.
  - (b) In Paul & Hall's MEDP, which A&G count a success, f becomes a cause iff ¬G is norm-violating. Yet the reason A&G report for f's non-causation ("this non-occurrence does not make any difference", 2024a p. 22) does not depend on that status.
  Diagnosis: condition (iii) uses unrestricted entailment, so (v) tests facts whose only link to e runs backward through C's own effects.
  Repair: require deviancy only of forward candidates given F′, i.e. ⟨L′,∅⟩[F′][{C′}] ⊨ E. This is what A&G's own rationale for (v) says: "Any one C′ in F∖F′ is a candidate cause of E because it entails the effect together with the propositions in F′ in the presence of the laws L′" (2024b p. 2158). The repair keeps every published verdict except MEDP, keeps the mentor case, and fixes the locusts. So the MEDP success was secured by the same mechanism that fails on the locusts.
- argument: the computations in `--checks` §5, plus the Proposition 1 mechanism (A4-01); MEDP's ¬G is excluded from F′ = {A, B} only through backward inference (B ∧ ¬G ⊢ F).
- key case or result: MEDP and locusts, each flipping with the status of an idle absence (both versions). The repaired theory disagrees with A&G's published verdicts on MEDP alone.
- nearest prior work as far as I know: A&G 2024a/2024b; Paul & Hall 2013, pp. 198–199 (known to me only through A&G's report). Difference: the hostage result, its diagnosis, and a repair with its exact cost.
- why it matters: It gives a precise repair target for the leading regularity theory. It also shows that A&G's treatment of non-transitivity (MEDP) and of preemption pull against each other.
- kill test: A&G endorse norm-sensitivity even for idle absences, with a principled rationale; or a repair that keeps MEDP and the locusts together.
- verifiability: exhaustive computation; the repair is a one-line change (`'2024b-fwd'` in `ag_cause`).
- main risk: idle-absence invariance is demanding. HP with licence-normality also flips in MEDP when ¬G is norm-violating, making f part of a cause with ¬G. The essay must either defend the invariance using Paul & Hall's reason, or restrict itself to the locust direction.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: medium-high.
- ambition: a constructive repair of a leading account, with its cost stated exactly.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors: A&G 2024a §4.4.3 (pp. 22–23); A&G 2024b §4.2 and fn. 2. Neither discusses the normative status of ¬G.
- prior-art risk: low-medium.

**Summary (≤150 words).** All five cards rest on verdicts computed in explicit causal models; the code reproduces every published verdict tested (A&G, Beckers, Halpern).
- A4-01: a preempted backup that only normal absences enable (locusts) refutes both versions of Andreas–Günther and HP plus Halpern–Hitchcock default normality. Normality-free accounts get it right but fail bogus prevention.
- A4-02: exhaustive check of 2,071 local typicality schemes. Exactly the "deviations need licences" schemes rescue HP; HH's Default Rule 1 is excluded.
- A4-03: modified-HP witnesses must violate equations, so with Default Rule 1 the modified definition makes Billy's throw part of the cause.
- A4-04: normality cannot give the L/R switch verdict alongside preemption.
- A4-05: A&G's verdicts flip with idle absences' status; a forward-candidate repair costs only MEDP.
