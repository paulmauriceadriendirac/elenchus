# A6 — Value of evidence and the dogmatism puzzle: attack cards (final)

**Pressure points worked from.** (1) Good's theorem for knowledge-based decision theories (KDTs: maximize expected utility with the prior conditioned on what one knows; Hawthorne and Stanley 2008 as axiomatized by Goldschmidt 2024) inside Goodman–Salow normality models — open per dossier #13 and L3 map 5.2.2. (2) Salow 2025 ("Fallibility and Dogmatism") dissolves the qualitative dogmatism puzzle via Preservation and Conditional Dominance but sets aside (n. 8) cases where one believes p and believes that further evidence will defeat it; Goodman and Salow's own non-statist dynamics ("Belief Revision Normalized", JPL 2025, hereafter BRN, §10; Goodman and Salow 2023 §8) produce exactly these. (3) BRN show that statism + collapse + comparability validate Preservation (their ♦−, = rational monotony) and that ♦− fails without collapse; they never connect this to decisions or the value of evidence. (4) BRN's gain-side failures (□+, Π+, frontloading) and Das 2023's "rationally biased inquiry". (5) Carter and Hawthorne 2024's derivative-norm reply to Ex Ante Dogmatism, unavailable under KK by their own n. 16. (6) Geanakoplos (1989, as reported by Das 2023): reflexive, transitive, nested information never has negative value. Read in full: Ahmed and Salow 2019; Salow 2023; Carter and Hawthorne 2024; Goodman and Salow 2023; Das 2023 (UCL accepted manuscript); Salow 2025 (ORA open access); Goldschmidt 2024 (LSE open access). Read in part: BRN (§§1–2, 4–7, 10, App. D; Oxford preprint pagination below). Not reachable (fetch requests filed): Goldstein 2024 book / 2026 "Omega Knowledge Matters"; Hong 2025 "Fanaticism and knowledge"; Hong 2026 "Normality in Action?" (abstracts only). All formal claims below were machine-checked (Python scripts in the session scratchpad, directory a6/: structs.py, check1–6.py, checkomega.py, checkI.py, cdcycle2.py, final_checks.py, final_checks2.py). A side result: Salow's Conditional Dominance can never cycle in statist structures, with or without comparability (proof: an element undominated in the union of the difference sets is undominated in each), so that line of attack was dropped.

## A6-01 — Act on what you ω-know: Good's theorem singles out ω-knowledge
- operator: TRN, IMP, GEN, HO
- thesis: In every statist normality structure, an expected-utility rule that conditions the prior on a factive "ken" containing everything one believes (knowledge on any completion, iterated knowledge, ω-knowledge) gives every free experiment non-negative value only if the ken is ≫-gapped (each situation in it is sufficiently more normal than each evidential possibility outside it); the smallest gapped ken is the set compatible with one's ω-knowledge on the I-completion, and acting on it satisfies Good's theorem for every experiment and decision problem; so knowledge simpliciter satisfies Good everywhere only on ranked (modular) normality, where every completion validates KK — and on graded normality inductive knowledge cannot shape expected-utility choice without making some free question harmful.
- argument:
  (P1) A KDT agent at situation x ranks options by expected utility under Pr(·|M(x)); after discovering the answer to a free question she ranks by Pr(·|M′(s)), M′ her post-discovery ken (Goldschmidt 2024 §4: P_E = P(·|E)).
  (P2) Lemma A (relative defeat ⇒ harm): if some s ∈ M(x) has M′(s) ⊄ M(x), a bet paying 1 on M(x) and −L elsewhere has strictly negative experiment-value at x for large L (the construction of Das 2023 n. 12).
  (P3) Lemma B (statism + INDUCTIVE LIMITS, completion-independent): if m ∈ M(x), v ∈ E∖M(x) and not m ≫ v, then the binary question "is the situation sufficiently more normal than v?" puts v into m's post-discovery ken. Hence Good at x ⇒ M(x) is gapped.
  (P4) Gapped sets containing x are closed under intersection and pairwise nested; the smallest is the closure of {x} under not-≫ steps, i.e. the transitive closure of I-accessibility: the ω-knowledge set on the I-completion. It contains the belief set.
  (P5) Lemma C (Geanakoplos's laminar sufficiency): under statism these closures only shrink on discovery and are reflexive, transitive and nested, so ω-I-knowledge-based expected utility never gives a free experiment negative value.
  (P6) Knowledge simpliciter is gapped at x iff it equals its ω-closure there; globally this holds iff ≫ is modular (negatively transitive), plus collapse below the top level for K-normality; then all completions validate KK given transparency.
  (C) ω-knowledge is the strongest knowledge-like state whose expected-utility use respects "look before you leap"; knowledge does so only on ranked normality; on ≫-connected (graded) evidence the only Good-respecting ken is the evidence itself.
- key case or result: Bjorn (Goodman and Salow 2023 §§1, 6) offered a free device that reports only whether the first scale's error is below δ. On graded normality the answer "no" widens his knowledge beyond what he knew, so a KDT agent prices the free report negatively; in that model ω-knowledge coincides with his evidence, so the ω-rule there is just evidential expected utility. Machine checks: every statist structure up to 5 states with comparability (9,211 at n = 5) gives zero mismatches for the global equivalences; every gap failure (2,736 cases at n = 4; 126 non-comparable cases at n = 3) yields a defeating binary question; 115,952 random decision problems show no negative value under the structural condition; for the ω-I rule, 64,416 problems over comparable and non-comparable structures show none either; for I-knowledge simpliciter, negative value was found in all 300 tested situations where knowledge equals ω-knowledge but post-discovery KK fails (so the local condition is necessary, not sufficient).
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Goodman and Salow 2025 (BRN), Props. 1–2 and App. D.2: statism + collapse + comparability validate ♦− (= rational monotony; Lehmann and Magidor's modularity characterization) — difference: belief revision only and no decisions; I give an exact value-of-evidence characterization, extend it to non-transparent knowledge with a completion-independent necessity lemma, and identify ω-knowledge as the strongest Good-respecting ken. Salow 2025 §§3–4: Weak Dominance licenses dogmatism exactly when Preservation fails; Conditional Dominance does not — difference: KDT entails Weak-Dominance verdicts, so the dissolution is unavailable to KDT; on graded normality the failure arises at every situation with inductive knowledge, and the theorem supplies the exact repair. Goldschmidt 2024 — difference: his axiom K2 quantifies over sub-kens E∩p, while normality knowledge after discovering p is not E∩p. Geanakoplos 1989 / Das 2023 — difference: here evidence is transparent and partitional; the non-partitionality is induced by inductive knowledge. Goldstein 2024/2026 argue that ω-knowledge is required for permissible action (abstracts only) — I could not check whether a value-of-evidence argument appears there.
- why it matters: Knowledge-first decision theorists (Hawthorne–Stanley; evidential probability conditioned on knowledge; Schulz at low stakes) must choose between Good's theorem and graded normality. KK theorists' K-normality knowledge fails the test while anti-KK theorists' ω-knowledge passes. Goldstein's thesis that ω-knowledge governs action gets an independent decision-theoretic rationale, together with the cost that on graded normality it collapses into evidential probability.
- kill test: (i) Goldstein 2024 ch. 1 or Hong 2026 already gives a Good-type argument for ω-knowledge or against action-guidance by normality knowledge on these grounds; (ii) a statist structure in which ω-I expected utility gives a free experiment negative value (none found); (iii) BRN App. E or Goodman–Salow 2021 (TARK) linking modularity to the value of information.
- verifiability: Lemmas A–C have half-page proofs; the equivalences are checked exhaustively on small structures; the Bjorn case is closed-form.
- main risk: "KDT treats knowledge as certainty, so of course defeat makes evidence harmful" (Harman/Kripke). Reply: the exact boundary, the positive ω-theorem and the KK consequence are new. Second, Lemma B assumes arbitrary binary questions can be asked for free; restricting the class of questions weakens but does not remove the result. Third, statism: BRN reject it in general, but the non-statist cases only add failures (A6-02).
- feasibility in 6,000 words: high — one theorem with three lemmas, one worked case, one dialectical section.
- ambition: what changes in the debate if this is right: "look before you leap" becomes a criterion that selects the epistemic state fit to guide expected-utility action: ω-knowledge, not knowledge. The collapse/graded-normality and KK debates acquire a decision-theoretic stake, and knowledge-first decision theory keeps Good's theorem only by accepting ranked normality or switching to ω-knowledge.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors (sections/pages): Ahmed and Salow 2019 §§2–5 (pp. 1–17: GT, clock, Allais, Conditionality, n. 13 on Geanakoplos/Dorst); Salow 2023 §§1–5 (informal argument, Sure Things, self-identifying evidence, nn. 19–26); Carter and Hawthorne 2024 §§3–5 and n. 16; Goodman and Salow 2023 §§1–8 (pp. 91–139, esp. 104–110, 124–128); Das 2023 §§2–4 and appendix; Salow 2025 §§1–6; Goldschmidt 2024 §§1–5; BRN §§1–2, 4–7, 10 and App. D (preprint pp. 1–35, 42–45).
- prior-art risk: medium — every ingredient is published (Geanakoplos; Lehmann–Magidor; BRN Prop. 1; Das's bet). The risk is that Goldstein's book or Hong 2026 draws the ω/value-of-evidence connection; I found no such assembly in the texts I read.

## A6-02 — Dogmatism about the known: where Salow's dissolution fails
- operator: TEN, CX, REP
- thesis: In Goodman and Salow's own non-statist models of memoryless processes (Flipping for All Heads, Decay: BRN §10; Goodman and Salow 2023 §8), discovering something one already believes and knows destroys belief and knowledge of another truth. There the verdict that one must not observe an outcome one already knows follows from three sources: Strong Dominance, which Salow 2025 treats as the minimal practical role of belief; Conditional Dominance, read with BRN's own notion of conditional belief; and knowledge-based expected utility. The verdict is avoided only by reading conditional belief as post-discovery belief, a reading BRN App. D.3 reject for non-statist models. So Salow's dissolution cannot cover the cases he set aside (n. 8) unless Strong Dominance is restricted to beliefs stable under discovery of what one believes, or the □− failures are given up.
- argument:
  (P1) □− fails (BRN §10, Prop. 22): the agent believes q ("the coins do not all land heads on flip 1") and p ("they all land heads by flip y+1"); discovering q makes her stop believing p. Her evidence is transparent, so she can foresee this.
  (P2) Strong Dominance (Salow 2025, p. 25): if you justifiably believe B's consequences would be better than A's, choose B. She believes p and believes that watching flip 1 will make her drop p, so she believes not watching keeps a true belief that watching destroys.
  (P3) With accuracy about p as the only concern (Salow's own restriction, n. 7), Strong Dominance requires her not to watch an outcome she already believes and knows. A knowledge-based expected-utility agent with a bet on p would pay to avoid watching.
  (P4) Conditional Dominance (Salow 2025 §4) is silent only if "belief on hypothesis D" is belief after discovering D, as his Objection 2 principle assumes (pp. 33–34). BRN App. D.3 deny this equivalence for non-statist structures and give a counterexample. On their restricted-accessibility notion she believes p conditional on D, so Conditional Dominance also says: do not watch.
  (P5) Avoiding observation of what one already knows cannot be rationally required; this anti-dogmatism is what Salow's paper aims to secure.
  (C) Something must go: unrestricted Strong Dominance, BRN's □− failures, or the D.3 notion of conditional belief. The minimal repair is to restrict Strong and Conditional Dominance to beliefs stable under discovery of what one believes ("□-stable dominance"). This coincides with Strong Dominance wherever □− holds, which includes every statist structure (BRN Prop. 1), and it blocks every dogmatic verdict in BRN's §10 class.
- key case or result: a computed instance of BRN's §10 generalized probability structure (per-flip success 0.1, threshold 0.85, de se question soon/middle/late). B(E1) = flips 2–41; after discovering "not on flip 1" (already believed) B(E2) = flips 3–42. K-normality knowledge at actual flip 10 moves from 2..41 to 3..42 (defeat at actuality). Knowledge-based value of watching = −1.000 against 1.000 for a bet on "by flip 41". Conditional Dominance with BRN conditional belief recommends not watching; on the discovery reading it is silent.
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Salow 2025 n. 8 — sets such cases aside as "highly unusual". Difference: they are BRN's and Goodman–Salow's central memoryless cases (Decay, Bus Driver style); both Strong Dominance and Conditional Dominance with D.3 yield dogmatism there; and I give the minimal repair. BRN §10 — difference: no discussion of decision or dogmatism. Carter and Hawthorne 2024 — difference: their evidence might defeat; here the evidence is already known and certainly defeats, so their higher-order reply cannot help, since the evidence destroys the first-order knowledge any higher-order inquiry needs. Goodman and Salow 2023 §8 (No Defeat From What You Already Know fails) — difference: the practical consequence.
- why it matters: Salow's dissolution is the most recent and most minimal. Showing that its residue lies in its own author's preferred models forces a choice among three commitments. It also shows that non-statist inductive dynamics carry practical costs their proponents have not discussed.
- kill test: a Goodman–Salow text already draws and resolves this consequence; or a principled reading of Strong Dominance on which p is not available as a premise when the option to watch is on the table (pragmatic encroachment), at low cost (but see A6-05).
- verifiability: the case is a finite computed model; the inconsistency is a five-line derivation; minimality is checked by dropping each conjunct (statist models satisfy the rest; Conditional Dominance on the discovery reading is silent; without anti-dogmatism, trivially consistent).
- main risk: a referee replies that with total accuracy (she also gains the lower-bound belief "after flip x+2") watching is not dominated. Reply: Salow n. 7 adopts the single-proposition restriction for the puzzle itself, and the expected-utility bet version does not depend on it.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: high.
- ambition: what changes in the debate if this is right: the leading dissolution of the dogmatism puzzle is shown incomplete exactly where Goodman and Salow's dynamics are most distinctive. Either discovery-stability becomes a condition on the practical role of belief, or those dynamics are rejected for knowledge.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors (sections/pages): Salow 2025 §§1–6, nn. 7–8, Objections 1–2 (pp. 23–36); BRN §§1–2, 6–7, 10 and App. D.3 (preprint pp. 1–8, 17–21, 32–35, 44–45); Goodman and Salow 2023 §8 (pp. 134–139); Carter and Hawthorne 2024 §§3–4.
- prior-art risk: medium — Salow flags the cases himself, and Goodman–Salow work I could not see may treat the practical side.

## A6-03 — Factivity as a shield: the value of evidence favours knowledge over belief as the guide to action
- operator: DIF, TRN
- thesis: In Goodman–Salow models where learning what one already believes can create new beliefs (□+, Π+ and frontloading fail, BRN §7) but cannot destroy them (□− holds, as in all their simple probability structures, BRN Prop. 7), acting on what one believes (expected utility under the prior conditioned on one's belief set) makes some free confirmation of what one already believes strictly harmful, whereas acting on what one knows (K-normality) never gives negative value to discovering what one already knows; the difference is factivity, so Good's theorem discriminates between knowledge-first and belief-first formulations of the practical role of outright attitudes.
- argument:
  (P1) Belief-guided expected utility: by the lemma "value of evidence ≥ 0 for all decision problems iff each posterior equals the prior conditioned on the cell", a free question has non-negative value for every decision problem iff, for every answer π consistent with belief, B(E∩π) = B(E)∩π (AGM Inclusion plus Preservation, restricted to answers).
  (P2) A □+ failure (p ⊇ B(E), B(E∩p) ⊊ B(E)) makes the belief-guided plan "biased" in Das's sense toward the newly believed proposition; by Das 2023 Prop. 3 some decision problem then gives negative value.
  (P3) Knowledge-guided expected utility (K-normality, weak statism, comparability): if x knows p, then for every s ∈ K(x), post-discovery knowledge B(E∩p) ∪ ↑s lies inside K(x) (□− plus upward closure of belief sets under chaining) and is reflexive, transitive and nested; Geanakoplos's theorem gives non-negative value.
  (C) Gain-side revision failures harm belief-guided but not knowledge-guided choice; only loss-side failures (♦−, □−: A6-01, A6-02) harm both.
- key case or result: BRN §7, Flipping for Heads with E′ = {s1..s5} discoverable, t = 1 − 0.5^4 + 0.01 (computed): B(S) = {s1..s5}, B(E′) = {s1..s4}, so learning E′, which she already believes, adds the belief "not s5". Value of that free confirmation for a bet against s5 at 0.97: belief-guided −0.0023; knowledge-guided, at every situation that knows E′, minimum over 3,600 random decision problems ≈ −7e−16 (zero). At abnormal situations that do not know E′ the knowledge-guided value can be negative, but that is an ordinary ♦− loss, covered by A6-01.
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Das 2023 (rational bias from evidence externalism plus conditionalization; Props. 1–3) — difference: here bias arises with transparent, partitional evidence from inductive belief dynamics, and factive knowledge is immune. BRN §7 (□+, Π+ and frontloading fail) — difference: the decision-theoretic consequence and the belief/knowledge contrast. Salow 2025 — difference: in □+ cases no option dominates, so his qualitative dominance principles register nothing; the phenomenon is invisible to that framework.
- why it matters: a checkable consideration in the knowledge-first versus belief-first debate about practical reasoning (knowledge-reasons norms; Salow's belief-based formulation of the puzzle; "belief is weak" views). It also exhibits a new species of the dogmatism puzzle: avoiding evidence in order not to gain beliefs.
- kill test: a model in which knowledge-guided expected utility gives negative value to discovering what one already knows while □− holds (none found; proof sketched in P3); or a showing that every belief-guided theory anyone holds satisfies □+ in BRN's cases (e.g. Leitgeb-style stability), which would shrink the target to BRN-belief.
- verifiability: one lemma (posterior = conditional prior), Das's Prop. 3, a short laminarity proof, and a computed case.
- main risk: "no one holds belief-guided expected utility." Reply: it is the natural quantitative form of "reasoning from" justified beliefs, the attitude Salow 2025 assigns the practical role, and the result is then a reason to formulate such norms with knowledge. Scope may still be judged modest.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: high.
- ambition: what changes in the debate if this is right: factivity — not certainty, stability or credence 1 — becomes the feature that lets an outright attitude guide action without generating evidence-avoidance. That gives knowledge-first views a decision-theoretic argument that does not depend on intuitions about cases.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors (sections/pages): BRN §§6–7 (Defs. 6.1–6.2, Props. 5–8; preprint pp. 17–21) and App. D.2–D.3; Das 2023 §§2–3 and appendix; Salow 2025 §§2–4; Goodman and Salow 2023 §8 (pp. 134–139).
- prior-art risk: medium-low — I found no discussion of gain-side revision failures in connection with the value of evidence.

## A6-04 — Carter and Hawthorne's derivative-norm reply is idle in statist normality models
- operator: HID, DIS, HO
- thesis: Carter and Hawthorne's reply to Ex Ante Dogmatism needs, at one and the same situation, knowledge that further evidence could defeat and a failure of KK (so that more evidence can serve an inquiry into whether one knows). In statist normality structures these never co-occur on the I-, K- or KM-completion: K-normality allows defeat but validates KK; the margin completions allow KK failures but, with comparability, validate No Defeat. So within that framework the ex ante puzzle arises only for KK-knowers, for whom Carter and Hawthorne concede (n. 16) that their reply is unavailable. On the I-completion, ω-knowledge — the aim of their "epistemically ideal" inquirer — cannot be defeated by statist evidence at all.
- argument:
  (P1) The reply (§§4.1–4.2) requires that the agent knows p, fails to know^n p for some n, and could come to know^n p by drawing more; the puzzle requires that drawing more could defeat her knowledge of p.
  (P2) Goodman and Salow 2023 (p. 128): MARGINS + COMPARABILITY + STATISM entail NO DEFEAT; I- and KM-normality entail MARGINS.
  (P3) Goodman and Salow 2023 (p. 107): K-NORMALITY + TRANSPARENCY entail KK.
  (P4) Hence, in statist comparable structures, defeat-possibility and KK failure never co-occur at a situation.
  (P5) Closures under I-accessibility only shrink under statist discovery (A6-01, P5), so ω-knowledge on the I-completion is never lost.
  (C) For normality theorists the reply is idle. Where the puzzle arises, the reply is unavailable; where it would be available, the puzzle does not arise. The puzzle must instead be met by a conditional principle in Salow's style, or by taking ω-knowledge as the aim of inquiry.
- key case or result: Bjorn's second weighing (Goodman and Salow 2023 p. 127). Under K-normality he loses knowledge when the two readings differ by more than (2 − √2)c, yet KK holds throughout. Under MARGINS he never loses knowledge. Exhaustive check of statist structures (defeat count, KK-failure count, co-occurrence count): comparable, 4 states — K (1128, 0, 0), I (0, 552, 0), KM (0, 552, 0); non-comparable, 3 states — K (36, 0, 0), I (0, 24, 0), KM (18, 12, 0). No co-occurrence anywhere, although KM without comparability does allow defeat (cf. Goodman and Salow 2023 n. 35).
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Carter and Hawthorne 2024 n. 16 (reply unavailable to KK sympathizers) and the No-Defeat result of Goodman and Salow 2023 — difference: the co-occurrence impossibility across completions shows the reply is idle throughout the statist normality framework; the ω-knowledge aim is offered as the alternative. Brown 2025 and Beddor 2024 (other replies) were not read here (map summaries only).
- why it matters: it tells Carter and Hawthorne which epistemologies their reply suits — non-statist or non-comparable ones — and redirects the ex ante debate for normality theorists toward conditional principles or ω-knowledge.
- kill test: a statist structure, on one of the three completions, where defeat and KK failure co-occur (none up to 4 comparable or 3 non-comparable states); or evidence that Carter and Hawthorne intend non-statist probabilistic models (BRN's preferred class), where co-occurrence may occur under I-normality. That would confine the result to statist models.
- verifiability: a short proof from two published results plus an exhaustive check; the non-statist case is left open and should be checked on BRN's probability structures.
- main risk: the increment is modest (two published results conjoined) unless the ω-knowledge alternative is developed; BRN's preferred models are non-statist.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: medium — needs a careful translation of their Minimality and Transparency principles into normality terms.
- ambition: what changes in the debate if this is right: the derivative-norm strategy loses its natural home in the most developed formal theory of inductive knowledge.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors (sections/pages): Carter and Hawthorne 2024 §§2–5 and nn. 16–18; Goodman and Salow 2023 pp. 104–110, 124–128 and n. 35; BRN §§2, 5.
- prior-art risk: medium.

## A6-05 — Encroachment cannot rescue knowledge-based decision theory
- operator: REV, LIM
- thesis: Salow (2025 §6) objects informally that pragmatic encroachment cannot be confined to the rare failures of Preservation. The gap theorem makes the objection exact and stronger. On graded normality every situation with inductive knowledge has a free binary question that makes knowledge-based expected utility dogmatic. So any encroachment strong enough to restore Good's theorem for KDT must, whenever such a question is available, shrink the agent's knowledge to a ≫-gapped set — on a ≫-connected evidence set, to her evidence. The mere availability of free re-checking would erase all inductive knowledge.
- argument:
  (P1) Lemma B of A6-01 (statism, INDUCTIVE LIMITS): if knowledge at x is not gapped, the question "is the situation sufficiently more normal than v?" has negative KDT value at x.
  (P2) On a ≫-connected evidence set the only gapped set containing x is the whole set.
  (P3) Such questions are available whenever an instrument or informant can report a coarse-grained error bound (a second device, a re-weighing, "is the suitcase over 17.5 kg?").
  (P4) Encroachment that restores Good for KDT must therefore make knowledge gapped whenever such a question is on the table.
  (C) Encroachment buys Good's theorem for KDT only by making inductive knowledge vanish whenever free checking is possible, which in ordinary life is almost always.
- key case or result: Salow's Suitcase (2025, p. 29). Believing 12–18 kg on a 15 kg impression, the question "over 17.5 kg?" defeats on the answer "yes". To keep KDT from paying to avoid it, encroachment must remove the knowledge "under 18 kg" — and by the same construction every other inductive bound — as soon as the question is available.
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Salow 2025 §6 (encroachment uncontained, argued informally); the encroachment proposals he cites (Fantl and McGrath 2002; Weatherson 2005; Hawthorne 2004, as reported by Salow) — difference: an exact result on which the cost is total rather than merely uncontained, and which applies to the KDT form of the practical role.
- why it matters: it closes the main remaining route by which a knowledge-based decision theorist could keep both Good's theorem and graded inductive knowledge, leaving ranked normality or ω-knowledge (A6-01) as the options.
- kill test: an encroachment view on which the availability of free evidence is not a feature of the practical situation that can encroach, or on which only salient questions count. Showing that defeating questions are rarely salient would blunt the result.
- verifiability: a corollary of A6-01's checked lemmas; the ≫-connectedness of Bjorn and Suitcase models follows from their continuity.
- main risk: thin as a standalone essay; better as a section of the A6-01 essay. Encroachers may restrict encroachment to salient options.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: medium (as a standalone essay), high (as a section).
- ambition: what changes in the debate if this is right: pragmatic encroachment stops being a cheap fix for the dogmatism puzzle in knowledge-first decision theory.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors (sections/pages): Salow 2025 §§3–6; Goodman and Salow 2023 §§3–6; Goldschmidt 2024 §5.4 (Reliance and encroachment).
- prior-art risk: low-medium.
