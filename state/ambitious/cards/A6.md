# A6 — Value of evidence and the dogmatism puzzle: attack cards (DRAFT v1, verification in progress)

**Pressure points worked from.** (1) Good's theorem for knowledge-based decision theories (KDTs: maximize expected utility with the prior conditioned on what one knows, Hawthorne and Stanley 2008 as axiomatized by Goldschmidt 2024) inside Goodman–Salow normality models, flagged open in the dossier (#13) and L3 map 5.2.2. (2) Salow 2025 ("Fallibility and Dogmatism") dissolves the qualitative dogmatism puzzle via Preservation and Conditional Dominance, but sets aside (n. 8) cases where one believes p and believes that further evidence will defeat it; Goodman and Salow's own dynamics (2023 §8; "Belief Revision Normalized", JPL 2025, hereafter BRN) produce exactly such cases. (3) BRN proves that statism + collapse + comparability validate Preservation (their ♦−) and that ♦− can fail without collapse; nobody has connected this to the value of evidence. (4) Salow 2023 locates failures of Good's theorem at Sure Things and at evidence structure; knowledge-based evaluation adds a third locus (anticipated non-conservative revision of knowledge). (5) Carter and Hawthorne 2024's derivative-norm reply to ex ante dogmatism is, by their own n. 16, unavailable under KK, which K-normality with transparency validates. (6) Geanakoplos (1989), as reported by Das (2023), gives sufficient laminar conditions for the value of information under non-partitional information; Das gives regular-prior refinements. Anchors read in full: Ahmed and Salow 2019; Salow 2023; Carter and Hawthorne 2024; Goodman and Salow 2023; Das 2023 (UCL accepted manuscript); Salow 2025 (ORA open access); Goldschmidt 2024 (LSE open access); BRN (§§1–2, appendix fragments; full read in progress).

## A6-01 — Good's theorem for knowledge-based decision holds exactly where normality is level-structured
- operator: TRN, IMP, GEN
- thesis: In every statist normality structure, an agent who maximizes expected utility relative to her prior conditioned on what she knows strictly prefers to avoid some free, transparent, partitional experiment at situation x whenever some situation she leaves open at x is not sufficiently more normal than some evidential possibility her knowledge excludes (a completion-independent necessary condition), and this "gap" condition is also sufficient under K-normality with comparability; so such a decider respects Good's theorem at every situation and for every experiment iff "sufficiently more normal" is modular (negatively transitive), plus collapse below the top level for K-normality — which fails on every graded (Bjorn-style) model.
- argument:
  (P1) A KDT agent at x ranks options by expected utility under Pr(·|K(x)); after discovering cell π she ranks by Pr(·|K'(s)) where K'(s) is her post-discovery knowledge (Goldschmidt 2024's KDT; Hawthorne and Stanley 2008).
  (P2) Defeat test: if for some k ∈ K(x) the post-discovery knowledge K'(k) contains a state outside K(x), a bet paying 1 on K(x) and −L off it has strictly negative experiment-value by x's lights for large L (construction as in Das 2023 n. 12).
  (P3) In a statist structure, INDUCTIVE LIMITS puts v into K'(k) whenever the discovered cell contains k and v but no u with u ≫ v; the question "is the situation sufficiently more normal than v?" provides such a cell whenever ¬(k ≫ v).
  (P4) Hence (necessity, any completion satisfying INDUCTIVE LIMITS): Good at x for all experiments ⇒ every k ∈ K(x) is sufficiently more normal than every v ∈ E(x)∖K(x).
  (P5) Sufficiency (K-normality, transparency, comparability): under the gap condition no discovery defeats x's knowledge and post-discovery knowledge is reflexive, transitive and nested, so Geanakoplos's laminar theorem gives non-negative value for every decision problem.
  (P6) Globally the gap condition holds at all (x, E) iff ≫ is negatively transitive (and, for K-normality, ≽ has no strict distinctions inside any non-top ≫-level); in a ≫-connected (graded) evidence set it fails at every situation with inductive knowledge.
  (C) KDT violates Good's theorem in every case of graded inductive knowledge, and respects it exactly on level-structured normality, where every completion validates KK given transparency.
- key case or result: Bjorn with a free second device that reports only whether the first scale's error is below δ (δ inside his knowledge margin): on graded normality the answer "no" widens his knowledge beyond what he knew, so KDT prices the free report negatively. Formal core: the gap theorem (P4–P6); the belief-level instance is Arrow's choice axiom for B(·), which for maximal-element choice from a transitive ≫ holds iff ≫ is modular.
- nearest prior work as far as I know: Goodman and Salow 2025 (BRN), Prop. 1–2: statism+collapse+comparability ⇒ ♦− (Preservation) and ♦− can fail otherwise — difference: they treat belief revision only, give sufficient conditions; I give the exact condition (modularity of ≫, weaker than collapse+comparability), lift it to knowledge with a completion-independent necessary condition, and prove it equivalent to Good's theorem for KDT. Salow 2025: Weak Dominance licenses dogmatism exactly when Preservation fails; Conditional Dominance does not — difference: KDT entails Weak-Dominance verdicts, so Salow's dissolution is unavailable to KDT, and the theorem shows KDT's dogmatism is ubiquitous on graded normality, not confined to unusual cases. Goldschmidt 2024 axiomatizes KDT with an epistemic Sure-Thing axiom (K2) over sub-kens — difference: his K2 presupposes that the ken one would have on learning p is E∩p; the theorem shows this is exactly what graded normality denies. Geanakoplos 1989 / Das 2023: value of information under non-partitional evidence — difference: here evidence is transparent and partitional; the non-partitionality and the defeat are generated by inductive knowledge.
- why it matters: Knowledge-first decision theorists (Hawthorne–Stanley, Williamson-style evidential probability on knowledge, Schulz at low stakes) must choose between Good's theorem and graded normality; anti-collapse and margin-for-error theorists inherit a practical dogmatism; "look before you leap" becomes an argument for level-structured normality, hence for KK.
- kill test: a published proof that KDT satisfies the value of information iff knowledge revision is AGM-expansive, or that Good's theorem forces collapse/modularity in normality models (check BRN appendices D–E in full, Goodman–Salow 2021 TARK, Hong 2025/2026, Schulz 2017).
- verifiability: proof by two lemmas (bet construction; Geanakoplos laminar theorem); exhaustive machine check of the structural equivalences on all statist normality structures up to 5 states, plus random decision-problem sampling (in progress).
- main risk: a referee says "this is just the dogmatism puzzle"; reply must show the exact boundary and the ubiquity claim are new and that Salow's CD cannot be grafted onto an expected-utility theory.
- feasibility in 6,000 words: high — one theorem, two short lemmas, one worked case, one dialectical section.
- ambition: what changes in the debate if this is right: the value of evidence becomes a constraint on the structure of inductive knowledge; the KK/cliff-edge and collapse debates acquire a decision-theoretic stake; knowledge-first decision theory loses "look before you leap" unless it accepts level-structured normality.
- what I read to check it is not already in the anchors (sections/pages): Ahmed and Salow 2019 §§2–5 (GT, clock, Allais, Conditionality); Salow 2023 §§1–5 (informal argument, Sure Things, self-identifying evidence); Carter and Hawthorne 2024 §§3–5 and n. 16; Goodman and Salow 2023 §§1–8 (pp. 91–139); Das 2023 §§2–4 and appendix; Salow 2025 §§2–5 and n. 8; Goldschmidt 2024 §§1–5; BRN §§1–2 and pp. 38–39 (n. 45).
- prior-art risk: medium — every ingredient is known (Geanakoplos, Arrow's axiom, BRN Prop. 1); the risk is that the assembly appears in BRN's appendices or in Goodman–Salow work in progress.

## A6-02 — Dogmatism about the known: the residue of Salow's dissolution
- operator: TEN, CX, REV
- thesis: Given the failures of "no change from learning what you already believe/know" that Goodman and Salow defend (2023 §8; BRN §§7, 10), any principle that lets knowledge guide action by dominance — even Strong Dominance, which Salow 2025 treats as the minimal practical role of belief — requires refusing (and paying to avoid) the free discovery of propositions one already knows, and knowledge-based expected utility additionally requires avoiding confirmations that would only add beliefs; so Goodman and Salow's dynamics and Salow's anti-dogmatism cannot both stand unless Strong Dominance is restricted.

- key case or result: Decay / Flipping for All Heads (Goodman and Salow 2023, pp. 135–138): discovering a trial outcome one already knows shifts the believed upper bound, destroying knowledge of p ("at most n trials").
